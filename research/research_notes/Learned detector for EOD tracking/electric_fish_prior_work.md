# Prior work on detecting and tracking wave-type electric fish by EOD frequency, ML on electric-fish data, and EOD signal facts for a learned detector

Scope note: these notes come from about 35 searches and fetches. Primary full texts that were read: Raab et al. 2022 (Frontiers), Henninger et al. 2018 (bioRxiv version of the J Neurosci paper), Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv version of the JEB paper), Madhav et al. 2018 (Europe PMC full text, first half), Fortune et al. 2020 (Frontiers), and Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv preprint). ## 1. Existing EOD-frequency tracking tools and papers: how they detect fundamentals and link them, reported accuracies and failure cases

### Takeaway
Every published wave-type pipeline uses the same three steps: (1) peaks in a per-electrode or electrode-summed power spectrum; (2) hand-tuned harmonic-group rules that turn the peaks into fundamentals; (3) frequency-gated linking across time, which since Madhav 2018 and Raab 2022 also uses the spatial power profile across electrodes. None of these pipelines uses a learned fundamental detector. Every group names the same hard cases: crossings during rises, fish sharing or nearly sharing a frequency for long periods (unresolvable below roughly 0.5-2 Hz), smeared peaks during fast modulations, low-SNR gaps, and harmonics of low-frequency species that land in another species' fundamental band.

### Cited Findings

**Raab, Madhav, Jayakumar, Henninger, Cowan & Benda 2022, Front Integr Neurosci 16:965211 ("Advances in non-invasive tracking of wave-type electric fish in natural and laboratory settings"; the wavetracker paper; bioRxiv preprint 10.1101/2022.06.02.494479)**
- Detection: power spectral densities per electrode from overlapping snippets shifted by Δt ≈ 300 ms. Frequency resolution is 0.6 Hz, or 0.3 Hz for field recordings with high fish density. Sampling is 20 kHz, 16 bit. PSDs are summed across electrodes and converted to dB re 1 mV²/Hz. Peaks are found with the Todd & Andrews (1999) method and grouped into a fundamental plus at least two harmonics, following Henninger et al. 2020 and the thunderfish harmonics module. The FFT lengths did not render in the HTML I fetched. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- Tracking: each signal is a feature vector of EOD frequency plus log power on every electrode. A candidate pair needs |Δt| ≤ 10 s and Δf ≤ 2.5 Hz. The frequency error is a logistic function of Δf (turning point 0.35 Hz, slope 0.08 Hz), which saturates at about 0.8 Hz difference. The field error is the Euclidean distance between spatial profiles rescaled to 0-1, normalised through a cumulative distribution taken from a 30 s night-time window. The combined distance weights the field error twice the frequency error. Linking runs in 30 s windows shifted by 10 s. Pairs are merged in ascending order of distance, only the central 10 s of each window is kept, and traces are then joined to earlier results by smallest distance. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- Validation: scored only on "conflict" cases, against manually corrected tracks from an 8×8 array (Rio Rubiano, Colombia, 10 April 2016, 10 h 50 min). On a 5-min snippet (464 conflicts) the correct-assignment rates were: frequency alone 94.83% (440/464), field alone 99.57% (462/464), combined reported as "99.87% (462/464)". That combined figure is internally inconsistent, since 462/464 = 99.57%. Snippet AUCs were 95.16%, 99.77% and 99.86%. On the full recording (261,344 conflicts): frequency 99.73%, field 99.81%, combined 99.95%, with AUCs of 99.79%, 99.85% and 99.98%. At about 15 s per error, the estimated manual correction time is about 3 h (frequency only), 2 h (field only) and about 30 min (combined). There is no validation against synthetic data or video. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- Failure cases the paper names: crossing EODf traces, especially during communication (fixed thresholds are insufficient); fish with similar orientation and close positions, whose field profiles look alike; detection gaps from low SNR, distance or objects blocking the field; dense groups at similar frequencies (e.g. 670-672 Hz), where superimposed signals prevent reliable assignment; entwined diffuse frequency modulations, not yet classified. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- The algorithm is described as a clustering method, not ML. Deep networks (DeepLabCut-style pose estimation, acoustic annotation tools) are suggested as future improvements, with the caveat that they need large training sets. Software: github.com/bendalab/wavetracker, fishgrid (C++ acquisition), thunderfish (harmonics). No raw dataset deposit is mentioned. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full); preprint: [bioRxiv 2022.06.02.494479](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.02.494479v1.full)

**Henninger, Krahe, Sinz & Benda 2020, J Exp Biol 223:jeb206342 (bioRxiv 10.1101/550814); multispecies community in Darién, Panama, without tagging**
- Spectra: PSDs with 8192 FFT points, 5 overlapping sub-windows at 50% overlap, a 1.22 s total window, 85% overlap between analysis windows, 20 kHz per channel. Frequency resolution Δf = 2.44 Hz. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Harmonic grouping, the ancestor of thunderfish and wavetracker grouping: peaks are found in log PSDs with a relative threshold of 1 dB. The search starts at the strongest peak f_max and tests fundamentals f1 = f_max/n for n ≤ 4, with a nominal tolerance of ±Δf/2 that was set in practice to ±0.7Δf. A group is discarded if it has fewer than 3 harmonics, if more than one of its frequencies already belongs to another group, if more than a quarter of its harmonics are missing, or if no peak exceeds 50 µV²/Hz. Fundamentals outside 40-1500 Hz or near the 60 Hz mains are discarded. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Named failure modes: peaks smeared by rapid frequency rises or by electric noise pulses, and peaks near the noise floor. The authors say neither was a problem in their data set. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Linking: a detection is linked to a previous fish if the EODf differs by less than 10 Hz. A new candidate must be robust over several seconds, and a fish stays "active" for 10 min. The resulting 461 detections in 25 h "likely overstate" the number of individuals. Crossing or converging frequencies are called "a critical problem" at higher densities or with social fish such as Eigenmannia; spatial power distribution might resolve them (citing Madhav 2018). They were not an issue in this data set. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Array: 54 electrodes (9×6) at 30 cm spacing, covering 240×150 cm, about 30 cm below the surface. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full); published version: [JEB PDF](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/223/3/jeb206342/1982005/jeb206342.pdf)

**Henninger, Krahe, Kirschbaum, Grewe & Benda 2018, J Neurosci 38(24) ("Statistics of natural communication signals observed in the wild..."; bioRxiv 10.1101/114249)**
- Same 54-electrode 9×6 field array, 20 kHz, 16 bit, more than 400 GB per day. EODf detection uses 1.22 s windows (8192-point FFT, 5 sub-windows, 85% overlap). Fish are identified from harmonic structure, and detections are matched across electrodes and then across successive windows. There is no manual correction of EODf tracks; only chirps were manually verified. — [Henninger et al. 2018 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Chirp detector (signal processing): bandpass filters at ±7 Hz around EODf and around EODf+10 Hz (3rd-order forward-backward Butterworth, applied 5×), with RMS envelopes over 10 EOD cycles. An event counts as a chirp if it appears synchronously in both passbands on more than two electrodes and exceeds an amplitude threshold. Thresholds were set to keep false positives very low, so "some chirps were likely missed". — [Henninger et al. 2018 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Scale: in the Berlin breeding-tank data, over 1.3 million chirps were scanned and 76 female long chirps found. The field data comprise 406 simultaneous pairings over 5 nights. — [Henninger et al. 2018 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full); [search summary of J Neurosci version](https://whale.am28.uni-tuebingen.de/git/jgrewe/gp_neurobio/raw/commit/258cd80a615f4010395a43ec1cf57a706c6994ed/papers/Henninger_etal_JNeurosci_2018.pdf)

**Madhav, Jayakumar, Demir, Stamper, Fortune & Cowan 2018, Sci Rep 8:5830 (Fortune/Cowan labs; Eigenmannia virescens)**
- Frequency step: MATLAB `spectrogram()` per electrode; the window length was not visible in the text I read. A harmonic "signature" is a Fourier-magnitude peak in an assumed fundamental range of 200-700 Hz above threshold τ1, and it also needs a second-harmonic peak above τ2, with τ2/τ1 = 1/8. τ1 is set manually. The 1/8 ratio was chosen heuristically and helped reject spurious tracks from 60 Hz mains harmonics. Signatures with the same fundamental are clustered across electrodes. Tracks are linked over time with the Hungarian algorithm (max gap 5 s, max fundamental difference 1 Hz). A MATLAB GUI lets the user split, join or delete tracks. Frequency tracking "was not separately tested because it involves human validation". — [Madhav et al. 2018 (Europe PMC full text)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Field facts: lab fish held nearly constant frequencies, typically more than 5 Hz apart. Field fish varied by more than 20 Hz and "sometimes shared frequencies for tens of seconds". Two fish sharing a frequency for an extended period violates the clustering assumption. Close interactions under one body length (contact, biting) cannot be resolved. — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Code for the Madhav algorithm: doi 10.7281/T1/XTSKOW (JHU data archive), as cited by Fortune et al. 2020. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)

**Fortune, Andanar, Madhav, Jayakumar, Cowan, Bichuette & Soares 2020, Front Integr Neurosci 14:561524 ("Spooky interaction at a distance in cave and surface dwelling electric fishes")**
- Used the Madhav 2018 algorithm on grids with 50 cm spacing: 8 electrodes at the surface site, 16 in the cave. Fish were identified by their time-varying fundamental, and position and pose came from the power distribution at each EODf across the grid. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)
- Close frequencies are common in the field. In surface Eigenmannia, 294 of 1,119 potentially interacting pairs had mean dF < 10 Hz and 142 had mean dF < 5 Hz (5 sessions). In cave fish the counts were 18 and 4 of 2,356 pairs (14 sessions). Low-dF pairs were often less than 50 cm apart. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)

**Raab, Bayezit, Erdle & Benda 2021, bioRxiv preprint 10.1101/2021.02.04.429572 (Apteronotus leptorhynchus competition and rises; Benda lab). Only the preprint was checked, not whether a journal version exists.**
- Tracking: peaks in the power spectrum summed across channels are assigned to fundamentals and harmonics, and traces are followed over time by EODf and power distribution across channels. FFT length is given as "215", probably 2^15 (1.63 s windows, 80% overlap). At 20 kHz that equals a 0.61 Hz bin, the same resolution as the current wavetracker. Three of 45 pairings were excluded "because the EODf difference between fish was below 0.5 Hz, so traces could not be extracted". — [Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full)
- Rises were detected by their onset peak (≥ 5 Hz above the preceding trough) on the 1.63 s FFT trace, so sub-second signals overlapping with chirps were not detected. — [Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full)

**Other / newer (2022-2026) work found**
- A Tübingen lab-course chirp-detection repository (GP2023, with Raab and Weygoldt among the commit authors) uses signal processing on grid recordings. It extracts features from the raw signal and counts a chirp only when peaks appear on all three features. Its own README says it "excels in assigning detected chirps to the correct fish, but the actual chirp detection is not that reliable". Normalising features to generalise the detector lets noise cause false detections, "especially during amplitude breakdowns"; manually set thresholds are more reliable. — [GP2023_chirp_detection README](https://whale.am28.uni-tuebingen.de/git/raab/GP2023_chirp_detection/src/commit/a60271bb392dacadd577c3049b55a029816b68e2); [poster script](https://whale.am28.uni-tuebingen.de/git/raab/GP2023_chirp_detection/raw/commit/677c29b77f19043fc2930b0f020db02449bd27f1/Chirpdetection%20poster%20script.md)
- Pulse-type tracking in the wild (Gymnotus omarorum; Uruguay group with Benda lab co-authors): the preprint says pulse EOD rates vary too much for frequency-based tracking, and that individual monitoring had previously needed video plus ML. — [bioRxiv 2024.06.29.600875 (preprint)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.29.600875v1.full). A 2025 iScience paper by Migliaro, Pedraja, Mucha, Benda & Silva tracks individual pulse fish in the wild with electrode arrays and low-cost amplifiers. I saw only a search snippet for it and did not verify the details. — [PMC11910113 PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/57868fd52ab1a95162c76e4e514d5936/main.PMC11910113.pdf)
- Thunderfish pulse-fish pipeline (not wave-type): preprocessing, peak detection, clustering on width and height features of peak-trough pairs, then post-processing. — [thunderfish pulses docs](https://bendalab.github.io/thunderfish/pulses)

### Inferences
- The detection front end has barely changed since Henninger 2018: a 1-2.5 s FFT window, a peak picker, and harmonic rules with fixed thresholds (1 dB relative, 50 µV²/Hz absolute, ≥3 harmonics, ≤1/4 missing, f_max/n with n ≤ 4). All the progress since then went into linking (spatial profiles, Raab 2022). That fits the user's measured failure modes, which are mostly detection failures (faint fish, merged peaks, false harmonic lines).
- The "≥ 3 harmonics" rule (Henninger) or "fundamental plus ≥ 2 harmonics" rule (Raab) probably explains why faint fish are missed: a fish at < 6 dB SNR on its fundamental likely has harmonics below threshold, so the group is rejected. Madhav's looser rule (fundamental plus 2nd harmonic at ≥ 1/8 of the threshold) shows that a weaker-harmonic acceptance criterion has been tried. These are judgements, not tested claims.
- The Raab 2022 accuracy is measured on assignment conflicts, not on detection recall or precision. No published pipeline reports frame-level detection recall versus SNR, so the user's dense-mixture benchmark seems to have no published counterpart.
- With the f_max/n (n ≤ 4) search, a strong Sternopygus or Eigenmannia harmonic in the Apteronotus band can be taken as a fundamental if its own lower harmonics are weak or rejected. This matches failure mode (c), false lines from harmonics of low-frequency fish.

- I could not fetch the thunderfish `harmonics` module docs (404 on two URLs), so the current default parameters (`freq_tol_fac`, `min_group_size`, `max_divisor`, mains handling) are unverified here.

- I found no published per-frame detection recall or precision, or fragmentation statistics, for any wave-type tracker.

## 2. Machine learning on electric-fish recordings (chirp detectors, pulse-fish classification, species ID, learned EODf detection or tracking)

### Takeaway
Learned methods in this field are rare and recent. They are object detectors for chirps on spectrograms (Faster R-CNN in Tübingen projects), classical classifiers such as SVMs for assigning pulse-fish EODs to individuals, supervised video tracking for assigning pulses, and an early neural-network localisation study. I found no published learned EODf detector, salience model or learned tracker for wave-type fish. A deep-salience detector would be new in this field.

### Cited Findings
- weygoldt/chirpdetector: "Detect brief communication signals of weakly electric fish in the frequency domain" "using deep neural networks". It runs in two steps: GPU spectrograms go into a DNN that outputs bounding boxes, then chirps are assigned to fish "based on power spectral densities of the frequency components of a single chirp". It requires fish frequencies to be tracked first with wavetracker. The README marks it as work in progress, with release expected in 2024. — [chirpdetector GitHub](https://github.com/weygoldt/chirpdetector)
- The chirpdetector docs name the detector as "such as a faster-R-CNN" on spectrogram images and refine detections by "assigning chirp times to the fundamental frequencies of each fish". The "Creating a dataset" page was a placeholder ("Wow, such empty"). The docs navigation includes a "yolo-helpers" page. — [chirpdetector: How it works](https://weygoldt.com/chirpdetector/how_it_works/); [dataset page](https://weygoldt.com/chirpdetector/dataset/)
- efishSignalDetector (raab namespace on the Tübingen Gitea, 2023) is "a neural network framework for detecting electrocommunication signals from spectrogram images". It uses a pretrained Faster R-CNN with a ResNet50 backbone, trained on PNG spectrogram images with CSV bounding-box labels. — [efishSignalDetector repo](https://whale.am28.uni-tuebingen.de/git/raab/efishSignalDetector/src/commit/3dbc78685d4c42392ecaa8daaefca7c4c8cdbc24)
- Pedraja, Herzog, Engelmann & Jung 2021, Front Behav Neurosci 15:718491: supervised learning models (for visual tracking) are combined with position data to attribute pulse EODs to individual Gymnotus petersii during dyadic fights, and coupled with boundary-element modelling. The authors report "high performances and accuracy of position estimation and the attribution of EODs to individuals". — [Pedraja et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.718491)
- Matias et al., "Individual discrimination of freely swimming pulse-type electric fish from electrode array recordings" (arXiv 1412.1759, 2014): Fourier and dual-tree complex wavelet packet features with SVMs plus a continuity constraint. EODs are assigned to each fish with "only two errors per million discharges". — [arXiv 1412.1759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1759)
- Kiar et al. 2013 (ICEBI, Carleton, Adler group) tested neural networks for localising and tracking weakly electric fish from EODs, with error measured against true position. I saw the search snippet only. — [Kiar et al. 2013](https://www.sce.carleton.ca/faculty/adler/publications/2013/kiar-ICEBI2013-localization-electric-fish.pdf)
- Deep learning appears in adjacent electric-fish work but not on EOD-frequency detection: SLEAP pose estimation for mormyrid interaction studies ([bioRxiv 2025.02.24.639824, preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.24.639824v1.full.pdf)), and reinforcement-learning agents of active sensing ([CCN 2024 paper](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/329_Paper_authored_202404_CCN_2024_Elephantfish_Agent_20240419_1619_named.pdf); [arXiv 2511.08436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08436v1)).
- Raab 2022 cites Pedraja et al. (2021) as supervised-learning background and proposes DNNs only as future work, noting the need for large training sets. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- A search for CNN-based EOD waveform species classification (mormyrid or gymnotiform) found no such study. — [search result set including arXiv 1412.1759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1759)

### Inferences
- The only learned spectrogram models in this community are box detectors for transient events (chirps). They need fundamental tracks from wavetracker as input, so they depend on the detector this project wants to replace. A dense per-bin salience model is a different output type: per-frame, per-bin regression or classification, closer to music multi-pitch "deep salience" than to object detection.
- The chirpdetector and efishSignalDetector projects show that training labels in this community are bounding boxes on spectrogram images, not per-bin f0 masks. Per-bin labels would have to come from tracks of single sources, as in the user's benchmark, or from synthesis.

### Gaps
- I could not find the chirpdetector training details: whether labels are synthetic or manual, the YOLO vs Faster R-CNN choice in the current version, spectrogram parameters, and performance. The docs pages were empty or high-level, and the repo README does not state them.
- I found no species-classification network for wave-type EODs, and no learned EODf detector or tracker, published or preprint.
- No ML paper reports the specific failure modes the user lists (sub-3 Hz pairs, harmonic false lines).

## 3. Signal facts a learned detector must model (harmonics, temperature, chirps and rises, beats, spatial field, noise)

### Takeaway
EODf is extremely stable on short timescales (sub-µs jitter; SD about 0.4 Hz over 5 min in a tube). It drifts with temperature (Q10 ≈ 1.37 measured in A. leptorhynchus) and with movement or social state (SD about 0.6-1.1 Hz over 5 min in the field). Rises reach 5-68 Hz (mean 17 Hz). Chirps are < 20 ms with < 150 Hz excursions (small) or about 160 ms with about 400 Hz excursions (long). Fish within about 0.5 Hz could not be separated by FFT tracking, and Sternopygus and Eigenmannia harmonics can match Apteronotus fundamentals in power. The field is dipole-like, with amplitude decay exponents below the ideal far-field value when fish are inside the array.

### Cited Findings

**Harmonic structure and waveform**
- In Darién, harmonics of Sternopygus and Eigenmannia extend into higher-frequency ranges, and "at times, a Sternopygus harmonic had power similar to an Apteronotus fundamental". Frequency alone is ambiguous for species ID. — [Henninger et al. 2020 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Species bands at one site (Darién): A. rostratus about 580-1100 Hz (lowest amplitude, 0.16 ± 0.04 mV); E. humboldtii about 200-580 Hz (0.46 ± 0.23 mV); S. dariensis about 40-220 Hz (0.47 ± 0.30 mV), sharing its range with the pulse fish Brachyhypopomus occidentalis (about 30-100 pulses/s per Henninger 2018). — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full); [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Eigenmannia EODf: 299.9-435.6 Hz (surface, n = 110) and 230.0-478.6 Hz (cave, n = 82) in Brazil; the genus is generally "between about 200 and 700 Hz". — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full). E. virescens in the Madhav study: 346-452 Hz. — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Madhav 2018 models each Eigenmannia as an oscillating horizontal current dipole that includes the first two harmonics, and requires a second-harmonic peak to accept a fundamental. — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- The A. leptorhynchus EOD is spatiotemporally complex. Electrocyte activation propagates down the body and rotates the field, and the temporal jitter of the periodic field is < 1 µs (Rasnow 1994 thesis). Inverse modelling finds an ultrafast travelling wave (> 200 m/s), well described by two spatial Fourier components varying in time (Shifman, Longtin & Lewis 2015, Sci Rep). The main primary source on waveform variation around the body is Rasnow & Bower 1996, J Comp Physiol A 178:383-396 (not read). — [Rasnow 1994 thesis](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/7719); [Shifman et al. 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4626797/); [FIU citation list](https://efish.fiu.edu/visitors/electric_field_animations.htm)

**Sex and EODf ranges in Apteronotus**
- A. rostratus: males 907 ± 77 Hz (n = 10), females 640 ± 23 Hz (n = 5); the female/male split is at about 750 Hz. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- A. leptorhynchus lab fish: sex assigned by EODf at 25 °C > 740 Hz (male); EODf did not correlate with size (9-19 cm). — [Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full)

**Stability and temperature**
- EODf SD over 300 s epochs is 0.64 Hz (surface Eigenmannia) and 1.11 Hz (cave), versus 0.38 Hz for fish confined in tubes (preliminary, n = 3). The authors attribute part of the variability to self-movement and social signals. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)
- Raab et al. 2022 call wave-type EODfs among the most stable oscillating signals in natural systems, and use EODf as the main tracking criterion. — [Raab et al. 2022 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/965ef8f34555451caad8adb77eece028)
- Temperature: the median Q10 across A. leptorhynchus was 1.37, computed within 5-min snippets, and used to normalise EODf to 25 °C. Tank temperature was 25 ± 0.5 °C, with about 1 °C of drift per trial. — [Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full). Field water in Darién varied 25-27 °C daily, and EODf temperature sensitivity was expected to shift frequencies diurnally. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full). The classic primary study is Dunlap, Smith & Yekta 2000, Brain Behav Evol 55:152-162 (Q10 values not retrieved). — [Dunlap lab listing](https://commons.trincoll.edu/kdunlap/?p=407)

**Chirps**
- A. rostratus in the wild: small chirps are < 20 ms long with < 150 Hz EODf excursion and minimal amplitude reduction. Rates reach 3 Hz, and up to 8,400 chirps per individual per night. Female long chirps are 162 ± 39 ms (n = 54) with about 400 Hz excursion and a strong EOD amplitude decrease, and can be up to 600 ms by the detector's definition. The male response doublet follows 229 ± 31 ms after the long chirp. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- A. leptorhynchus chirp types 1-4 (Engler et al. 2000; Zupanc et al. 2006) are classified by duration, frequency excursion and amplitude reduction. Type 2 is the most common, at 15-20 ms and about 50-100 Hz. Type 1 is rarer, with a larger excursion and similar duration. Types 3-6 are longer and rare. Tamburello & Krahe 2007 split type 2 into 23-86 Hz and 86-183 Hz sub-categories. Females produce almost only type 2. One automated criterion is instantaneous frequency ≥ 10 Hz above baseline for ≥ 5 ms. — [McGill MSURJ review article](https://msurjonline.mcgill.ca/article/view/133); [Hupe & Lewis 2008, JEB 211:1657](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-split/211/10/1657/17420/Electrocommunication-signals-in-free-swimming). These are secondary summaries of the primary typing papers.

**Rises**
- In 37 analysed A. leptorhynchus competition trials: 8,530 rises, with sizes from the 5 Hz detection threshold to 68 Hz (mean 17 Hz). In the 3 h dark phase, losers emitted 184 ± 105 rises (range 0-419 per trial) and winners 18 ± 17. From earlier literature: rise durations range from less than a second to a few minutes, and decays from about a second to almost a minute. — [Raab et al. 2021 (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full)
- Henninger 2020 names "smeared peaks from rapid frequency rises" as a detection failure mode. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)

**Beats and close frequencies**
- Courtship difference frequencies in A. rostratus were 205-415 Hz, and male-male aggression below 245 Hz. Beat amplitudes at chirp distances ranged from 100% down to about 1% of EOD amplitude. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- About 13% of surface Eigenmannia pairs (142/1,119) had mean dF < 5 Hz. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)
- FFT tracking at 1.63 s windows could not extract traces when ΔEODf < 0.5 Hz (3 trials excluded). — [Raab et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.04.429572v1.full). Dense groups at 670-672 Hz prevent reliable assignment. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- Eigenmannia in the field "sometimes shared frequencies for tens of seconds". — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)

**Spatial field and amplitude decay**
- Inside the Darién array, the amplitude decay exponent was q = 1.34 ± 0.24, below the far-field dipole value of 2 (attributed to boundary effects). Estimated detection range: 166 ± 14 cm (A. rostratus, n = 10), 255 ± 76 cm (Sternopygus, n = 97), 253 ± 54 cm (Eigenmannia, n = 64). — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Field amplitude versus distance was fitted with a power law. At 177 cm the estimated field was ≤ 0.34 µV/cm, against a behavioural threshold of about 0.1 µV/cm. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Fortune et al. model field strength as a current dipole (p = Id). — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)

**Noise**
- Mains hum: Henninger 2020 discards fundamentals near 60 Hz. Madhav's harmonic-ratio rule (τ2/τ1 = 1/8) was chosen partly to reject 60 Hz harmonics, and 60 Hz interference is a named field problem. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full); [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Electric noise pulses smear peaks, and pulse-type fish (Brachyhypopomus) share the Sternopygus band. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)

### Inferences (my judgement; not from sources)
- Spectrogram geometry: a 0.61 Hz bin at 20 kHz corresponds to a 2^15-sample (1.64 s) window, the same as Raab 2021. The 0.16 s "frames" are therefore hops of a heavily overlapped (about 90%) 1.64 s window. So:
  - A chirp (< 20 ms) occupies about 1% of a window. It appears mostly as a small power dip and broadband smear across about 10 consecutive frames, not as a resolved excursion. The detector should treat chirps as a brief loss of line power, not as a line jump.
  - A rise onset of, for example, 20 Hz within 1 s smears a line over about 30 Hz (about 50 bins) within one window, so the peak drops sharply in power. This matches "smeared peaks from rapid frequency rises" (Henninger 2020).
  - Two fish Δf apart produce a beat of period 1/Δf. With a 1.64 s window, the Hann main lobe is ±2 bins (±1.2 Hz). Below about 1.2-2.5 Hz the lines merge into one peak whose power oscillates between (a1+a2)² and (a1−a2)² at Δf across frames. Below about 0.6 Hz (one beat per window or slower) the merged peak looks like one slowly modulated fish. This fits the < 0.5 Hz exclusion in Raab 2021 and the user's < 3 Hz failure mode.
- The beat-modulation depth of a merged peak differs across electrodes, because a1/a2 differs per electrode with each fish's position and orientation. Per-electrode spectrograms over time therefore carry information that can separate two fish in one bin (a rank-2 temporal and spatial structure). The summed spectrogram destroys this. That argues for giving the network per-electrode inputs with temporal context of at least one beat period.
- Temperature is a common-mode drift: with Q10 = 1.37, ln(1.37)/10 ≈ 3.1% per °C, so an 800 Hz fish moves about 25 Hz per °C. A 2 °C diurnal swing in the field moves all fish together by tens of Hz. The detector should be translation-equivariant in frequency, and the training data should include slow common drift.
- Harmonics: because the spectrogram is linear in frequency, harmonic relations are multiplicative (k·f0), so a plain 2D CNN cannot see them without wide receptive fields. Deep-salience models handle this with harmonic stacking (HCQT-like channels). On a linear axis, an equivalent is to stack spectrogram slices resampled at k·f for k = 1..K as input channels. A low-frequency fish (40-220 Hz Sternopygus, 200-600 Hz Eigenmannia) has harmonics at k·f0 inside the Apteronotus band. A per-bin "is this a fundamental?" decision therefore needs input from f/2, f/3 and f/4 (sub-harmonic evidence), not only from k·f. This is the cue the f_max/n search used.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the claim that in A. leptorhynchus the fundamental can be weaker than the 2nd harmonic at some electrodes. It most likely comes from Rasnow & Bower 1996 (J Comp Physiol A 178:383) or Assad, Rasnow & Stoddard 1999 (J Exp Biol), and I could not access either.
- I found no source on harmonic power profiles per species, such as "strong odd harmonics" in Eigenmannia or relative harmonic amplitudes in Sternopygus.
- I did not retrieve the Dunlap et al. 2000 Q10 values for A. leptorhynchus EODf. The only measured value found is 1.37, in Raab 2021.
- I did not find quantitative rise kinetics (onset slopes in Hz/s, durations) beyond Raab 2021's sizes and the literature ranges it cites.
- I found no Fotowat paper on the field geometry.

## 4. Position estimation from electrode amplitudes and its use for identity; can spatial cues separate fish closer than the frequency resolution?

### Takeaway
Position from amplitudes is accurate to about 5-10 cm (weighted average of amplitudes; Henninger) or about 20 cm (dipole particle filter; Madhav). Spatial profiles resolve identity conflicts better than frequency: 99.57% vs 94.83% on hard conflicts in Raab 2022. All published methods, however, need each fish's spectral peak to be resolved before computing its spatial profile. No one has reported separating fish that share one spectral peak by their spatial signatures.

### Cited Findings
- Henninger 2020: position is a weighted average of electrode positions with sqrt(amplitude) weights. The 4-electrode variant needs at least 2 electrodes above 15 µV, using n = 4 if at least 4 exceed 1 µV and otherwise n = 2. This proved "more robust than fitting a dipole model". The dipole fit has 8 parameters (so at least 8 electrodes), a regulariser α of 10⁻² then 10⁻³, and a free power-law exponent q. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full); [JEB abstract summary](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/223/3/jeb206342/1982005/jeb206342.pdf)
- Henninger 2020 accuracy: in the tank test (4×4 grid), median error was about 5 cm for spacings ≤ 36 cm and about 10 cm at 108 cm spacing. In simulations of moving fish (10 cm/s), median position error was clearly below 10 cm and orientation error well below 15°. The dipole fit needs narrow spacing and degrades off-plane. Tracking is confined to the array. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Henninger 2018: position precision was 4.2 ± 2.6 cm at array level and 6.2 ± 3.8 cm at 15 cm vertical distance (simulated). The estimates are "not physically based" and are sensitive to electrical noise and to fish near the array edges. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Madhav 2018: a particle filter (Bayesian) over the state x, y, planar orientation and frequency, with a horizontal dipole model including two harmonics. Lab accuracy against video: over 90% of positions within 20 cm (about 1.5 body lengths) in both TUBE and FREE trials; over 80% (TUBE) and over 90% (FREE) of orientations within 30°. Outside the grid, TUBE orientation was no better than shuffled. Field (restrained fish): position RMSE 6.6-43.9 cm and orientation RMSE 3.1°-57.9°; 10 of 12 positions within 1.5 body lengths. — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Raab 2022: the spatial "field" profile alone beat frequency alone on assignment conflicts (99.57% vs 94.83% on 464 conflicts; 99.81% vs 99.73% over 261,344 conflicts), and the combination was best (99.95%). Field is weighted 2× frequency. Failures occur when fish have similar orientations and close positions. — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- Madhav 2018: two fish sharing a frequency for an extended period violates the clustering assumption, and interactions closer than one body length cannot be resolved. — [Madhav et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5895713/fullTextXML)
- Fortune 2020: low-dF Eigenmannia pairs were often less than 50 cm apart. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)

### Inferences (my judgement)
- The pairs that matter most for failure mode (b) (within 3 Hz) are also often physically close, which Fortune 2020 found for low-dF pairs. Spatial separability is therefore weakest exactly when it is needed most. Still, on a 3×3 or 5×3 grid with 30-50 cm spacing, two fish 20-50 cm apart, or with different orientations, give clearly different electrode-amplitude vectors (position errors of about 5-20 cm imply that the amplitude patterns resolve such separations).
- Inside one merged bin, per-electrode power over time is |a1,e·e^{iφ1(t)} + a2,e·e^{iφ2(t)}|². Its time series across electrodes contains the two spatial vectors and the beat frequency. A network given per-electrode spectrograms over at least one beat period, or an explicit low-rank factorisation, could in principle report two fundamentals in the same bin. Per-electrode complex STFT phase (dipole sign flips between head and tail sides) would add information that magnitude-only inputs drop. No source tests this. It is a hypothesis to evaluate on the dense benchmark.
- Because every published identity cue works after detection, giving the salience network a spatial cue mostly helps the detection side: not suppressing a second, faint fish that sits under a strong neighbour's main lobe at some electrodes but not at others.

### Gaps
- I found no published study of separating co-frequency fish (within one FFT bin) by spatial or beat cues.
- I could not access Assad et al. 1999 or Rasnow & Bower 1996, so electrode-level head/tail phase and harmonic maps are not quantified here.
- I found no Fotowat work on field geometry.

## 5. Public labelled datasets of EOD frequency tracks

### Takeaway
I found no public, labelled benchmark of EOD frequency tracks. The closest resources are the G-Node release of the Darién extracted frequencies and positions (Henninger 2020), the Fortune lab's Brazil data and scripts, and the Madhav code archive. Raw grid data are generally "available on request" (terabytes).

### Cited Findings
- Henninger 2020: extracted EOD frequencies and position estimates are at doi 10.12751/g-node.87584d. Scripts and raw data (2.0 TB) are available on request. Analysis was done in Python 2.7. — [Henninger et al. 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v2.full)
- Henninger 2018: raw data on request; Panama field data 2.0 TB; Berlin breeding data 3.7 TB of EOD recordings plus 11.4 TB of video. — [Henninger et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/114249v2.full)
- Fortune 2020: data and scripts at https://web.njit.edu/~efortune/Brasil. The Madhav 2018 code is at doi 10.7281/T1/XTSKOW. — [Fortune et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2020.561524/full)
- Raab 2022: "original contributions are in the article and supplementary materials", with no raw dataset deposit mentioned. Code is on GitHub (wavetracker, thunderfish, fishgrid). — [Raab et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnint.2022.965211/full)
- G-Node GIN holds A. leptorhynchus electrophysiology datasets (e.g. Grewe & Benda 2017, doi 10.12751/g-node.5b08du, electroreceptor afferent recordings, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), not EOD grid recordings or track labels. — [G-Node GIN g-node.5b08du](https://gin.g-node.org/doi/g-node.5b08du); [G-Node keyword listing](https://doi.gin.g-node.org/keywords/apteronotus%20leptorhynchus)

### Inferences
- Training labels for a salience network will have to come from the user's own corrected tracks and single-source mixtures (the dense benchmark design), or from synthesis. The Henninger 2020 G-Node release might serve as out-of-distribution field validation for the frequency tracks only. It was produced by an automatic tracker at 2.44 Hz resolution without manual correction of EODf, so it is not ground truth.
- The Madhav 2018 lab data (video-validated positions, Eigenmannia, 9-electrode grid) would be the only public source with an external position ground truth, if the JHU archive contains raw recordings. Unverified.

### Gaps
- I did not check what exactly is inside g-node.87584d, the JHU archive (10.7281/T1/XTSKOW) or the NJIT Brasil folder (raw data or only extracted tracks, file formats, licence).
- I found no labelled chirp dataset (e.g. the chirpdetector training set) published openly.
