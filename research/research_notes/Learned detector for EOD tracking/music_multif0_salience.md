# Learned multi-f0 / salience / pitch tracking from music and speech, as candidate designs for EOD-fundamental detection

Scope note: these notes cover music/speech multi-pitch salience (deep salience, Basic Pitch, harmonic-aware layers, transcription models), single-f0 trackers (CREPE, SPICE, PESTO, RMVPE, FCPE), and multi-pitch streaming / voice assignment. Facts are under "Cited Findings" with a source each. Arithmetic I did myself, and all transfer judgements, are under "Inferences". Primary PDFs read in full this session: deep salience (ISMIR 2017), Basic Pitch (ICASSP 2022, arXiv v2), CREPE (ICASSP 2018), PESTO (ISMIR 2023, arXiv v3). The SS-MPE (arXiv 2024), Cuesta & Gómez (TISMIR 2022) and RMVPE (Interspeech 2023) full texts were read through a summarising fetch tool. Most other items come only from abstracts or search snippets, and are marked that way.

## 1. Deep salience (Bittner et al., ISMIR 2017) and the HCQT: design, results, weaknesses, and how harmonic stacking moves to a LINEAR frequency axis

### Takeaway
Deep salience is a small, fully convolutional, pooling-free network. It maps a 6-channel harmonic CQT (h = 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; 20-cent bins) to a per-bin sigmoid salience. It is trained with BCE against Gaussian-blurred targets (zero within a quarter tone), then peak picking with a validation-tuned threshold (0.3). Its precision was good, but recall on inner voices was weak, and the best threshold changed a lot between datasets. The harmonic-stacking idea moves to a linear axis cleanly and exactly, by gathering the spectrum at h·f. The log-axis "shift by a constant" trick does not move, and a log axis cannot give 0.6 Hz resolution at 2 kHz without a huge oversampling cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Venue/authors:** "Deep Salience Representations for F0 Estimation in Polyphonic Music", Bittner, McFee, Salamon, Li, Bello. 18th ISMIR, Suzhou, 2017. Code and trained models: github.com/rabitt/ismir2017-deepsalience — [Paper PDF](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Framing:** salience estimation is posed as de-noising. A network maps a time–frequency representation to a salience map of the same shape, with values in [0,1]. The authors present the conv stack as a learned generalisation of cascaded HPSS median filtering plus harmonic summation, so the "de-noising" and "harmonic emphasis" stages are learned together — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **HCQT definition:** H[h,t,f] is a 3-D array (harmonic, time, frequency). H[h] is a standard CQT whose minimum frequency is scaled to h·fmin, with the same bins per octave and number of octaves. So bin k of H[h] is exactly the h-th harmonic of bin k of H[1], and "2-D CNNs … can now efficiently exploit locality in time, frequency, and harmonic". A plain CQT can only align harmonics h = 2^n, so odd harmonics are hard to capture — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **HCQT parameters:** h ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, i.e. one subharmonic plus the fundamental and 4 harmonics. Hop ≈ 11 ms. 6 octaves at 60 bins/octave (20 cents/bin, 360 bins). fmin = 32.7 Hz (C1). Built on librosa's CQT. The subharmonic is there "to help disambiguate between the fundamental frequency and the first harmonic": at a true f0 the subharmonic channel should be low in energy, while one octave up it is not — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Targets:** same shape as H[1]. Ground-truth f0 is quantised to the nearest bin and set to 1, then Gaussian-blurred in frequency "such that the energy … decays to zero within a quartertone". The stated reasons are to soften the penalty for near-misses and because human labels may not be accurate to 20 cents — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Architecture:** fully convolutional with batch norm on each layer's input and ReLU. Layers 1–2 have 128 and 64 filters of 5×5 (≈1 semitone × 50 ms). Layers 3–4 each have 64 filters of 3×3. Then 8 filters of 70×3 (≈14 semitones, "to capture relationships between frequency content within an octave"), followed by a sigmoid output; the 8-map layer is called the "penultimate layer" in the analysis. All layers are zero-padded so the output keeps the input's T×F shape. There is **no pooling**, "since we do not want to be invariant to small shifts in time frequency" — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Loss/optimiser:** element-wise binary cross-entropy with continuous targets y, ŷ ∈ [0,1]; Adam — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Training data construction (relevant to label completeness):** 240 multitracks (108 MedleyDB plus 132 non-redistributable pop), split by artist. ≈10 h train, 2 h validation, 28 MedleyDB tracks for test. They rebuilt "partial mixes" from only the stems that carry human f0 labels, plus monophonic stems and percussion, "with all 'unknown' pitch content removed". Mix weights came from a least-squares fit to the original mixes. Unlabelled monophonic stems got **pYIN output as proxy ground truth** — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Peak picking / threshold:** thresholding at 0.5 was rejected because the blurred targets make bins next to a peak exceed 0.5 too, which gives "multiple estimates very close to one another". Instead they peak-pick, then apply a minimum-amplitude threshold that maximises multi-f0 accuracy on validation (0.3). On Bach10 the best threshold was much lower, **0.05 vs 0.3**. Even with that oracle threshold and polyphony capped at 4, Bach10 accuracy reached only 63%, against 68% (Duan) and 76% (Benetos) — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Results:** the CNN under-performed Benetos and Duan on Bach10 by about 10 percentage points, but beat both on Su and MedleyDB, and won on every MedleyDB track. The best mean accuracy on any set (Su) was "only 41%". It made fewer octave errors: 3% of mistakes on MedleyDB vs 5% (Benetos) and 7% (Duan) — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Known weakness (authors' own analysis):** "good precision" but low recall, mainly on inner (harmony) voices. The model retrieves melody and bass but "often misses notes that occur in between". The authors attribute this to the training mixes: most auto-annotated stems were bass or vocals, "with few examples with simultaneous harmonically related pitch content" — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Learned feature maps:** some penultimate maps emphasise harmonic content; others suppress octave mistakes, broadband noise, or low-frequency noise — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **A later independent comparison** (Cwitkowitz & Duan, arXiv 2024) reported Deep-Salience P/R/Acc of 86.0/61.0/55.5 on Bach10 and 74.1/47.9/40.4 on Su. Precision well above recall again. They note some baselines were evaluated on data used in their own training — [SS-MPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15569v1)
- **Efficient "HCQT approximation" used later:** Basic Pitch does not compute separate CQTs. It copies one CQT and shifts it "vertically by the number of frequency bins corresponding to each harmonic" (following its ref. [23]) — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Log-axis harmonic-aware alternatives:** HPPNet's harmonic dilated convolution sets the dilation rates to the log-frequency spacing between a fundamental and its overtones, runs several dilated convs and sums their outputs — [HPPNet arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14339). HarmoF0 uses "multiple rates dilated causal convolution (MRDC-Conv) … to capture the harmonic structure in logarithmic scale spectrograms" — [HarmoF0 (paperswithcode)](https://ml.paperswithcode.com/paper/harmof0-logarithmic-scale-dilated-convolution)
- **Linear-axis harmonic-aware alternative:** "Harmonic Convolution" (Zhang, Wang, Gan, Wu, Tenenbaum, Torralba, Freeman; ICLR 2020) builds kernels around the harmonic series instead of local neighbourhoods. It was shown on unsupervised audio restoration and source separation — [ICLR 2020 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2020/poster/1684); [MIT-IBM summary](https://mitibm.mit.edu/research/blog/deep-audio-priors-emerge-from-harmonic-convolutional-networks/). "Harmonic Lowering" (Takeuchi, Kashino, Ohishi, Saruwatari; Interspeech 2020) computes harmonic convolution faster. It unrolls the input into a redundant layout so ordinary convolution applies, for 2–7× speed-ups with no approximation — [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2020/takeuchi20_interspeech.html)

### Inferences
- **Why stacking is exact on a linear axis (my derivation):** take a linear grid f_k = k·Δ starting at 0 Hz (Δ = 0.61 Hz now). Then the h-th integer harmonic of bin k sits exactly on bin h·k. A "linear HCQT" channel is therefore a strided gather, H_lin[h, t, k] = S[t, h·k], with no interpolation for integer h. Subharmonics f/2 and f/3 land on half- and third-bins for odd k and need interpolation, or a finer base grid.
- **Pitfall the log version does not have:** a fish's f0 is not on a bin centre. Harmonic h of a true f0 at fractional index k0 lies at h·k0, up to h/2 bins from the sampled position h·round(k0). For h ≥ 3–4 a plain gather can step off the spectral main lobe. The fix is to max-pool or sum-pool S over a window of about h bins centred on h·k before the stride. This is the same tolerance a classical harmonic-group search uses, and wavetracker's harmonic groups already encode it.
- **Equivariance after stacking:** a fish shifted by Δf Hz moves its h-th harmonic by h·Δf Hz in S, but by exactly Δf/Δ bins in *every* stacked channel. So a conv over k on H_lin is (approximately) equivariant to f0 shifts. The same property is why HCQT works for convs on a log axis. On a raw linear spectrogram, a conv cannot be harmonically equivariant: the h=2 peak sits f0 bins away from the fundamental, so the distance depends on f0. A rise also moves the h-th harmonic h times faster in S, while in stacked coordinates all channels move together, which suits temporal kernels.
- **Residual non-equivariance on a linear axis:** in H_lin the h-th channel's peak width shrinks to about 1/h bins in the stacked coordinate (the main-lobe width is constant in Hz in S). Mains-hum combs stay at fixed absolute frequencies, so they do not shift along with fish. Harmonics above Nyquist are missing: at 20 kHz lab sampling, a 2000 Hz fish's h=5 sits at 10 kHz, which is Nyquist. Mask those channels per bin instead of feeding zeros silently.
- **Why log-frequency does not fit (my arithmetic):** 20-cent bins (deep salience) are 11.6 Hz wide at 1000 Hz and 23 Hz wide at 2000 Hz. 1/3-semitone bins (Basic Pitch, PESTO) are 9.7 Hz wide at 500 Hz. Fish 3 Hz apart at 600 Hz differ by 8.6 cents, and at 1000 Hz by 5.2 cents, so both fall well inside one music bin. Keeping 0.61 Hz at 2000 Hz on a log grid needs about 2,270 bins/octave, or about 11,500 bins over 60–2000 Hz, against about 3,180 linear bins. It would oversample 60 Hz about 34-fold (0.018 Hz bins). A true constant-Q filterbank at that Q would need windows of tens of seconds at 60 Hz. Conclusion: keep the linear STFT and do harmonic stacking by gather and pool. A log axis would only make sense as a resampled view for a low-resolution "context" branch.
- **Target blur must shrink:** "zero within a quarter tone" is ±23 Hz at 800 Hz, which would merge fish more than 10 Hz apart into one blob. For EODs the blur σ should be ≲ 1 bin (≈0.6 Hz), so two fish 3 Hz apart (≈5 bins) stay as two peaks. Better still, define it in Hz rather than bins (see memory note: physical units). The authors' warning that blurred targets create clustered supra-threshold bins means peak picking needs a minimum peak distance below ~3 Hz, or a non-maximum suppression aware of the target width.
- **Threshold calibration did not transfer** between datasets (0.3 vs 0.05). For faint fish, it is better to pass the continuous salience on to the tracker and linker as a score with a low acceptance threshold than to hard-threshold at the detector. The model's high-precision, low-recall bias on non-dominant voices is the same bias that would hurt failure mode (a), faint fish, unless training mixtures contain many labelled faint and inner lines.
- **The subharmonic channel (h = 0.5, and probably 1/3)** maps directly onto failure mode (c), harmonics of low-frequency fish read as false fish. A candidate at 2·f_low has strong energy at f/2. Including f/2 and f/3 channels is cheap and well motivated.
- **Kernel extent in frequency:** deep salience needed a 14-semitone kernel to see octave relations. With explicit sub- and super-harmonic channels, octave information is already in the channel dimension, so the frequency kernel only has to span neighbour interactions. About ±10 bins (≈±6 Hz) covers two fish that share one peak.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full Harmonic Convolution (ICLR 2020) text (OpenReview blocked). The exact operator is not verified here. My recollection is that it samples a linear-frequency STFT at harmonic multiples k·ω/n with an "anchor" n and interpolates fractional bins; treat that as unverified.
- The melody-extraction results and the conclusion/future-work section of deep salience were not extracted.
- I found no published multi-f0 network that does harmonic stacking on a *linear* STFT axis at sub-Hz resolution. Absence of evidence after limited searching, not proof.

## 2. Basic Pitch and later multi-f0 / transcription models (Onsets and Frames, MT3, harmonic-aware convolutions, 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Basic Pitch (ICASSP 2022) shows that a tiny model (16,782 parameters) using shifted-CQT harmonic stacking (7 harmonics plus 1 subharmonic, 3 bins per semitone), a limited frequency receptive field and three sigmoid heads (pitch, note, onset) is competitive at multi-pitch estimation. All the music models handle unknown polyphony the same way: independent per-bin sigmoids followed by peak picking. Their frequency grids are semitone-derived (20–33 cents), far too coarse for EODs. Note-level models (Onsets and Frames, MT3) quantise to semitones and do not fit continuous EOD lines. Recent self-supervised multi-pitch work (SS-MPE, 2024) is the closest 2023–2026 idea, but it still trails supervised models.

### Cited Findings
- **Basic Pitch ("NMP"), Bittner, Bosch, Rubinstein, Meseguer-Brocal, Ewert; ICASSP 2022 (arXiv 2203.09893):** fully convolutional, with "a total of only 16,782 parameters". Three posteriorgram outputs: Yo (onset), Yn (note activity) and Yp (pitch activity). Yo and Yn have 1 bin per semitone, Yp has 3 bins per semitone, all at the CQT frame rate — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Basic Pitch input:** CQT with 3 bins/semitone, hop ≈ 11 ms, 22,050 Hz audio, 2-s training excerpts. "Harmonic stacking" with 7 harmonics and 1 sub-harmonic, done by copying and shifting the CQT. The Yp branch resembles deep salience with fewer layers and uses "the same octave plus one semitone-sized kernel in frequency, which we found to be helpful for avoiding octave mistakes". "An added benefit to using a limited receptive field in frequency is that it removes the need for pitch shifting data augmentations" — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Multi-output design:** Yp is "closest" to the audio. Yn is computed from Yp by two small conv layers ("musical quantization"). Yo is computed from Yn plus audio features, following Onsets and Frames. The loss is the sum of three BCEs. Onsets use class-balanced BCE with weights 0.05 (negative) and 0.95 (positive) because Yo is very sparse. Augmentations: noise, EQ filters, reverb. Adam, learning rate 1e-3, batch 16 — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Ablations:** removing harmonic stacking ("NMP − H") "substantially reduces performance across all metrics and datasets". Supervising Yp improves frame-level note accuracy everywhere, with mixed effects on note F-measures — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Decoding and pitch bends:** multi-pitch estimates are made by "simply peak picking Yp across frequency and retaining all peaks greater than τn". Note events come from onset peaks (> 0.5), traced through Yn with an 11-frame tolerance; notes shorter than ≈120 ms are dropped. The authors state that Yp's 3-bin/semitone output can still give continuous pitch "by using the amplitude values of the estimated f0 bin, and those of its neighboring bins". The repo describes the tool as "audio-to-MIDI converter with pitch bend detection" — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893); [spotify/basic-pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)
- **Basic Pitch training data:** public datasets only (Molina, GuitarSet, MAESTRO, Slakh, Phenicx, iKala, MedleyDB), via mirdata. The MedleyDB and iKala note labels were generated automatically with pyin-notes — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Basic Pitch multi-pitch results:** frame accuracy 72.5 ± 3.8 vs 55.7 ± 2.9 (deep salience) on Bach10, and 37.7 ± 15.4 vs 43.6 ± 7.9 on Su. The authors call this "small-scale validation". The SS-MPE paper's re-evaluation gave Basic Pitch P/R/Acc 90.2/75.7/70.0 (Bach10), 88.2/71.1/64.9 (URMP) and 54.2/44.1/32.8 (Su) — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893); [SS-MPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15569v1)
- **Basic Pitch efficiency:** on a 2017 MacBook Pro CPU, a 7 min 45 s file took 24 s at 951 MB peak memory. MI-AMT (U-Net with attention, > 20M parameters) took 96 s at 3.3 GB. Onsets and Frames peaked at 5.4 GB — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Onsets and Frames (Hawthorne et al.):** described in the Basic Pitch paper as a piano model that "jointly predicts onset and note posteriorgrams using a CNN and RNN consisting of approximately 18M parameters". It reaches 95.2% Fno on MAESTRO vs 70.9% for Basic Pitch — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893). **Conflict:** HPPNet's Table 1 lists the Onsets & Frames baseline at about 4.3M + 3.5M parameters — [HPPNet arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14339). The two papers probably refer to different O&F versions; unresolved.
- **HPPNet (Wei, Li, Yu, Li; ISMIR 2022):** harmonic dilated convolution (log axis) plus a frequency-grouped recurrent network "to model pitch invariance over time". On MAESTRO: frame F1 93.15%, note F1 97.18%, with about 421K (acoustic model) + 99K (frequency-grouped LSTM) parameters — [HPPNet arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14339); [ISMIR 2022 poster](https://ismir2022program.ismir.net/static/posters/32.pdf)
- **HarmoF0 (ICME 2022):** single-f0, log-scale multi-rate dilated convolutions — [HarmoF0](https://ml.paperswithcode.com/paper/harmof0-logarithmic-scale-dilated-convolution). On clean MDB-stem-synth it scored 97.94% RPA, slightly above RMVPE's 97.11% (as reported by the RMVPE paper) — [RMVPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15412v1)
- **MT3 (Gardner, Simon, Manilow, Hawthorne, Engel; ICLR 2022):** an off-the-shelf T5 Transformer that transcribes "arbitrary combinations of musical instruments" from six multitrack datasets, reaching state-of-the-art per dataset — [MT3 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03017v4); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/mt3-multi-task-multitrack-music-transcription/). A secondary summary describes the output as musical events tokenised into a compact vocabulary for seq2seq decoding — [liner.com review (secondary)](https://liner.com/review/mt3-multitask-multitrack-music-transcription)
- **2023–2026 self-supervised multi-pitch — SS-MPE (Cwitkowitz & Duan; arXiv 2402.15569, Feb 2024, preprint):**
  - *Input and architecture:* HCQT with h = {0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, 60 bins/octave, 440 bins from 27.5 Hz, hop 256 at 22.05 kHz, dB clipped to [−80, 0] and scaled to [0, 1]. A fully convolutional 2-D autoencoder (from Timbre-Trap) with about 545K parameters.
  - *Objectives* (summed, unweighted): a "harmonic" positive-only BCE toward a weighted harmonic average X̄ = Σ X_h / h⁴; a "support" negative-only BCE against bins with no first-harmonic energy; L1 sparsity; timbre invariance under random Gaussian EQ curves; and geometric equivariance under frequency shifts up to ±2 octaves, time shifts and time-stretch γ ∈ [0.5, 2].
  - *Training and decoding:* trained on unlabelled NSynth single notes, 3–4 days on one RTX 2080 Ti. Peak picking with an untuned threshold of 0.5.
  - *Results and stated limits:* P/R/Acc of 65.1/82.5/57.1 (Bach10), 61.6/63.9/44.4 (URMP) and 50.4/52.8/34.1 (Su). The authors note sensitivity to false alarms from harmonics and percussive artefacts, and missed F0s — [SS-MPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15569v1)
- **Follow-up (Cwitkowitz & Duan, ISMIR 2025; arXiv 2506.23371):** adding the self-supervised objectives to supervised MPE produced a model that "simultaneously overfits to the supervised data while degenerating on data used for self-supervision only" — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23371v1); [ISMIR 2025 program](https://ismir2025program.ismir.net/poster_261.html)
- **Multi-instrument via clustering (Tanaka et al., ISMIR 2020):** an instrument-independent multi-pitch estimator produces a "condensed pitchgram", which deep spherical clustering of spectrogram and pitchgram then splits into "a specified number of musical instrument parts" — [ISMIR 2020 paper](https://program.ismir2020.net/static/final_papers/6.pdf); [poster page](https://program.ismir2020.net/poster_3-01.html)

### Inferences
- **Polyphony:** deep salience, Basic Pitch, HPPNet and SS-MPE all treat polyphony as unknown and variable, via per-bin sigmoids, peak picking and a threshold. No count head is needed, which suits 0–30+ fish. None was trained or tested anywhere near 30 simultaneous f0s; typical test polyphony is 1–5 (Bach10 is 4-part).
- **Frequency resolution and sub-bin handling:** deep salience uses 20 cents with argmax-level peaks. Basic Pitch's Yp uses 33 cents, with neighbour-bin interpolation for bends. HPPNet and MT3 work at the semitone/note level. None reaches the < 1 Hz needed here, and none regresses an explicit sub-bin offset. For EODs I would add an offset-regression channel (predict the Hz offset of the true f0 from the bin centre at each positive bin), or use a 3-bin parabolic or weighted refinement. A wide window would mix neighbouring fish (see RMVPE's 9-bin average in §3).
- **Data needed:** deep salience used about 10 h; Basic Pitch used seven public datasets; SS-MPE needed no labels but trails supervised models by 10–25 accuracy points on most sets. The benchmark's unlimited mixtures from train sources are a strong position compared with music, where labelled polyphonic f0 data is the bottleneck. Supervised training with exact labels should be the default; self-supervision is at best a regulariser. Given the ISMIR 2025 degeneration finding, it would need careful checks.
- **Multi-head idea worth copying:** Basic Pitch's supervised bottleneck (Yp feeding Yn feeding Yo) and its class-balanced sparse-event head carry over. Candidates: a fundamental-salience head plus a "harmonic-of-another-fish" or "spurious line" head, which attacks failure mode (c) directly; a rare-event head for chirps and rise onsets with heavy positive weighting; and possibly a "two fish in this peak" head for failure mode (b). The note and onset heads themselves are irrelevant.
- **Note-level, semitone-quantised models (O&F, MT3, HPPNet) do not transfer** to continuous EOD lines. MT3's "instrument" tokens give class identity, not individual identity, so they cannot separate 30 same-species fish. Its autoregressive token output is also a poor match for dense frame-level f0 at 0.6 Hz.
- **SS-MPE's harmonic prior** (Σ X_h / h⁴) is a music spectral-decay assumption. EOD harmonic profiles differ by species (some wave-type fish are near-sinusoidal, others harmonic-rich), so any self-supervised "harmonic" target would need a learned or species-agnostic weighting. Its stated weakness, false alarms on harmonics, is exactly failure mode (c).

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Onsets and Frames architecture, its versions, or its parameter count from the primary paper; the two secondary numbers above conflict.
- MT3's exact token vocabulary, time resolution and data hours were not verified from the full text.
- 2023–2026 coverage is incomplete. I did not survey, for example, the TISMIR 2025 extended PESTO for any multi-pitch variant, YourMT3+, or transformer-based multi-f0 salience models. Only SS-MPE and its ISMIR 2025 follow-up were checked.
- No found multi-f0 model reports sub-semitone accuracy for *two simultaneous sources a few cents apart*. That is the regime that matters here, and I found no evidence on it.

## 3. Single-f0 trackers (CREPE, SPICE, PESTO, RMVPE, FCPE): what carries over to many simultaneous lines

### Takeaway
The single-f0 models contribute three ideas that carry over: Gaussian-blurred classification targets with local weighted-average decoding (CREPE, RMVPE), self-supervision through transposition equivariance (SPICE, PESTO), and resolution-preserving architectures (PESTO keeps full frequency resolution "to distinguish close but different pitches"). Their softmax or argmax single-label outputs, mel or log inputs, and 20–33-cent grids do not carry over. Equivariance self-supervision holds on a linear axis only after harmonic stacking.

### Cited Findings
- **CREPE (Kim, Salamon, Li, Bello; ICASSP 2018):**
  - *Input and network:* 1024 samples of raw audio at 16 kHz (64 ms). Six conv layers lead to a 2048-d latent and a dense layer of 360 sigmoid outputs (20-cent bins, C1–B7, 32.70–1975.5 Hz).
  - *Targets and decoding:* Gaussian-blurred targets with 25-cent standard deviation, BCE loss, batch norm and dropout 0.25. The estimate is the weighted average of bin pitches by output activation.
  - *Data:* MDB-stem-synth is 230 MedleyDB stems (25 instruments, 15.56 h) re-synthesised by analysis/synthesis "with a perfect f0 annotation that maintains" timbre; RWC-synth is 6.16 h. Synthesised datasets were chosen because manual annotation does not "guarantee a 100% perfect match".
  - *Results:* on MDB-stem-synth, RPA 0.967 vs 0.919 (pYIN) and 0.925 (SWIPE); with a 10-cent tolerance, 0.909 vs 0.826 and 0.816. More noise-robust than both — [CREPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06182)
- **CREPE size:** 22.2M parameters (as tabulated by PESTO) — [PESTO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265)
- **SPICE (Gfeller et al.), as described by PESTO:** self-supervised. It takes CQT frames of pitch-shifted inputs, "learns the transposition between these", and reconstructs the CQT frame. Absolute pitch is set by calibrating an offset on synthetic data. 2.38M parameters, trained on private data; RPA 90.6% (MIR-1K) and 89.1% (MDB-stem-synth) — [PESTO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265)
- **PESTO (Riou, Lattner, Hadjeres, Peeters; ISMIR 2023, extended in TISMIR, doi 10.5334/tismir.251):** [PESTO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265); [TISMIR version](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.251)
  - *Input:* single CQT frames from nnAudio (GPU), fmin 27.5 Hz, 3 bins/semitone, K = 99·3 bins.
  - *Simulated pitch shift:* crop the CQT at an offset k ∈ {−16…16} bins. This is "significantly faster" than phase-vocoder shifting and avoids boundary effects.
  - *Network:* layer norm, two 1-D convs with skip connections, four more 1-D convs (leaky ReLU 0.3, dropout 0.2), then a **Toeplitz fully-connected layer** (constant diagonals, m+n−1 parameters, equivalent to a convolution) and a softmax. Kernel and padding are chosen so frequency resolution "is never reduced", which "helps the model to distinguish close but different" pitches.
  - *Losses:* a class-based equivariance loss through a fixed projection (α = 2^{1/36}), an invariance cross-entropy between augmented views, and a shifted cross-entropy, weighted by a gradient-based scheme. Absolute pitch is argmax plus a fixed shift p0 calibrated on synthetic harmonic signals.
  - *Size and results:* 28.9k parameters. RPA 96.1% (MIR-1K) and 94.6% (MDB, cross-dataset), vs CREPE's 97.8% and 96.7%. Training needs only 545 MB of GPU memory.
  - *Ablations:* without L_equiv, or with a non-Toeplitz FC layer, the model collapses (RPA 5.8% and 5.7%). Without L_SCE, cross-dataset RPA drops 94.6 → 86.9%.
  - *Noise:* trained on clean vocals, RPA falls to 50.0% at 0 dB with background music. Mixing in background music at a random level β during training raises it to about 81–83% at 0 dB.
  - *Authors' framing:* resolution is "a sixth of a tone", but "could eventually be increased by changing the resolution of the CQT", and the work "paves the way towards self-supervised multi-pitch estimation".
- **RMVPE (Wei, Cao, Dan, Chen; Interspeech 2023):** [RMVPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15412v1); [Dream-High/RMVPE](https://github.com/Dream-High/RMVPE)
  - *Task:* extracts the *vocal* pitch directly from polyphonic music, without a separation front end.
  - *Input:* log-mel, 256 bins, 30–8000 Hz, 20 ms hop.
  - *Network and output:* deep residual U-Net, BiGRU (256) and sigmoid FC to 360 bins of 20 cents. Weighted cross-entropy with ω = 5 on one-hot targets.
  - *Decoding:* a local weighted average over the 9 bins centred on the argmax; voicing threshold 0.5.
  - *Results:* polyphonic RPA on MIR-1K 95.42% vs 91.05% (CREPE) and 88.63% (HarmoF0). At 0 dB, average RPA 93.60% vs CREPE 81.16%. No parameter count or speed reported.
- **FCPE:** a repository exists (MIT licence) — [CNChTu/FCPE](https://github.com/CNChTu/FCPE). I found no paper text describing it.

### Inferences
- **Equivariance self-supervision on a linear axis:** PESTO's core trick (crop-shift of the input ≡ transposition, so the output must shift by the same k) is false on a raw linear spectrogram, because harmonics move by h·Δ. It is true in the linear-gather harmonic stack from §1: re-gathering S at h·(k+Δ) simulates an exact f0 shift of Δ bins for every source in the frame. A "translate the stacked input, require the salience to translate by the same amount" consistency loss is therefore valid. It needs only the gather indices, not resynthesis. Caveats: mains-hum combs would wrongly move with the shift (mask them, or add hum after shifting), and Nyquist limits differ per channel.
- **Softmax/argmax heads are single-source by construction** (PESTO, CREPE-style decoding) and must become per-bin sigmoids for 30 fish. PESTO's class-based equivariance loss was defined on a probability distribution. A multi-label version would use per-bin BCE or MSE between shifted outputs, which is what SS-MPE did.
- **The Toeplitz / no-downsampling lesson** carries over directly: never pool in frequency if close lines must stay separable. Deep salience's no-pooling rule and PESTO's full-resolution 1-D convs agree on this.
- **Local weighted-average decoding** (CREPE over all bins, RMVPE over 9 bins) assumes one source per neighbourhood. At 0.61 Hz bins, a 9-bin window spans 5.5 Hz, so a neighbour fish within 3 Hz would bias the estimate. Use ≤ 3-bin refinement, or an explicit offset head.
- **Noise mixing as augmentation** (PESTO's β mixing, which lifted 0 dB performance a great deal) maps to adding real field-noise segments, including hum combs, at random levels to the clean-source mixtures. The benchmark already has lab and field-noise variants, so this costs little.
- **Viterbi or HMM smoothing** in single-f0 trackers is per-source. The existing frame-to-frame tracker plays that role for many sources. (My recollection is that the CREPE repo offers optional Viterbi smoothing; the ICASSP paper text I read does not mention Viterbi. Not re-verified.)
- **Raw-waveform CREPE-style inputs** would need ≈1.6 s windows for 0.61 Hz resolution (≈32k samples at 20 kHz), and they give up the explicit linear-frequency geometry. Spectrogram input is the better fit.

### Gaps
- SPICE's own paper (IEEE/ACM TASLP 2020; arXiv 1910.11664) was not read. The details above come via PESTO.
- FCPE: no technical details found (architecture, input, results).
- I did not determine whether the TISMIR PESTO version adds sub-bin decoding or multi-pitch experiments.
- CREPE's Viterbi option is not verified from a primary source this session.

## 4. Multi-pitch streaming and voice assignment (identity over time)

### Takeaway
Every learned streaming or voice-assignment method found assumes a small, fixed and known number of streams: 2 speakers (Liu & Wang 2018, uPIT plus factorial HMM), 4 SATB voices (Cuesta & Gómez 2022), or "a specified number" of instrument parts (Tanaka et al. 2020). They fail exactly where streams overlap in range: same-gender speakers, alto/tenor. Duan et al.'s constrained clustering found that timbre consistency alone is not enough. Nothing found handles 30 near-identical sources with frequent crossings. For EODs, identity is best kept in the tracker and linker, with learned per-detection embeddings (spatial electrode pattern plus local spectral shape) as extra costs.

### Cited Findings
- **Duan, Han, Pardo, "Multi-pitch Streaming of Harmonic Sound Mixtures", IEEE/ACM TASLP 22(1):138–150, 2014:** streaming is posed as constrained clustering, one cluster per source. The objective minimises within-cluster timbre inconsistency, with constraints on pairs of pitch estimates from their time–frequency relationships, and a new algorithm satisfies as many constraints as possible. Harmonic structure and the uniform discrete cepstrum (UDC) worked for music; MFCC and UDC for speech. "Timbre consistency alone is insufficient for effective streaming" — [Duan lab multipitch project page](https://labsites.rochester.edu/air/projects/multipitch/multipitch.html); [index record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/52138.htm). An ISMIR 2009 precursor was tested on ten 4-part Bach chorales — [ISMIR 2009 PDF](https://labsites.rochester.edu/air/publications/DuanHanPardo_HarmonicallyInformedMultipitchTracking_ISMIR2009.pdf)
- **Cuesta & Gómez, "Voice Assignment in Vocal Quartets Using Deep Learning Models Based on Pitch Salience", TISMIR 5(1):99–112, 2022:** [TISMIR article](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/121); [helenacuesta/voas-vocal-quartets](https://github.com/helenacuesta/voas-vocal-quartets)
  - *Pipeline:* the Late/Deep CNN multi-f0 salience (Cuesta et al., ISMIR 2020; 360 bins, 20 cents, 6 octaves from 32.7 Hz, 11 ms hop) feeds VoasCNN or VoasCLSTM. These split the mixture salience into **four fixed** per-voice salience maps (S, A, T, B).
  - *Architectures:* a shared trunk of 32 3×3, 32 3×3, 16 70×3 and 16 70×3 conv layers. Per-voice branches use two 3×3 convs (CNN) or two ConvLSTM layers (CLSTM), each ending in a sigmoid. Input patches are 360 × 128 frames (≈1.5 s).
  - *Training data:* synthetic, built from 5,381 public-domain 4-part scores. MIDI is turned into f0 tracks, with frame-wise Gaussian jitter (SD 5 bins), a 7-frame median filter and a 1-bin blur.
  - *Loss and decoding:* summed per-voice BCE. Decoding takes the per-voice argmax with tuned per-voice thresholds (≈0.15–0.29).
  - *Results:* both models beat an HMM baseline by about 10% F on synthetic data, and run in 20–30 s per example on CPU vs > 20 min for the baseline.
  - *Failure modes:* alto and tenor are the weakest voices because their ranges overlap ("voice confusion, not missed pitches"). Voice crossings are not modelled explicitly. In solo passages the same bin can go to several voices (VFA ≈ 50% in one example). There is no temporal-continuity constraint; Viterbi is listed as future work.
- **Cuesta et al. ISMIR 2020** ("Multiple F0 Estimation in Vocal Ensembles using Convolutional Neural Networks"): code at [helenacuesta/multif0-estimation-polyvocals](https://github.com/helenacuesta/multif0-estimation-polyvocals). Architecture details were not verified this session.
- **Liu & Wang, "Permutation Invariant Training for Speaker-Independent Multi-Pitch Tracking", ICASSP 2018:** two concurrent speakers. The pitch range 60–404 Hz is quantised into 67 bins at 24 bins/octave, plus an unvoiced state (68). A BLSTM has two 68-way softmax output layers, one per speaker. Utterance-level PIT picks the speaker-to-output permutation that minimises the utterance loss and keeps it for all frames. A speaker-dependent factorial HMM connects frame posteriors over time. On GRID, uPIT alone "does not generate promising results for same-gender speaker pairs", which needed a uPIT speech-separation front end and shared label permutations. Same-gender pairs are hard "because same-gender pairs have very close pitch ranges" — [Liu & Wang PDF](https://pnlwang.github.io/papers/Liu-Wang1.icassp18.pdf)
- **Tanaka et al. ISMIR 2020:** deep spherical clustering of a condensed pitchgram plus spectrogram into a specified number of parts. It uses timbral and pitch cues and handles unknown instruments. A later TISMIR paper moves the clustering to the note level — [ISMIR 2020 paper](https://program.ismir2020.net/static/final_papers/6.pdf); [poster page](https://program.ismir2020.net/poster_3-01.html)

### Inferences
- **Scale:** learned identity outputs with fixed slots (Cuesta: 4; Liu & Wang: 2) do not scale to a variable 0–30+ fish. PIT over 30 slots is computable with Hungarian matching, but the slot semantics are arbitrary, and crossings make the "which slot" decision ambiguous exactly where errors matter. Factorial HMMs blow up combinatorially: 68² states for 2 speakers, and impossible for 30 sources over about 3,000 bins.
- **Range-ordering identity cues fail here.** Cuesta's model relies on SATB voices sitting in different ranges; it confuses alto and tenor where they overlap. Liu & Wang struggle when pitch ranges are close. EOD fish are all "same-gender": same species, a few Hz apart, crossing. Pitch range carries almost no identity information.
- **Timbre ≈ harmonic profile is weak** for conspecific EODs, much as Duan found timbre alone insufficient. The strongest identity cue in this problem is the per-electrode amplitude pattern, which has no music or speech analogue (music models are mono). It drifts as fish move, so a cue built on it must model slow change; the current tracker's amplitude-similarity term already does this.
- **Most transferable idea: embedding-based (deep-clustering style) streaming** — per-detection or per-bin embeddings trained with a permutation-free loss, so the number of clusters is free at inference. It would feed the existing tracker or linker as an extra assignment cost, rather than replacing assignment. Tanaka et al. show such clustering on pitchgrams works for a few parts; nothing shows it for tens of near-identical sources.
- **Crossings:** no music or speech method found models crossings explicitly. EOD crossings are frequent and the main source of identity swaps and fragmentation (failure mode d), so the tracker or linker, not the salience network, should own crossing resolution. A salience network can still help by keeping two peaks through near-coincidence (failure mode b), e.g. via per-electrode channels and a "two sources here" head.

### Gaps
- Duan et al. 2014: I could not read the full text. Whether the number of sources is assumed known is my recollection (I believe so), not verified. Accuracy versus polyphony was not extracted.
- Liu & Wang 2018: numeric results (E_total, same- vs different-gender) were not extracted.
- I found no learned method that outputs both pitches and stream identity for more than 4 simultaneous sources, nor any that evaluates crossings explicitly. This search was limited (a few queries); speech multi-talker pitch tracking after 2018 (e.g. work combining separation and PIT pitch networks, mentioned in one snippet) was not followed up.

## 5. Practical facts: code, licences, sizes, speed, data amount, synthetic/re-synthesised labels

### Takeaway
Everything relevant is open source under permissive licences except PESTO (LGPL-3.0). The models are small (17k–545k parameters for the salience-style nets; CREPE 22M is the outlier). Inference is cheap: Basic Pitch transcribes about 8 min of audio in 24 s on a laptop CPU. The labels that worked best were re-synthesised from analysed f0 (MDB-stem-synth) or built from partial mixes with proxy labels (deep salience). A direct analogue of both exists for the EOD benchmark.

### Inferences
- **Compute is not the constraint.** A deep-salience-sized network over a linear stack (≈3,200 bins × 6–8 harmonic channels × per-electrode channels) is small next to an RTX 4080's 16 GB. Activation memory scales with bins × frames × channels, so per-electrode branches should share weights and be pooled early. Basic Pitch notes that parameter count and memory are not the same thing.
- **An "EOD-stem-synth"** is the direct analogue of MDB-stem-synth. Re-synthesise each train source from its tracked f0 trajectory and per-harmonic, per-electrode amplitudes, then mix. That gives exact f0 labels and removes the incomplete-negative problem (untracked real lines in the sources). The cost is a domain gap: real phase noise, EOD waveform detail and amplitude modulation are lost. The deep-salience "partial mix" approach is what the current benchmark already does (summed real recordings, single-source tracking as proxy truth). Its known weakness was proxy-label error from pYIN, and here the proxy is the single-source tracker. A mixed strategy fits the measured caveats: train on both re-synthesised and real mixtures, and mask the loss on real mixtures in time–frequency regions with unlabelled source energy.
- **Licence:** PESTO's LGPL-3.0 matters only if its code is vendored. The ideas (Toeplitz layer, equivariance loss) can be re-implemented freely.

### Gaps
- Deep salience's parameter count and inference speed are not stated in the paper; my estimate is unverified.
- The Basic Pitch train/test track counts per dataset could not be reliably mapped from the extracted table.

## 6. Fit to the EOD problem: what transfers, what breaks

### Takeaway
The architectural core transfers well: harmonic stacking (re-derived as a linear gather with sub- and super-harmonic channels), fully convolutional pooling-free salience, per-bin sigmoid multi-label output with peak picking, blurred targets, auxiliary heads, noise-mixing augmentation, and equivariance-based consistency. What breaks are the music-specific assumptions: log-frequency semitone grids (20–33 cents, i.e. 5–23 Hz at EOD frequencies), quarter-tone target blur and evaluation tolerance, mono input, few and distinct sources, onsets and notes, and 11 ms frames. Identity over time does not come from any music or speech method at this scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Music evaluation tolerance:** standard multi-f0/RPA tolerance is a quarter tone (50 cents) — [PESTO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265); [CREPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06182). Note-level F also uses a quarter-tone pitch window — [Basic Pitch arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)
- **Typical music polyphony in the benchmarks:** Bach10 is 4-part — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf). Vocal quartets have 4 voices — [TISMIR article](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/121). Speech multi-pitch has 2 speakers — [Liu & Wang PDF](https://pnlwang.github.io/papers/Liu-Wang1.icassp18.pdf)
- **Recall vs dominance:** deep salience retrieved dominant lines (melody, bass) and missed inner voices — [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf). SS-MPE false-alarmed on harmonics — [SS-MPE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15569v1)
- **Resolution-preserving design helps separate close pitches:** [PESTO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265); [Paper](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- **Same-range sources cause identity confusion:** [TISMIR article](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/121); [Liu & Wang PDF](https://pnlwang.github.io/papers/Liu-Wang1.icassp18.pdf)

### Inferences
**What breaks, and why:**
1. *Frequency grid and tolerance.* 50 cents is 23 Hz at 800 Hz, so a music-style metric would score a neighbouring fish as a hit. All music-derived resolutions and blurs must be re-specified in Hz. Evaluation must use Hz tolerances well under the minimum fish spacing; the project's benchmark already does this.
2. *Log axis.* Infeasible at the needed resolution (see §1 arithmetic). Use a linear gather for harmonic stacking.
3. *Polyphony and sameness.* Music sources are few and timbrally distinct, and sit on a semitone lattice that rarely puts two sources within 10 cents for long. EOD fish are many, near-identical, off-lattice and persistently close (< 3 Hz). Nothing in the music training regimes exposes the model to "two sources in one peak". Synthetic mixtures must over-sample close pairs and crossings deliberately.
4. *Harmonic overlap is systematic, not accidental.* In music, harmonic coincidence is mainly octave and fifth relations. Among fish spread over 60–2000 Hz, any low-frequency fish's h-th harmonic lands near some other fish's fundamental. Sub-harmonic channels (f/2, f/3) and a "harmonic-of-another-line" auxiliary target address failure mode (c) more directly than anything in the music literature.
5. *Mono vs multi-electrode.* All reviewed models are single-channel. The per-electrode spectrograms are the one cue that can split two fish in a single peak: different fish give different amplitude ratios across electrodes. The electrode count varies (2–16+), so per-electrode features need a shared encoder plus a permutation-invariant pooling (mean, max or attention) before the salience head. No music model provides this; it is new design work.
6. *Time scale.* Music models use about 11 ms hops and 50 ms kernels. Here frames are 0.16 s, lines are quasi-stationary over seconds, and chirps (~100 ms) are sub-frame. Longer temporal kernels (or a recurrent/temporal-conv stage like RMVPE's BiGRU or Cuesta's ConvLSTM) are justified to integrate faint lines over time (failure mode a). They risk smoothing rises, so rises need to be in the training mixtures. Chirps cannot be resolved at this frame rate by any salience design; a separate event head or a finer-time branch would be needed.
7. *Onset/note machinery.* Basic Pitch's note and onset heads are irrelevant. Only the sparse-event head design (heavily class-weighted BCE) carries over, for chirp and rise events.
8. *Identity.* No music or speech streaming method scales to 30 near-identical crossing sources (§4). Keep the tracker and linker. At most, add learned per-detection embeddings, with a weight-shared spatial-pattern encoder as the most promising cue.

**What transfers well (ranked by expected value, my judgement):**
- (i) Linear harmonic stack with h ∈ {1/3, 1/2, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, pooled per harmonic.
- (ii) A fully convolutional, pooling-free salience network with full frequency resolution and a narrow Hz-defined target blur.
- (iii) Per-bin sigmoid plus peak picking, emitting the continuous salience to the tracker instead of a hard threshold.
- (iv) An explicit sub-bin offset head.
- (v) Auxiliary heads for harmonic, spurious lines and "two-in-one-peak".
- (vi) Real-noise mixing augmentation at random levels.
- (vii) Shift-consistency regularisation in stacked coordinates (PESTO/SS-MPE-style), used only as an auxiliary loss and with the ISMIR 2025 degeneration caveat.
- (viii) Re-synthesised sources for exact labels, combined with real mixtures under masked loss.

**Expected limits:** music salience models showed high precision and low recall on non-dominant sources. If the EOD model inherits that, the hardest frames (< 6 dB SNR, failure mode a) may still be missed. Some of those truth frames are below the noise by construction (the truth caveat), so recall there is bounded no matter the method. Detection metrics should be stratified by SNR, so that improvements on recoverable frames are not hidden by unrecoverable ones.

### Gaps
- No music or speech study found measures multi-f0 performance as a function of inter-source spacing in Hz or cents. Transferability to sources < 3 Hz apart is therefore untested and must be measured on the EOD benchmark.
- No reviewed method uses multi-channel (multi-microphone) input for multi-f0 salience. Spatial-cue designs need sources outside this scope, e.g. multi-channel speech separation or array processing, which another researcher may cover.
- No direct evidence on how many synthetic-mixture hours a linear-axis salience net needs at this resolution. Music figures (≈10 h for deep salience) are only loose anchors.
