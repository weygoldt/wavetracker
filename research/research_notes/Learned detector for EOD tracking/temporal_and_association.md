# Temporal context and joint detection/association architectures for EOD-line detection and tracking

Scope: (1) how to give a per-frame time-frequency (TF) detector temporal context; (2) whether and how to predict identity cues jointly (detection + embedding, per-bin embeddings, curve-as-object, end-to-end trackers). Judged against the four measured failure modes of the dense benchmark: (a) faint-fish misses (<6 dB SNR), (b) lines within 3 Hz, (c) false lines (harmonics, strays) that become long false identities, (d) fragmentation. Target metric: IDF1 (now ~0.61-0.65).

Notation used in Inferences: "frame" = one wavetracker spectrogram frame (0.16 s); "bin" = 0.61 Hz. All receptive fields (RF) are given in seconds and Hz, not samples, because the user's spectrogram is defined in physical units.

---

## 1. Temporal context for a per-frame TF detector (CRNN, TCN/dilated conv, U-Net, Conformer, transformers, axial attention, SSMs): use in multi-f0 / SED / contour tracing, receptive field, and evidence of gain for weak continuous lines

### Takeaway
Every strong audio-detection family adds temporal context beyond the local conv kernel (BiLSTM/BiGRU, Conformer, or alternating time/frequency sequence models), and the one clean ablation found (Onsets and Frames) shows a ~10-point frame-F1 drop when all recurrent layers are removed; the original deep-salience model had only ~0.17 s of temporal RF and its authors named recurrent layers as the next step. For weak lines specifically, the closest evidence (passive-sonar "lofargram" lines, dolphin-whistle contours) says that learned TF-image models detect lines far below per-frame visibility, which is what temporal integration along a line buys.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline: deep salience has very little temporal context**
- Deep salience (Bittner et al., ISMIR 2017) is a 5-layer fully convolutional net on a harmonic CQT (HCQT; harmonics h = 0.5,1,2,3,4,5 stacked as channels so that one local kernel sees f0 and its harmonics). Layers: 128 and 64 filters of 5x5 ("~1 semitone and 50 ms"), two layers of 64 3x3, final 8 filters of 70x3 (14 semitones), no pooling "since we do not want to be invariant to small shifts in time frequency"; hop ≈ 11 ms; output is a per-bin sigmoid salience trained with cross-entropy against Gaussian-blurred f0 targets — [Bittner et al. 2017, ISMIR](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000085.pdf)
- Decoding is peak picking with a single global threshold tuned on validation; thresholding at 0.5 is avoided because Gaussian-blurred targets produce "multiple estimates very close to one another". On Bach10 the validation-chosen threshold (0.3) was far from the optimum (0.05), and the authors attribute the performance gap to recall — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000085.pdf)
- The authors' own conclusion: "there is a wide space of model architectures that can be explored to add more temporal information, such as recurrent neural networks" — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000085.pdf)
- The HCQT design is motivated by the fact that aligning harmonics lets "a series of local convolutions capture relationships across frequencies that would require much larger receptive fields when using, for example, a single CQT" — [Bittner et al. 2018, multitask f0 (arXiv preprint)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00381)

**CRNN: the standard way to add temporal context (SED, transcription)**
- CRNN for polyphonic sound event detection (Çakır et al., IEEE/ACM TASLP 2017): CNN for local spectro-temporal patterns + RNN for longer temporal context; compared against CNN, RNN and other methods on four datasets with "considerable improvement" for the CRNN; the paper won the IEEE SPS Best Paper Award 2023 — [Çakır et al. 2017, arXiv](https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/1702.06286/); [award page](https://www.cs.tut.fi/~heittolt/articles/ieee-signal-processing-societys-best-paper-award-2023). A secondary summary gives average absolute F1 gains of ~6.6-13.6% over CNN/RNN baselines (mapping to baselines unclear, not verified in the paper) — [Emergent Mind summary](https://api.emergentmind.com/papers/1702.06286)
- Onsets and Frames (Hawthorne et al., ISMIR 2018): conv acoustic model + BiLSTM (128 units each direction); log-mel input, hop 512 at 16 kHz (32 ms); trained on 20 s splits, inference on the full un-split file. Ablation (MAPS, Table 2): full model frame F1 78.30 / note F1 82.29 / note-with-offset F1 50.22; "(k) No LSTM, shared conv" 67.60 / 75.34 / 37.03; "(h) Shared conv" (LSTMs kept) 76.85 / 81.64 / 43.61; forward-only (causal) LSTMs (c) 75.98 / 80.77 / 46.36 and (d) 76.30 / 82.27 / 49.50. Authors: "forward-only RNNs have only a small accuracy impact as they can be used for online piano transcription" — [Hawthorne et al. 2018 (arXiv 1710.11153)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.11153)
- Other piano-transcription papers report smaller LSTM-removal effects: HPPNet reports that F1 with a normal LSTM "decreases about three percentage points and one percentage point on frame-wise and note-wise" relative to its variant; a 2026 preprint reports 4.10 points lost in onset/offset F1 when removing the LSTM from its SFT-CRNN — [HPPNet (arXiv 2208.14339)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14339); [distribution-matching preprint (arXiv 2605.17405)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17405)

**Self-attention / Conformer**
- Conformer = self-attention + depthwise convolution in one block (Gulati et al., Interspeech 2020) — [Gulati et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08100)
- Conformer SED (Miyazaki et al.) won DCASE 2020 Task 4: 51.1% event-based macro F1 on the evaluation set, best of 72 submissions; 50.7% on validation vs 34.8% baseline (the baseline is a CRNN, and model/training also differ, so this is not a controlled Conformer-vs-CRNN comparison). Earlier self-attention SED (ICASSP 2020, DCASE 2019 data): CRNN baseline 30.61% → 34.28% — [DCASE 2020 tech report](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2020/technical_reports/DCASE2020_Miyazaki_108.pdf); [LY Corp publication page](https://research.lycorp.co.jp/en/publications/1411); [ICASSP 2020 slides](https://de.slideshare.net/NU_I_TODALAB/weaklysupervised-sound-event-detection-with-selfattention)

**Dilated / temporal convolutional networks**
- A generic TCN (dilated causal 1-D convs + residuals) "outperforms canonical recurrent networks such as LSTMs across a diverse range of tasks and datasets, while demonstrating longer effective memory" (Bai, Kolter, Koltun 2018, arXiv preprint) — [Bai et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271)

**Axial (alternating time-axis / frequency-axis) models on spectrograms**
- Axial attention factorises 2-D attention into one attention along each axis (Ho et al. 2019, arXiv preprint) — [Ho et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12180)
- TF-GridNet (Wang et al., IEEE/ACM TASLP 2023) alternates an intra-frame (across-frequency) BLSTM, a sub-band (across-time) BLSTM and full-band self-attention on a complex STFT — a state-of-the-art speech separator — [Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12433)
- BS-RoFormer (Lu et al., ICASSP 2024) interleaves transformers along time and along frequency (band-split) for music source separation — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02612)
- Dual-path RNN (Luo et al., ICASSP 2020) handles very long sequences by chunking: an intra-chunk RNN and an inter-chunk RNN alternate — [Luo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06379)

**State-space models (S4 / Mamba)**
- S4 (Gu et al., ICLR 2022) and Mamba (Gu & Dao 2023, arXiv preprint; selective SSM, linear time in sequence length) — [S4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396); [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)
- SELD-Mamba (Aug 2024, arXiv preprint) replaced Conformer blocks with bidirectional Mamba in sound-event localisation and detection on DCASE 2024 Task 3 data and reports effectiveness — [Mu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05057?context=cs)
- BioMamba (Dec 2025, arXiv preprint) on the BEANS bioacoustics benchmark: "comparable performance with AVES [a transformer] while consum[ing] significantly less VRAM"; the authors state no prior Mamba work on animal-sound detection — [Tang & Baskiyar 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03563v1)
- SSAMBA (May 2024, arXiv preprint): similar or better than transformer SSAST on audio event classification — [SSAMBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11831v1)
- I found no Mamba/SSM work on pitch or multi-f0 estimation (search returned none).

**Closest domains: dim narrowband lines and tonal contours**
- DeepLofargram (2019, arXiv preprint): a CNN performs a binary detection test on noise-dominated lofargrams (passive-sonar spectrograms) and a visualisation/saliency branch recovers line pixels; detection limit "about −24 dB on average and −26 dB for some cases"; time integration helps classical detectors but "does not hold for irregularly fluctuating lines". Prior state of the art cited: contour energy minimisation and HMMs — [DeepLofargram (arXiv 1912.00605)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00605); [search summary of paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00605)
- Multiple DeepLofargram (JASA 2025): weakly supervised detection and recovery of multiple dim lines with a "recovery loss" to keep lines continuous — [JASA 2025](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/160/3/2608/3405449/Multiple-DeepLofargram-Weakly-supervised-detection?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- DeepWhistle (Li et al., IJCNN 2020): a network on spectrogram patches predicts which TF peaks belong to whistles, trained on synthetic data built from background recordings; it learns "archetypical patterns, such as crossings and frequency-modulated sweeps" — [St Andrews portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/learning-deep-models-from-synthetic-data-for-extracting-dolphin-w/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341478046_Learning_Deep_Models_from_Synthetic_Data_for_Extracting_Dolphin_Whistle_Contours). Integrated into the graph tracker Silbido as "Silbido Profundo": precision >96% and recall ~80% on a hard two-species benchmark subset — [Silbido profundo record](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftstandrewserep:oai:research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk:10023%2F26799)
- SAM-whistle (Sci. Rep. 2025): Segment-Anything encoder adapted to produce whistle energy maps, contours formed by a rule-based graph search; F1 0.897 (P 0.920, R 0.876); 3 s windows with whistles carried across window boundaries; authors say a learned tracker could improve "complex and overlapping patterns" and would need adaptation "to separate very close harmonics" — [SAM-whistle (Research Square preprint)](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-6550893/v1/539bcbb0f2421a650fd76385.pdf); [Sci. Rep. article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-21642-x)
- "Mind the Gap" (Marine Mammal Science 2026) compares Silbido Profundo, SAM-whistle, CREPE-tt and SMC-PHD and names overlapping harmonics, low SNR and multi-loop shapes as the typical failures — [Tyarks et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mms.70263) (full text not retrieved; numbers not extracted)

### Inferences
- **RF of the baseline.** From the kernel sizes above, deep salience's temporal RF is 1+4+4+2+2+2 = 15 frames ≈ 0.17 s at 11 ms hop (my calculation). The same stack on wavetracker's 0.16 s frames would span ~2.4 s, so kernel sizes "in frames" mean very different physical context depending on hop; RF should be specified in seconds.
- **What RF is needed here (judgement from the problem physics, not literature).** (i) Faint-line recall: integrating along a line over N frames raises effective SNR roughly with √N for a stationary line (coherent-integration intuition); the DeepLofargram −24 dB result is consistent with large learned integration windows, but its window length was not retrieved. Seconds (≈10-60 frames) of context are the relevant scale. (ii) Close lines / crossings: two lines a few Hz apart drifting at ~0.1-1 Hz/s overlap for seconds to tens of seconds, so disambiguating which branch continues where needs context longer than the overlap; a local CNN (≤ 2-3 s) cannot do it, a BiGRU/Conformer/Mamba over 30-120 s can in principle. (iii) Rises (~60 Hz in seconds) need the model not to over-smooth in frequency; chirps (<1 frame) are invisible at 0.16 s hop and should be handled separately.
- **Which family.** Given a CNN-literate user and a 16 GB GPU: a CNN front-end (2-D, harmonic-aware) followed by a bidirectional GRU or a stack of dilated 1-D convs along time, applied per frequency bin (TF-GridNet's "sub-band across-time" pattern), is the lowest-risk way to get 10-100 s of context. Self-attention along time is cheap (a 60 s chunk = 375 tokens); attention along a full linear frequency axis (thousands of bins) is expensive, so frequency-axis context is better obtained with convs/harmonic stacking or banded attention. Mamba is an option for very long time context with linear cost but has no pitch-domain track record (gap).
- **Failure-mode mapping.** Temporal context should help (a) faint recall and (d) fragmentation most directly (the detector can bridge brief dropouts if trained with continuous targets), and (c) false-line suppression partly (a stray line lacks the long-term co-modulation with a fundamental that a true harmonic has; a long time RF lets the model see that a candidate co-varies with a lower line at 1/k of its frequency). It helps (b) close lines only if the output can represent two peaks within 3 Hz (output resolution, target width) — a pure salience map with Gaussian-blurred targets merges them regardless of temporal context.

### Gaps
- No controlled ablation of temporal context specifically for multi-f0 salience on weak, continuous tonal lines was found; Onsets and Frames (piano, onset-driven) is the cleanest ablation but its signal type differs.
- DCASE literature gives no explicit "required RF in seconds" figure that I could retrieve.
- DeepLofargram's input window length (frames/seconds) and its baselines' numbers were not in the abstract; full text not retrieved.
- No published SSM/Mamba model for pitch tracking or contour tracing was found.

## 2. Joint detection + embedding tracking from MOT (JDE, FairMOT, CenterTrack, Tracktor, ByteTrack, TrackFormer, MOTR, MOTRv2, MeMOTR, MOTIP, SambaMOTR): how a per-detection embedding head would work and what it would buy

### Takeaway
MOT evidence splits by regime: when objects look alike and association is the bottleneck (DanceTrack), appearance embeddings learned jointly with detection (FairMOT) or added to motion (DeepSORT) do not help or hurt, while motion models and query-based end-to-end trackers help; when detection is the bottleneck (MOT17), tracking-by-detection with a strong detector plus ByteTrack's low-score recovery wins. ByteTrack's two-stage association (match confident detections first, then let unmatched tracks claim low-score detections) is the most directly transferable idea for faint fish.

### Cited Findings

**Joint detection + embedding**
- JDE (Wang et al., ECCV 2020): one network outputs detections and per-detection appearance embeddings, replacing a separate re-ID model — [Wang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12605)
- FairMOT (Zhang et al., IJCV 2021): built on anchor-free CenterNet; argues anchors make re-ID training ambiguous ("one anchor may correspond to multiple identities and multiple anchors may correspond to one identity, especially in crowded scenes") and that detection and re-ID "need different features"; it treats both tasks equally rather than "detection first, re-ID secondary" — [FairMOT (arXiv 2004.01888)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01888)
- CenterTrack (Zhou et al., ECCV 2020): feeds the previous frame and previous-detection heatmap into a CenterNet detector which predicts per-object offsets to the previous frame — tracking as local displacement regression — [Zhou et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01177)
- Tracktor (Bergmann et al., ICCV 2019): uses the detector's box regression to carry each track into the next frame ("tracking without bells and whistles") — [Bergmann et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05625)

**ByteTrack (low-score detection recovery)**
- ByteTrack (Zhang et al., ECCV 2022) associates "almost every detection box instead of only the high score ones": high-score boxes are matched first, then unmatched tracklets are matched to low-score boxes by similarity, which recovers occluded/weak true objects and discards background; applied to 9 trackers it improves IDF1 by 1 to 10 points; MOT17 test: 80.3 MOTA, 77.3 IDF1, 63.1 HOTA at 30 FPS on a V100. Authors' caveat: taking every box naively "will introduce more false positives" — [ByteTrack ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/315_ECCV_2022_paper.php); [arXiv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2110.06864)

**When objects look identical: DanceTrack (CVPR 2022)**
- Dancers in near-identical clothing; re-ID features that separate on MOT17 are "highly entangled" on DanceTrack. Oracle (GT boxes): on MOT17 IoU-only association gives AssA 97.3; on DanceTrack AssA 53.6 with DetA/MOTA near 100 → association is the bottleneck — [DanceTrack (arXiv 2111.14690)](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3)
- Association ablation (val, YOLOX detector): IoU only HOTA 44.7 / AssA 25.3; SORT (Kalman) 47.8 / 31.0; DeepSORT (SORT + appearance) 45.8 / 29.7, i.e. adding appearance made it worse; OC-SORT 52.1 / 35.3; an LSTM motion model 51.6 HOTA — [DanceTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3)
- Extra cues (CenterNet + BYTE): boxes 36.9 HOTA; + segmentation masks 39.2; + pose 41.0; + (out-of-domain) depth 35.1 — [DanceTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3)
- Test-set HOTA, MOT17 vs DanceTrack: CenterTrack 52.2 / 41.8; FairMOT 59.3 / 39.7; QDTrack 53.9 / 54.2; MOTR 57.2 / 54.2 (AssA 40.2); ByteTrack 63.1 / 47.7 (AssA 32.1); OC-SORT 63.2 / 55.1 — [DanceTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3); OC-SORT is CVPR 2023 — [Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14360). (The DanceTrack repo lists ByteTrack at 47.1; the paper table says 47.7 — [DanceTrack GitHub](https://github.com/DanceTrack/DanceTrack).)

**Query-based end-to-end trackers**
- TrackFormer (CVPR 2022) and MOTR (ECCV 2022) carry each identity as a "track query" in a DETR decoder from frame to frame; new objects come from detect queries — [TrackFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02702); [MOTR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.03247)
- MOTRv2 (CVPR 2023): initialises proposal queries from a separately trained YOLOX detector (all boxes with score >0.05 kept "to maximize recall"); 69.9 HOTA on DanceTrack for the base model; 73.4 with extra association, train+val training and test ensemble; using YOLOX proposals improves HOTA, DetA and AssA — [MOTRv2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09791)
- MeMOTR (ICCV 2023): long-term memory buffer of track states; DanceTrack test HOTA 68.5 or 70.0 depending on detector — [MeMOTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15700); numbers via [SambaMOTR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01806)
- MOTIP (CVPR 2025): treats association as ID classification — past trajectory embeddings act as in-context ID prompts and the model predicts the ID label of each current detection; DanceTrack HOTA 67.5 (base) / 70.0 (DAB variant); SportsMOT 71.9 — [MOTIP (arXiv 2403.16848)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16848); [CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Gao_Multiple_Object_Tracking_as_ID_Prediction_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- SambaMOTR (ICLR 2025): synchronized set-of-sequences SSM modelling the interaction of all tracks jointly; +3.8 HOTA and +5.2 AssA over MeMOTR on DanceTrack; 67.2 (Deformable DETR) / 69.0 (DAB-D-DETR) HOTA — [Samba (arXiv 2410.01806)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01806); [GitHub](https://github.com/mattiasegu/sambamotr)
- Reproducibility caveat: an independent 2026 benchmark (arXiv preprint) reports SambaMOTR at 53.8 HOTA (large std) vs MOTIP 61.7 with the same detector — [arXiv 2609.22291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22291)
- Tracking-by-detection catches up: CAMELTrack (2025, arXiv preprint), an online tracking-by-detection method with learned multi-cue association, reports 69.3 HOTA on DanceTrack — [CAMELTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01257)

### Inferences
- **How an embedding head would look here.** On the salience map's output stride, add a per-(frame,bin) embedding head (e.g. 16-64 dims) read out at each detected peak; train with a contrastive/metric loss so peaks of the same fish across a time window are close and others far (JDE/FairMOT style; Payer et al. 2018 do exactly this with ConvGRU, see §3). The tracker's cost then combines |Δf|, electrode-amplitude distance and embedding distance; the fragment linker gets mean embeddings per fragment.
- **What it could buy over frequency + electrode amplitude.** Possible extra identity cues not in today's features: harmonic-amplitude profile (waveform shape, species/individual-specific), relative phase between harmonics, per-electrode phase. Whether these are individually distinctive among same-species fish in the mixtures is untested. DanceTrack is the cautionary result: when appearance is near-identical, adding it can lower AssA (DeepSORT 29.7 < SORT 31.0). Fish of one species with similar waveforms are the DanceTrack regime; mixed-species recordings are closer to MOT17.
- **What transfers with least risk.** ByteTrack's two-threshold association is a tracker change, not a network change, and targets failure mode (a) directly: let existing tracks claim sub-threshold salience peaks near their predicted frequency, while new identities require high salience — which also protects against (c), since false lines usually start as low-score detections. This is consistent with deep salience's own finding that its errors came from recall at a single global threshold.
- **Motion over appearance.** DanceTrack's results (Kalman/LSTM/OC-SORT beat appearance) suggest that a better learned frequency-motion model (predicting each track's next frequency, handling rises non-linearly as OC-SORT does) is a stronger identity cue than embeddings when fish look alike.
- **End-to-end query trackers** (MOTR family) win on DanceTrack mainly in association, but (i) they depend on a strong external detector (MOTRv2), (ii) published numbers were hard to reproduce independently, and (iii) 30+ simultaneous lines × hours of data is an unusual regime for them. Judgement: high cost, uncertain payoff for this project.

### Gaps
- FairMOT's and JDE's exact MOT17 IDF1 were not verified in this session (search surfaced conflicting reproductions).
- Training compute (GPU count/days) for MOTR-family models was not retrieved; I could not confirm whether they are trainable on one 16 GB GPU.
- No MOT benchmark with 30+ near-identical objects that frequently cross (closest: DanceTrack, up to 40 people, average 9 tracks per video) was found.

## 3. Per-TF-bin embeddings for source assignment (deep clustering, deep attractor networks, PIT, associative embedding, discriminative loss): identity across crossings and scaling to 30+ sources

### Takeaway
Per-bin embedding methods solve exactly the "which bin belongs to which source" problem and are count-agnostic (cluster at test time), and one (Payer et al. 2018) combines them with ConvGRUs to track identities through video; but identity is only kept within the window the network sees, permutation-based training costs O(C!) unless the assignment is solved with Hungarian matching (shown up to 20 speakers), and long recordings still require stitching identities across windows.

### Cited Findings
- Deep clustering (Hershey et al., ICASSP 2016): a network maps each TF bin to an embedding so that bins dominated by the same source cluster together; separation by k-means; trained on two-speaker mixtures, it generalised to three-speaker mixtures — [Hershey et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04306)
- Deep attractor network (Chen, Luo, Mesgarani, ICASSP 2017): pulls TF-bin embeddings toward per-source "attractor" points (centroids) and builds masks from similarity to attractors — [Chen et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08930)
- Utterance-level PIT (Kolbæk et al., IEEE/ACM TASLP 2017): frame-level PIT leaves the output-to-speaker permutation free per frame (a speaker "tracing" problem); uPIT fixes one permutation for the whole utterance so each output stream keeps one speaker — [Kolbæk et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06284)
- Scaling PIT: standard PIT is O(C!) in the number of sources C; Dovrat, Nachmani & Wolf (Interspeech 2021) solve the assignment with the Hungarian algorithm (O(C³)) and separate up to 20 speakers ("twice the number tackled by any existing method") — [Dovrat et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.08955)
- Wavesplit (Zeghidour & Grangier, IEEE/ACM TASLP 2021): infers per-time-step speaker vectors, aggregates them by clustering into a sequence-level set of speaker representations, and conditions separation on them — identity as a persistent per-source vector rather than an output slot — [Zeghidour & Grangier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08933)
- Associative embedding (Newell et al., NeurIPS 2017): a single network outputs detections plus a scalar/vector "tag" per detection; a grouping loss pulls tags of the same instance together and pushes different instances apart; used for multi-person pose — [Newell et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05424)
- Discriminative loss (De Brabandere et al. 2017, arXiv): pixel embeddings with a pull term (to own-instance mean, inside margin δv) and a push term (between instance means, beyond margin δd), clustered at test time, count-agnostic — [De Brabandere et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02551). LaneNet (Neven et al., IEEE IV 2018) uses this loss to give each lane its own instance, handling a variable number of lanes — [Neven et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05591)
- Payer et al. (MICCAI 2018; Medical Image Analysis 2019): stacked hourglass with ConvGRUs predicts per-pixel cosine embeddings that are "unique for every instance throughout videos"; embeddings are clustered across consecutive frames (mean shift in the journal version) to produce tracked segmentations; state of the art on six ISBI Cell Tracking Challenge datasets — [Payer et al. (arXiv 1806.02070)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02070)
- Long-form separation (continuous speech separation, CSS): because PIT outputs can swap order between windows, "output permutation consistency between chunks is not guaranteed"; adjacent windows are aligned by mask similarity / MSE / cross-correlation in the overlap; heavy overlap is needed for reliable stitching and is costly; alternatives include Graph-PIT and long-span "group-wise PIT" — [RSAN CSS (arXiv 2110.14838)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14838); [Graph-PIT (arXiv 2107.14446)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.14446.pdf); [group-wise PIT (arXiv 2110.14142)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14142); [dual-path CSS (arXiv 2102.11634)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.11634)

### Inferences
- **Crossings.** A per-bin embedding can keep two fish apart through a crossing only if the network sees both approach and exit within its RF and the embedding encodes a cue that persists through the overlap (harmonic profile, electrode pattern, slope continuity). At the exact crossing frames both fish share bins, and hard per-bin assignment (one source per bin, as in deep clustering's binary masks) is wrong; a per-peak (per-detection) embedding with a soft/multi-label output is better suited.
- **Scaling to 30+.** Clustering-based methods do not fix the count (good), but k-means/mean-shift on many near-equidistant clusters is brittle; the 20-speaker Hungarian-PIT result shows slot-based outputs can scale, but slots must then be stitched across windows (CSS problem), which is what the existing tracker + linker already do.
- **Net effect on failure modes.** Per-bin embeddings mostly address (b) close lines and identity swaps at crossings within a window; they do not by themselves raise faint recall (a) and do not stop false lines (c). Fragmentation (d) across windows remains a stitching problem.
- A practical hybrid (judgement): add a light associative/discriminative embedding head at peak locations and feed it to the existing tracker/linker as an extra similarity term, trained on the dense mixtures (identity labels are free from the per-source ground truth). This is cheap to try and easy to ablate.

### Gaps
- No study applying deep clustering / associative embedding to many (>10) narrowband tonal sources was found.
- No result quantifying identity retention through line crossings for any per-bin embedding method was found.

## 4. Curve/line detection as objects (row-anchor lane detection, DETR-style curve sets, centreline tracing): is the "one frequency per time column per fish ≈ one x per row per lane" analogy productive?

### Takeaway
The analogy holds for the representation (row-anchor = per-time-column classification of each line's frequency) but breaks on scale: lane detectors assume a handful of lanes (fixed slots or few anchors), mostly non-crossing; set-prediction (DETR-style) curve models are the scalable part of the analogy, and a whistle-contour paper already does transformer instance segmentation of whole contours. For 30+ crossing lines over hours, curve-as-object inside a window still needs a cross-window tracker.

### Cited Findings
- UFLD (Qin et al., ECCV 2020): lane detection as row-based selection — for each predefined row anchor, classify which grid cell (x position) the lane occupies, per lane slot, for a fixed maximum number of lanes; lightweight version runs at 300+ FPS — [Qin et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11757). Reformulating segmentation as row-wise classification on a down-sampled map gains speed "at the cost of accuracy" — [Lane2Seq (arXiv 2402.17172)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17172). UFLDv2 (TPAMI 2022) adds hybrid (row and column) anchors and ordinal classification — [UFLDv2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07389)
- Reported CULane F1 (ResNet-34; numbers vary by paper and protocol): UFLD ≈ 70.9 (175 FPS), LaneATT ≈ 76.7 (171 FPS), BézierLaneNet 75.57, UFLDv2 76.00, CLRNet 76.9-79.73 — [Opto-Electronic Engineering table](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJ44b685cc2a46c605/figureandtable); [BézierLaneNet (CVPR 2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02431); [Lane2Seq](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17172)
- LaneATT (CVPR 2021) uses line anchors with attention; CLRNet (CVPR 2022) refines anchor priors across feature levels; PolyLaneNet (ICPR 2020) regresses one polynomial per lane; LSTR (WACV 2021) predicts a set of polynomial lane curves with a transformer and Hungarian set loss; BézierLaneNet (CVPR 2022) predicts Bézier control points — [LaneATT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12035); [CLRNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10350); [PolyLaneNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10924); [LSTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04233); [BézierLaneNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02431)
- CondLaneNet (ICCV 2021): detects lane instances, then uses instance-conditioned convolutions to predict a row-wise location per instance (count-agnostic row-anchor) — [Liu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05003)
- LaneNet (IEEE IV 2018): binary lane segmentation + per-pixel instance embedding (discriminative loss) clustered into a variable number of lanes — [Neven et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05591)
- Iterative centreline tracing: RoadTracer (CVPR 2018) grows a road graph step by step with a CNN deciding the next move, instead of segmentation + post-processing — [Bastani et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03680)
- Contours as instances in bioacoustics: "Automated detection and annotation of toothed whale whistles using transformer-based instance segmentation" (JASA, DOI 10.1121/10.0044187) predicts whole contours directly; on held-out species, locations and hydrophones: 89.99% precision, 80.65% recall — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/automated-detection-and-annotation-of-toothed-whale-whistles-using-transformer-based-instance-segmentation/). OpenWhistle, a large longitudinal dolphin-whistle benchmark, appears at NeurIPS 2026 — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/139275)

### Inferences
- **Where the analogy is productive.** (i) The row-anchor output (for each line instance, a per-time-column distribution over frequency bins plus an "absent" class) is a natural sub-bin-resolution output that could represent two lines 1-3 Hz apart as two instances (failure mode b) where a single salience map cannot. (ii) CondLaneNet-style "find instance start, then trace it row by row" is close to "find a fish, then follow it", i.e. a learned tracker inside a window. (iii) Curve parameterisations (polynomial/Bézier) are poor for EOD lines: they are piecewise flat with sudden rises and hours long; per-column (row-anchor) representations fit better.
- **Where it breaks.** Lane benchmarks have few lanes (typically ≤ 4-6), which is why fixed slots work; 30+ fish need instance queries (DETR/MOTR-like) or embeddings, which brings back the set-matching and stitching issues of §2-3. Lanes rarely cross; fish lines do.
- **Judgement.** A within-window "line instance" head (CondLaneNet/DETR-style, one query per line in a 30-120 s chunk) is the most ambitious joint option and would attack (b), (c) (a query can learn "this is a harmonic of query k") and in-window (d). It is also the costliest to build and train, and it still feeds a cross-window tracker. It is a research-branch option, not a first step.

### Gaps
- No lane/curve detection work with tens of simultaneous, crossing curve instances was found.
- The JASA whistle instance-segmentation paper's architecture details and its handling of crossings were not retrieved (only the abstract-level numbers).

## 5. Cell, particle and animal multi-object tracking: identity from appearance, learned linking, and "tracking-by-detection + learned linking" vs end-to-end when objects look identical

### Takeaway
In biology, the strongest recent systems keep detection/segmentation separate and learn the linking (Trackastra: transformer over a temporal window of detections; ultrack: ILP over many segmentation hypotheses), and they match or beat tuned classical trackers; identity-from-appearance (idtracker.ai, TRex) works when individuals differ visibly and the new idtracker.ai uses contrastive embeddings learned from video structure. This favours "per-frame detection + learned association" over end-to-end tracking for look-alike objects.

### Cited Findings
- Trackastra (Gallusser & Weigert, ECCV 2024): a transformer takes all detected cells in a temporal window and directly predicts pairwise associations; a "blockwise parental softmax" allows one-to-many (division) links but not invalid patterns; with these learned associations "simple greedy linking" suffices; performance on par with or better than highly tuned state-of-the-art cell trackers on bacteria, cell cultures and fluorescent particles — [Trackastra (arXiv 2405.15700)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15700); [ECCV 2024 entry](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2024/gallusser2024eccv-trackastra)
- Ultrack (Bragantini, Lange & Royer, ECCV 2024): builds a hierarchy of candidate segmentations (ultrametric contour maps), then an ILP selects non-overlapping segments that maximise overlap between adjacent frames, jointly yielding segments and tracks; can ensemble segmentations from several models; scales to millions of instances in terabyte 3D+t data; state of the art on nuclei- and membrane-based benchmarks — [Bragantini et al. (arXiv 2308.04526)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04526v2); [ECCV 2024 PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/10114.pdf)
- Payer et al. 2018/2019: end-to-end spatio-temporal embeddings (ConvGRU) for cell tracking, state of the art on six ISBI CTC datasets — [arXiv 1806.02070](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02070)
- TRex (Walter & Couzin, eLife 2021): deep-learning visual identification of up to ~100 unmarked individuals — [TRex (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8096434)
- New idtracker.ai (Torrents, Costa & de Polavieja, eLife 2025): reframes identification as representation learning — contrastive learning on image pairs known from video structure to be the same or different animals; removes the need for video segments where all animals are visible; reported more accurate and up to 700× faster than the previous version, and outperforming TRex on a 33-video benchmark; reviewers flagged that the TRex comparison may be biased (parameter asymmetry, TRex features omitted) — [eLife 107602](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107602); [reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/107602v1/reviews); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.30.657023.full.pdf)
- DanceTrack (look-alike humans): appearance hurts, motion helps (see §2) — [DanceTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3)
- Electric-fish background (from search-result excerpts; which of the two papers carries each statement was not verified in full text): wave-type EOD frequency is individual-specific and stable over hours to days in constant environments, which is why frequency serves as an identity tag; harmonic structure from power spectra has been used for fish identity; classical f0 extraction fails when spectral peaks are broadened by rapid frequency changes — [Henninger et al. (bioRxiv 550814; JEB 2020)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/550814v1.full); [Sci. Rep. 2018, behavioural mapping of Amazonian electric fish](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-24035-5). I found no published deep-learning detector or tracker for EOD frequency traces (search returned only classical methods).

### Inferences
- **Mapping to wavetracker.** The current pipeline (detections → tracker → learned fragment linker) is already the Trackastra/CAMELTrack pattern. The evidence suggests improving it by (i) better detections with calibrated scores, (ii) a learned association model that sees a temporal window of detections (Trackastra-style transformer over peaks in, say, 10-60 s) rather than only frame-to-frame costs, and (iii) multiple-hypothesis detections (ultrack-style) so the association stage can choose between "one merged peak" and "two close peaks" (failure mode b).
- **Appearance cues.** idtracker.ai's contrastive trick (positives = same fragment, negatives = co-occurring fragments, both known without labels) maps directly onto EOD fragments: two simultaneous fragments must be different fish. That gives free training pairs for an identity embedding on real recordings, not only on synthetic mixtures.
- **Look-alike regime.** When objects are visually identical (same-species fish with similar waveforms), the bio-tracking and DanceTrack evidence favour motion/continuity and learned linking over appearance embeddings; electrode-amplitude patterns (which change as fish move) behave like slowly varying "position", closer to a motion cue than to an appearance cue.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head comparison of end-to-end vs tracking-by-detection + learned linking on a look-alike bio dataset was found (Trackastra compares against classical trackers, not MOTR-type models).
- idtracker.ai/TRex identity accuracies at 100 animals were not re-verified in this session.

## 6. Handling chunked, hours-long input (overlapping windows, stitching, causal vs non-causal)

### Takeaway
All relevant systems process long signals in windows: per-window detection needs only overlap-and-crop (no identity), whereas any model that emits identities per window needs an explicit stitching step on the overlap (CSS literature) or a cross-window memory (MOTR-style track queries, dual-path inter-chunk models). Causality is not required offline, and the one ablation found shows bidirectional context is worth a few F1 points over forward-only.

### Cited Findings
- Onsets and Frames trains on 20 s splits and infers on the full file; forward-only LSTMs cost frame F1 78.30 → 75.98/76.30 and note-with-offset 50.22 → 46.36/49.50 — [Hawthorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.11153)
- SAM-whistle runs on 3 s windows and carries whistles across window boundaries in its graph search — [SAM-whistle preprint](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-6550893/v1/539bcbb0f2421a650fd76385.pdf)
- CSS pipeline = segmentation → separation → stitching; permutation consistency across chunks not guaranteed; stitching by overlap-region mask similarity, MSE or cross-correlation; large overlap is needed for reliable stitching and is computationally costly; Graph-PIT and long-span group-wise PIT reduce or avoid stitching — [RSAN (arXiv 2110.14838)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14838); [Graph-PIT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.14446.pdf); [group-wise PIT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14142); [meeting CSS with TF-GridNet (arXiv 2309.16482)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.16482)
- Dual-path RNN alternates intra-chunk and inter-chunk recurrence to model very long sequences — [Luo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06379)
- MeMOTR keeps a long-term memory of track states; SambaMOTR propagates a synchronized state for all tracks — [MeMOTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15700); [SambaMOTR](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01806)
- Mamba scales linearly with sequence length — [Gu & Dao 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)

### Inferences
- **Detector-only output (salience map):** process overlapping chunks (e.g. chunk length L, overlap ≥ the model's temporal RF on each side) and keep only the central part of each chunk; the result is identical to whole-file inference when overlap ≥ RF for convs, and approximately so for RNN/attention. No identity stitching is needed, which is a practical argument for keeping identity out of the network.
- **Identity-emitting output (embeddings, line queries):** per-chunk identities must be matched across chunks via the overlap (Hungarian on overlap similarity, as in CSS); the existing tracker + linker can play this role if per-chunk outputs are converted to fragments.
- **Sizing (my estimate):** a 60 s chunk at 0.16 s is 375 frames; time-axis attention or BiGRU over 375 steps is cheap; the frequency axis (thousands of 0.61 Hz bins across the data-derived range plus harmonics) dominates memory, so frequency should be handled by convs/strided bands, not full attention. Non-causal (bidirectional) models are appropriate for offline analysis; a causal variant only matters for real-time field monitoring.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying how chunk length/overlap affects tracking identity metrics for tonal-line tracking was found.

## 7. Bottom line: keep detection per-frame (with temporal context) and leave identity to tracker + linker, or predict identity cues jointly?

### Takeaway
The evidence favours a staged design: a per-frame salience detector with long temporal context (seconds to a minute) and calibrated scores, feeding a tracker that uses ByteTrack-style low-score recovery and a learned, windowed association model; joint identity heads are worth adding only as an optional extra similarity term, and full end-to-end tracking is not justified by current evidence for look-alike, many-object, hours-long data.

### Cited Findings
- Temporal context inside the detector gives large gains in transcription (frame F1 67.60 → 78.30 with LSTMs, Onsets and Frames) and the deep-salience authors named it the main missing piece — [Hawthorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.11153); [Bittner et al. 2017](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000085.pdf)
- Recovering low-score detections through existing tracks improves IDF1 by 1-10 points across 9 trackers — [ByteTrack](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/315_ECCV_2022_paper.php)
- Where objects look alike, appearance embeddings learned with the detector did not help (FairMOT 39.7 HOTA on DanceTrack vs 59.3 on MOT17) or hurt (DeepSORT below SORT), while motion modelling helped — [DanceTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14690v3)
- End-to-end query trackers lead DanceTrack (MOTRv2 69.9, MOTIP 70.0, SambaMOTR 69.0 HOTA) but MOTRv2 relies on an external detector, independent re-runs found much lower and variable numbers for SambaMOTR, and a tracking-by-detection method with learned association (CAMELTrack, 69.3) is competitive — [MOTRv2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09791); [MOTIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16848); [SambaMOTR](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01806); [arXiv 2609.22291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22291); [CAMELTrack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01257)
- In cell tracking, a learned windowed association on top of detections (Trackastra) matches or beats tuned trackers; multi-hypothesis segmentation + global optimisation (ultrack) scales to millions of instances — [Trackastra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15700); [ultrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04526v2)
- In the closest domain (whistles), working systems are learned TF detectors + rule-based graph trackers (Silbido Profundo, SAM-whistle), and their authors identify the tracker's handling of overlaps/close harmonics as the open problem — [SAM-whistle preprint](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-6550893/v1/539bcbb0f2421a650fd76385.pdf); [Silbido profundo](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftstandrewserep:oai:research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk:10023%2F26799)

### Inferences
Judgement, by failure mode (ranked by evidence strength):
- **(a) Faint misses:** temporal context in the detector (CRNN/BiGRU or dilated convs along time, RF of tens of seconds) + ByteTrack-style two-threshold association in the tracker. Strongest evidence; lowest cost.
- **(c) False lines:** a long-RF detector trained on mixtures where harmonics and strays are negatives (it can see harmonic co-modulation); plus "new identities need high scores" in the tracker. Moderate evidence (by analogy; no direct study).
- **(b) Lines within 3 Hz:** mainly an output-representation problem (target width, sub-bin output, multi-instance output), not a temporal-context problem. Options in order of cost: narrower targets + offset regression per peak; multiple-hypothesis peaks for the tracker to choose from (ultrack idea); per-peak embedding; within-window line queries (CondLaneNet/DETR-style). Weak direct evidence for any of them in this domain.
- **(d) Fragmentation:** learned windowed association (Trackastra-style over detections, extending the current fragment linker), optionally with a contrastive per-fragment embedding trained idtracker.ai-style from "co-occurring = different" pairs. Moderate evidence from cell/animal tracking.
- **Identity in the network** (embeddings, queries) has the most value inside a chunk for crossings and close lines, but forces cross-chunk stitching; keeping the network's output identity-free keeps chunked inference trivial and keeps the existing tracker/linker/GUI pipeline usable. Recommended order (judgement): (1) temporal-context detector, (2) ByteTrack-style association, (3) learned windowed linker, (4) optional embedding head, ablated on IDF1; end-to-end trackers only if (1)-(4) plateau.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares per-frame detection + tracker against joint detection-identity models for spectrogram line tracking; every recommendation above for (b) is transferred from other domains.
- How much each failure mode contributes to the IDF1 gap (0.61-0.65 → ?) is a property of the user's benchmark and must be measured there (e.g. oracle-detection and oracle-association runs, as DanceTrack did with GT boxes).
