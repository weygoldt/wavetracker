# Learned and strong classical methods for extracting many frequency contours/lines from spectrograms (bioacoustics, passive sonar, GW line searches, TF analysis) — transfer to EOD fundamentals

Scope note: covers odontocete whistle extraction (the most direct precedent), other taxa, passive sonar LOFAR line detection/tracking, continuous-gravitational-wave (CW) line searches, multicomponent instantaneous-frequency (IF) estimation, power-grid/machinery harmonic tracking. Music multi-pitch "deep salience" (Bittner et al.) and speech pitch are not covered here; other researchers cover them. "Preprint" = not peer reviewed at the time of reading. Numbers are as reported by the cited source. My own judgement is only in the "Inferences" subsections.

## 1. Odontocete whistle contour extraction: networks, synthetic data, post-processing, crossings, reported performance

### Takeaway
The Roch/Li group (SDSU) built the closest existing analogue of the planned EOD design: a small fully convolutional net predicts a per-time-frequency-bin "whistle confidence map", and an unchanged classical graph-search tracker (silbido) links its peaks. On a hard DCLDE-2011 subset, swapping heuristic peak picking for the learned map raised F1 from 0.634 to 0.884. Training on synthetic whistles blended into real noise recovered most of that gain (F1 0.792) with no or little annotation. Later work added GAN augmentation (small gains), pseudo-label training with recall-/precision-aware losses (F1 86–87%, matching fully supervised), and an end-to-end transformer instance-segmentation model. Probabilistic multi-target trackers (GM-PHD, SMC-PHD) reach high recall but fragment more and are less precise.

### Cited Findings
**Li et al. 2020, "Learning Deep Models from Synthetic Data for Extracting Dolphin Whistle Contours" (IJCNN 2020, invited paper; arXiv 2005.08894). The method is later called "Deep Whistle".**
- Input: log-magnitude spectrograms with 8 ms Hamming frames (125 Hz bins) and a 2 ms hop, clamped to 0–120 dB and normalised to [0, 1], limited to 5–50 kHz (361 bins), "the range of most delphinid whistles and their harmonics". The spectrogram is tiled into 100 ms × 6.25 kHz (50 × 50 bin) patches — [Li et al. 2020, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Network: 10 conv layers with 32 channels each. The first and last layers are 5×5; the 8 between them form 4 residual blocks of 3×3 convs, with BatchNorm and PReLU, all stride 1 and **no pooling**, "which makes predictions on individual time-frequency nodes". The output is a confidence map the same size as the input. Loss is Charbonnier (a smooth L2), with Adam, 600k iterations, and about 8 h of training on a GTX 1080Ti — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Reported receptive field: 46 ms × 2.875 kHz [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894). The same network is described as 56 ms × 3.125 kHz in [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf) (the two sources disagree).
- Labels: binary contour masks (1 in each bin on a whistle) blurred with a random Gaussian filter — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Synthesis: primitive shapes are injected into whistle-absent patches of real ocean recordings as X' = X + α(Y∗G), with α ~ U[0.03, 0.23], "signal strengths varying over 24 dB". The SNR therefore depends on the real background. Shape sources: (i) analyst whistle contours from DCLDE 2011 (contours only, no whistle audio); (ii) human edge annotations from natural images (BSDS500); (iii) Canny edges of BSDS500 images, which need no human annotation at all — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- "Recall-guided" adaptive synthesis: each epoch, positive shapes are resampled in proportion to (1 − recall) on validation patches, so hard shapes are re-synthesised in new backgrounds — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Data: 20 DCLDE 2011 sequences (14 train / 6 test), 7,161 training whistles and 911 test whistles (354 bottlenose, 557 common dolphin). Common dolphins were "more challenging ... with many simultaneous whistles, resulting in numerous whistle crossings" — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Post-processing: the confidence map is thresholded at τ = 0.5 and passed to the silbido graph search (Roch et al. 2011). At each step, peaks start new graphs or extend existing ones if they lie along a low-order polynomial prediction. Gaps up to a user-set interval are bridged. **A peak joining two graphs merges them, so internal nodes with several inputs and outputs are "potential whistle crossing points", and forward- and backward-predicting polynomial fits decide which branches belong together** — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Evaluation criterion: a detection matches if its mean frequency deviation is < 350 Hz. Only analyst whistles ≥ 150 ms long and with SNR ≥ 10 dB over at least 1/3 of their duration count; detections matching non-qualifying whistles are discarded — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Results (Table I, 911 test whistles): graph search alone P 0.634 / R 0.633 / F1 0.634. Fully supervised on real annotations (WGT): 0.956 / 0.822 / 0.884. Synthetic only: EdgeGT 0.913 / 0.638 / 0.751; EdgeCanny (no human labels) 0.900 / 0.603 / 0.722; µWGT (6.25 % of the real whistle shapes, synthesised) 0.900 / 0.673 / 0.770; µWGT + recall-guided 0.924 / 0.693 / 0.792 — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Data quantity: performance rose with training data, and the largest jump came as the fraction approached 25 % of the 148,224 patches — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Speed: 10 ms network plus 300 ms graph search per 1 s of audio, about 3× faster than real time — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)

**Conant et al. 2022, "Silbido profundo" (JASA 152(6):3800–3808, doi 10.1121/10.0016631).** Deep Whistle packaged into the MATLAB silbido tool via ONNX. Code: github.com/MarineBioAcousticsRC/silbido.
- Results on 1,025 DCLDE-2011 whistles (P / R / F1 / coverage): silbido graph search 63.4 / 63.3 / 63.4 / 79.5; SMC-PHD (Gruden & White 2020) 70.5 / 92.6 / 80.1 / 70.5; SMC-PHD (detections > 150 ms) 96.1 / 61.4 / 74.9; Deep Whistle (Li 2020) 95.6 / 82.2 / 88.4 / 86.6; silbido profundo 96.3 / 79.7 / 87.2 / 85.2. The best F1 came at confidence 0.5, "reasonably stable" around it — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf)
- Ran on 487 h of weakly labelled NOAA towed-array data at 5.5× faster than real time on one RTX 2080 Ti. It found valid whistles outside the analyst labels — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf)
- **False-positive categories: "spectral lines" (narrowband recording artefacts), rhythmic signals and burst pulses. "The graph search algorithm is designed to bridge small gaps ...; when there are series of short duration narrow-band energy, these signals can be incorrectly recognized as whistles."** The authors attribute the gain to the CNN considering "multiple peaks in context" rather than per-frame peak picking — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf)
- Ran unretrained on a songbird (speckled warbler) and on mysticete moans with "similar results", after adjusting only the frame and band parameters — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf)

**Li et al. 2023, "Learning Stage-wise GANs for Whistle Extraction in Time-Frequency Spectrograms" (arXiv 2304.02714, preprint; code announced at github.com/Paul-LiPu/CompositeGAN_WhistleAugment).**
- Three generation stages: a WGAN for background noise, a WGAN for contour shapes, and a CycleGAN with a U-Net generator that renders the whistle signal onto the noise. Patches are 64 × 64 (128 ms × 8 kHz), with 115,968 positive patches. The extraction network is identical to Li 2020 — [Li et al. 2023 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02714)
- Mean F1 (species-averaged) without → with GAN augmentation, by number N of real positive patches: N = 1,000: 79.74 → 80.73; 2,500: 83.04 → 84.73; 10,000: 84.70 → 85.21; all: 87.42 → 87.88. The maximum gain is 1.69 F1 points — [Li et al. 2023 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02714)
- Comparison (F1 / P / R): Roch 2011 graph search 75.95 / 81.1 / 72.3; Gruden & White 2020 SMC-PHD 83.40 / 76.55 / 92.45 (≥ 150 ms: 74.38 / 95.85 / 60.88); Li 2020 87.42 / 89.27 / 86.04; Li 2020 + GAN 87.88 / 89.60 / 86.63. They also state: "Multiple crossing whistles can be represented by a single graph" — [Li et al. 2023 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02714)

**Li et al. 2023, "Using deep learning to track time × frequency whistle contours of toothed whales without human-annotated training data" (JASA 154(1):502–517, doi 10.1121/10.0020274).**
- Pseudo-labels are generated by silbido graph search, or by SMC-PHD, on unlabelled data and drawn as 2-px polylines — [Li et al. 2023 JASA (NOAA repository)](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889)
- Key observation: graph-search pseudo-labels have "high bin-wise precision but low bin-wise recall" (many missed whistles). Training naively on them (L_base) gives F1 74.14 (P 94.15, R 61.53), *worse* than the graph search that produced the labels (75.95) — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)
- Fix: L_recall = L_base + k(1 − R_soft)^c · ||(ŷ − y)·y||. This adds a penalty on bins pseudo-labelled positive but predicted low, modulated focal-style by the soft recall. A mirror loss, L_prec, uses the soft precision for label sets dominated by false positives. Results: L_recall on graph-search labels gives F1 86.31; on SMC-PHD labels (≥ 150 ms) 87.20. Fully supervised on 12,539 expert whistles: 87.47 — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)
- Silbido metrics include deviation, coverage and **fragmentation (detected segments per ground-truth whistle)**. Fragmentation: supervised CNN 1.13, graph search 1.23, SMC-PHD 1.80, L_recall models 1.15–1.18 — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)
- SMC-PHD tracks identities via labelled particles, with a birth model built from measurements and training priors. It requires a "modest amount" of annotated data (3 min, 185 whistles) for its RBF motion model — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)

**Gruden & White (Southampton) PHD filters.**
- GM-PHD (JASA 2016): spectral peaks are detected first, then a GM-PHD filter tracks several whistle contours simultaneously. On >9,000 whistles of 6 species: precision 85 %, recall 71.8 %, mean deviation 104 Hz (about one frequency bin). Described as suitable for real time — [Southampton eprints 400139](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/400139)
- SMC-PHD (JASA 148(5):3014, 2020, doi 10.1121/10.0002257): a particle PHD adapted for frequency tracking, motivated by older methods that "fragment contours or detect only partial whistles" — [Southampton eprints 444504](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/444504)

**Roch et al. 2011, "Automated extraction of odontocete whistle contours" (JASA 130(4):2212–2223).** Adaptive polynomial prediction connects peaks into graphs; graphs that cross are merged, and contours are extracted "using information from both sides of crossings". The evaluation set was about 1 h of hand-annotated data from 5 species — [PubMed 21973376](https://www.pubmed.gov/21973376/)

**Zhang, Liu, Alksne & Roch, "Automated detection and annotation of toothed-whale whistles using transformer-based instance segmentation" (JASA, doi 10.1121/10.0044187; year not shown on the index page I read).** An end-to-end transformer predicts whole whistle contours as instances, with no separate peak or linking stage. Labels were refined iteratively with a human in the loop. Testing used held-out species, locations and hydrophones. Results: all whistles P 89.99 % / R 80.65 %; whistles > 150 ms P 85.81 % / R 88.44 % — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/automated-detection-and-annotation-of-toothed-whale-whistles-using-transformer-based-instance-segmentation/)

**DYOC (Research Square preprint):** YOLOv8m detection plus ResNet18 identification, used to semi-automatically annotate 8,730 common-dolphin contours from 808 min of audio, about 6× faster than manual — [CiteDrive/Research Square summary](https://researchsquare.com/article/rs-5234650/v1)

### Inferences
- The Li/Conant result is direct precedent for the wavetracker plan: keep the tracker and replace the peak detector with a learned salience map. In that setting most of the gain came from contextual peak selection, not from a new tracker. Precision rose sharply (0.63 → 0.96) along with recall, which is exactly the failure mode (c) false-line problem.
- Deep Whistle's receptive field is only about 25 × 25 bins (my arithmetic: one 5×5, eight 3×3 and one 5×5 layer gives 1 + 4 + 16 + 4 = 25 bins). At EOD resolution (0.61 Hz × 0.16 s) that is about 15 Hz × 4 s: enough for local continuity, but blind to harmonics hundreds of Hz away. Harmonic evidence must come through the input representation (e.g., harmonically stacked channels) or a much larger frequency receptive field.
- The pseudo-label losses (L_recall / L_prec) target exactly the EOD truth caveat "sources contain real lines the truth does not know about". When labels miss real lines (incomplete negatives), the background term should be down-weighted, which corresponds to L_recall's logic. When truth frames are attenuated below the noise, a precision-type relaxation or masking of the positive term is the analogue.
- Conant's false-positive list (narrowband "spectral lines", gap bridging that chains short narrowband energy into long contours) is the bioacoustic version of mains-hum combs and of the tracker turning stray peaks into long false identities.
- The same methods score differently across papers (e.g., SMC-PHD F1 80.1 in Conant 2022 vs 83.40 in Li 2023 on DCLDE subsets; Li 2020 WGT 0.884 vs 87.42 species-averaged). Evaluation protocol choices (duration and SNR filters, averaging) move F1 by several points, so the EOD benchmark should fix one protocol and report fragmentation and coverage alongside P/R.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain full texts for Gruden & White 2016/2020 (403 errors). I could not verify how their filters behave at exact crossings or with close parallel whistles, beyond "labelled particles".
- The transformer instance-segmentation paper (doi 10.1121/10.0044187): year, architecture details (e.g., query-based mask decoder), crossing handling and code availability were not visible.
- Not reviewed: Kershenbaum & Roch 2013 (image-processing paradigm), Mallawaarachchi et al. (whistle fundamental extraction), PAMGuard whistle-and-moan detector internals.
- None of these papers reports precision/recall as a function of SNR. They report a single operating point with an SNR ≥ 10 dB inclusion filter, so performance on near-noise-floor contours (the EOD failure mode (a)) is not documented.

## 2. Other taxa: bats, birds, frogs/insects, rodents, whale song, fish — CNN/U-Net segmentation and contour tracing

### Takeaway
Outside odontocetes, learned spectrogram methods mostly do event detection (boxes) or binary segmentation masks, not contour tracing with identities. The most relevant pieces are mask-based instance separation of overlapping bat calls (count sources → boxes → per-source masks) and U-Net or autoencoder segmentation of rodent USVs and fin-whale calls. Explicit harmonic or identity modelling is rare, and I found no learned frog, insect or fish-sound contour tracers.

### Cited Findings
- Birds: a U-Net maps bird-vocalisation spectrograms to binary masks of call blobs, suppressing other sounds, trained with spectrogram–mask pairs including recordings without birds (arXiv 1609.08408) — [arXiv 1609.08408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.08408)
- Fin whales: encoder–decoder semantic segmentation of calls in ocean-bottom-seismometer recordings, restricted to a 12–35 Hz band, with post-processing that extracts individual calls (arXiv 2609.13281, 2026 preprint) — [arXiv 2609.13281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13281)
- Humpback song: Ou et al. 2013 (JASA) extracted unit contours by searching for grey-level discontinuities in spectrogram images, extracting 951 units in 12 types with 0–4 % false alarms in noise from small boats and snapping shrimp. Pines 2018 (JASA abstract) tracked the two most energetic harmonics as tone pairs — [OUCI record, Ou et al.](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/45ygvG34); [bio.acousti.ca record](https://bio.acousti.ca/node/53295)
- Bats, overlapping calls: "Separation of overlapping sources in bioacoustic mixtures" (JASA 147(3):1688–1696, 2020, doi 10.1121/10.0000932) first detects the number of sources and their bounding boxes, then segments a mask per sound. It parses recordings with hundreds of overlapping bat echolocation signals, motivated by deep clustering failing with an unknown number of sources — [BVS record mdl-32237826](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-32237826)
- Bats: a bidirectional LSTM separated overlapping echolocation and communication calls of 6 species (Integrative Zoology 2022; bioRxiv 2019). Extracted call parameters did not differ significantly from the originals, and clustering of separated calls reached 93.8 % accuracy — [bioRxiv 2019.12.15.876656](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.15.876656v1)
- Rodent USVs: DeepSqueak (Neuropsychopharmacology 2019) uses Faster R-CNN boxes; VocalMat (eLife 2021) uses classical vision and ML and treats a harmonic as a separate continuous component overlapping the main one; SqueakOut (bioRxiv 2024 preprint) is a fully convolutional autoencoder segmenter with a 12,954-spectrogram dataset; HybridMouse (Frontiers 2021) is a CRNN detector — [VocalMat eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59161); [SqueakOut bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.19.590368.full.pdf); [HybridMouse](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.810590/full)
- A 2023 comparison of U-Net, autoencoder and RNN for mouse-USV segmentation found them better than existing tools — [PMC10336146](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10336146/)
- A review notes segmentation is hard because of overlap, variable duration and noise; PCEN preprocessing helps but does not solve these problems — [arXiv 2603.14917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14917)

### Inferences
- The bat "count → box → mask per source" scheme is the only bioacoustic precedent I found for outputting per-source instances when many overlap. For EOD it suggests an instance or query head (one mask or track per fish within a time window) as an alternative to pure salience peaks. Long-range identity would still need the tracker.
- Treating harmonics as separate overlapping components (VocalMat) is the default in this literature. It is the opposite of what EOD needs, where harmonics should be pooled into the fundamental's evidence.

### Gaps
- No learned frog, insect or fish-sound contour tracers were found. Frog-chorus and insect work seemed to be detection or classification only, but I did not run dedicated searches for each taxon.
- Bat FM-sweep contour extraction with deep learning (beyond separation) was not found.

## 3. Passive sonar LOFAR, GW continuous-wave line searches, multicomponent IF (radar/TF), power-grid and machinery tracking

### Takeaway
Classical low-SNR line tracking is dominated by HMM/Viterbi (dynamic programming) over a frequency-state lattice with ±1-bin transitions (sonar since Streit & Barrett 1990, Paris & Jauffret 2003; GW: SOAP, Suvorova et al.). These are single-track per band or produce "mixed" tracks that need separating when several lines are present. Learned LOFAR line detectors (DeepLofargram, SwDCNN, DRNet, LSE-Net) are trained and tested almost entirely on simulated random-walk sinusoids in synthetic noise and report −22 to −26 dB on that synthetic task. In the 2022–23 G2Net CW competition with real detector noise, GPU template and track statistics won; a CNN ensemble placed 3rd and a U-Net 9th. The ML teams handled real noise by injecting line-like artefacts into training. New TF work resolves crossings by lifting into frequency-rate (a query-based transformer) or by explicitly modelling interference.

### Cited Findings
**Passive sonar, classical**
- Paris & Jauffret 2003, "Frequency line tracking using HMM-based schemes" (IEEE TAES 39(2):439–449): a line is a first-order Markov random walk on the lofargram, decoded by Viterbi or forward–backward. In Monte Carlo tests, forward–backward with a nonparametric approach performed best, with no loss from unknown SNR — [search summary of TAES 2003](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/frequency-line-tracking-using-hmm-based-schemes/812396484480729089-2511)
- Lineage: Streit & Barrett 1990's HMM tracker handles a single line; Van & Alinat extended it to multiple lines; Paris & Jauffret built multi-line Viterbi and forward–backward trackers; Pulford & Tyson proposed a 1-D HMM — [Sensors 2019, 19(22):4866](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/22/4866)
- The multi-target/multi-line HMM extension uses a two-level scheme to cut computation and "yields a 'mixed' track that may need separating into individual tracks" — [search summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/multiple-target-tracking-and-multiple-frequency-line-tracking-using-hidden-markov-models/812459752159182849-3812)
- Sensors 2019 multi-line tracker: segments the lofargram into sub-lofargrams, runs an HMM in each and merges overlaps. The authors say HMMs perform better at low SNR and work "at very low detection thresholds or even on unthresholded data"; older lofargram trackers used alpha-beta or Kalman filters with nearest-neighbour association — [Sensors 2019 PMC6891505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6891505/)
- Multi-target trackers for narrowband sonar: an MHT approach by Brinkmann & Hurka (2010) [GI Digital Library](https://dl.gi.de/items/0ee2c2af-88c2-4d34-a931-d64ed1d6124e/full); random-finite-set/PHD tracking on real passive sonar, including a "tone tracking" chapter (Wood, Oxford DPhil 2011) [ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:09211ed9-7cc1-4401-9ae9-a1ebc2a1f782)
- Lampert & O'Keefe (York): a survey of spectrogram track detection (Applied Acoustics 2010) names low SNR as the key challenge. In Lampert's thesis no evaluated method performed adequately at real-life SNRs; Hough and Laplacian line detectors degrade at low SNR; the proposed active-contour tracker detects tracks down to about 0.5 dB (vertical), 3 dB (oblique) and 2 dB (sinusoidal) — [Lampert 2010 thesis (BMVA)](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/theses/2010/2010-lampert.pdf); [survey PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/46265/1/Lampert10Survey.pdf)
- Lampert's multi-scale piecewise-linear detector: plain thresholding works for spectrograms ≥ 3 dB SNR, which was 70 % of their test database — [academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/684440/A_Multi_scale_Piecewise_Linear_Feature_Detector_for_Spectrogram_Tracks)

**Passive sonar, learned**
- DeepLofargram (Han, Li, Liu, Ma; arXiv 1912.00605, 2019 preprint; arXiv lists related DOI 10.1121/10.0002172): AlexNet/VGG binary H0/H1 classifier plus a "recovery" net that produces a line saliency map by guided backpropagation. It uses a multi-task loss with class-balanced cross-entropy (about 90 % of pixels are non-line) — [arXiv 1912.00605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00605)
- DeepLofargram data: entirely simulated with Paris's model, a sinusoid with random-walk frequency in white Gaussian noise, with 1,000 noise-only and 1,000 signal lofargrams (500 × 256) per SNR. Training was a curriculum: pretrain at −20 and −23 dB, then fine-tune at −24 to −26 dB. Claimed boundary: −24 dB on average, −26 dB in some cases. Validated only "on representative simulation datasets" — [arXiv 1912.00605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00605)
- SwDCNN (Acta Acustica 48(4), 2023): sliding-window multi-scale ConvNeXt for lofargram lines under low SNR, interruptions, bending drift and "dense distributions". Its minimum detectable SNR is reported > 5 dB lower than traditional methods and > 2 dB lower than other deep nets — [JAC 2023](https://www.jac.ac.cn/cn/article/Y2023/I4/699)
- AINP + LR-DRNet (Remote Sensing 15:3268, 2023): impulsive-noise suppression followed by a detection-and-reconstruction net, detecting weak lines at mixed SNR down to −26 dB in simulation — [MDPI Remote Sensing 15(13):3268](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3268)
- LSE-Net: encoder–decoder with adapters and reverse cross-attention; outperforms DeepLofargram and TransUNet at −16 to −22 dB (year not shown). This comes from a search-engine summary only: I could not confirm which result page belongs to LSE-Net, so the link is a best guess — [search result](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/69c7724e8bbfbc51511e2ac1)
- U-Net++ LOFARgram preprocessing trained on data from a forward model of ship-noise LOFARgrams (Frontiers in Marine Science 2025, doi 10.3389/fmars.2025.1528111) — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1528111/full)

**Gravitational-wave continuous-wave (CW) line searches**
- SOAP (Bayley, Messenger & Woan, arXiv 1903.12614): Viterbi over 1800 s SFTs, running-median normalised and summed over one day, with transitions of −1, 0 or +1 bin per step. Each sub-band is 0.1 Hz wide (0.05 Hz overlap) and SOAP finds "the most probable track of a single variable-frequency signal" per sub-band; alternative tracks are lost. A "line-aware" statistic penalises signals with unequal power across detectors, as instrumental lines would have. Sensitivity at 1 % false alarm and 95 % efficiency: SNR about 60 in Gaussian noise; about 74 (depth about 13 Hz^-1/2) on S6 mock data. Timing: 0.56 s per band with one detector, 25.7 s with the line-aware lookup — [ar5iv 1903.12614](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.12614)
- SOAP + CNN (Bayley et al., PRD 102, 083024, 2020): a CNN looks at corresponding bands in each detector and classifies each band as signal, instrumental line or noise. A fully automated search reaches 95 % efficiency at SNR 110 at 1 % false alarm, depth 10 Hz^-1/2 — [arXiv 2007.08207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08207)
- Viterbi-HMM for wandering-frequency CWs (Suvorova et al. 2016): Viterbi tracking combined with F-statistic matched filters detected 41 of 50 synthetic Sco X-1 MDC signals, at about 10^3 CPU-hours per broadband search. Paper III (PRD 104, 042003, 2021) detects all available synthetic MDC signals — [arXiv 1606.02412](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02412v1); [arXiv 2107.12822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12822)
- CNNs for CW: Dreissigacker et al. 2019 (residual CNN) reached about 88 % detection probability, about 5 % sensitivity-depth loss vs coherent matched filtering, in the easiest case (T = 10^5 s, 20 Hz) [arXiv 1904.13291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.13291). Dreissigacker & Prix 2020 extended this to two detectors and realistic noise [arXiv 2005.04140](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04140). Joshi & Prix 2024 (PRD 110, 124071) used large-kernel CNNs over 20–1000 Hz on 10 days of H1/L1 data and were more sensitive than the earlier DNN in all benchmark cases [arXiv 2408.07070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07070v2)
- G2Net CW Kaggle (Oct 2022–Jan 2023; Tenorio et al. 2025, MLST; arXiv 2509.06445): 936 teams; SFT spectrograms from two detectors over 4 months, 0.2 Hz bands (360 bins); noise either simulated Gaussian or real O3 (shifted and lightly noised to hide its origin); duty cycle about 0.78 — [arXiv 2509.06445](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06445v2)
- G2Net results: 1st and 2nd place were GPU track or template statistics (about 3.5×10^8 templates for 1st); 4th was dynamic programming "similar to a Viterbi search"; ML placed 3rd (an ensemble trained on PyFstat data simulated on the fly), 8th (CNN + F-statistic, with separate nets for Gaussian and real noise), 9th (U-Net) and 10th (CNN). "ML teams injected line-like features into their training data"; track-based teams masked non-Gaussian bands. The U-Net showed "negligible degradation on real data" in the GCT range, while the DP track-finder's false-positive rate worsened about 10× on real noise. All top-10 beat SOAP's AUC — [arXiv 2509.06445](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06445v2)

**Multicomponent IF estimation / radar-style TF analysis**
- TFFR-Net (Pan, Li, Zhang, Wang; NeurIPS 2026 poster): lifts the signal to a 3-D time–frequency–frequency-rate space. A query-based masked transformer predicts per-ridge masks and IF-rate maps, with IF-rate feedback into the queries. The "crossing rate" falls from 78.6 % in 2-D to 0.9 % in 3-D in Monte Carlo tests. Synthetic benchmark of up to 6 chirps at 0–25 dB: IF MAE ≥ 5.8 % and Dice loss ≥ 5.4 % better than competitors. Claimed to generalise to real bat echolocation, power-system and earthquake signals — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149622)
- Sensors 2025 (25(7):2030): a U-Net produces a high-concentration TF representation, and a second network counts components and separates crossing IFs using their different linear chirp rates — [search summary of MDPI Sensors](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/7/2030)
- Polisano, Meignen, Laurent & Leterme 2025 (arXiv 2503.14990, preprint): splits the spectrogram into a mode part and an interference part (variational method vs supervised U-Net) and uses the interference map to adapt the window length locally, improving ridge detection for close modes. Meignen & Polisano 2025 (non-learned): local window optimisation plus ridge detection and spline fitting beat synchrosqueezing and chirplet methods for close or crossing modes — [arXiv 2503.14990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14990); [Meignen & Polisano 2025](https://polisano.pages.math.cnrs.fr/publication/meignen-novel-2025/)
- Adaptive Multi-Trace Carving (Zhu, Chen, Wong & Wu; arXiv 2005.06686, related DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2020.3030182): iterative dynamic programming finds the dominant trace, its energy is compensated, and the next trace is found. It targets very low SNR and is tested on synthetic and real power-signature and physiological data — [arXiv 2005.06686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06686)

**Power grid (ENF) and machinery**
- ENF: harmonic robust filtering plus a graph-based maximum-weight-clique choice of which harmonics to combine, then (weighted) maximum likelihood (Hua et al., arXiv 2011.03414). Another approach weights harmonics by SNR after interpolating to a common resolution — [arXiv 2011.03414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03414v1); [Waterloo report](https://sigport.org/sites/default/files/UWEngineer.pdf)
- Machinery: the multi-order probabilistic approach (MOPA) defines a per-harmonic PDF of shaft speed and combines them, preferring harmonics with more energy and less entropy. A Safran Bayesian HMM decodes instantaneous angular speed by Viterbi — [PHM Society paper](https://www.papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phmap/article/view/3718); [ASME Turbo Expo session](https://asme-turboexpo.secure-platform.com/a/solicitations/243/sessiongallery/19012/application/153005)

### Inferences
- The lofargram deep-learning SNRs (−24 to −26 dB) are per-sample time-domain SNRs on long-integration simulated lofargrams. They are not comparable to the "< 6 dB" per-bin spectrogram SNR of wavetracker's missed frames, and they say nothing about real-noise robustness.
- The GW line-aware statistic and Bayley's three-class CNN (signal / instrumental line / noise, using per-detector inputs) map onto EOD failure mode (c). A fish has a spatial amplitude pattern across electrodes, while mains hum is roughly common-mode, and a harmonic shares its fundamental's electrode pattern. A per-electrode input with an explicit "line/harmonic" negative class is the analogue.
- G2Net shows that when the signal model is simple and exact, well-engineered DP or template statistics still beat learned detectors at the lowest SNRs. Learned models were most valuable for robustness to real non-Gaussian noise, given realistic line-like training artefacts. A Viterbi/DP-on-salience back end may therefore still add sensitivity on top of a learned map.
- Single-track-per-band Viterbi (SOAP) and "mixed track" multi-line HMMs fail where EOD needs to succeed (30 fish a few Hz apart). Iterative "carving" (AMTC) or a multi-target tracker on learned salience are the classical multi-line options.

### Gaps
- No full texts were read for SwDCNN, DRNet, LSE-Net or Brinkmann & Hurka (MHT), so I could not check their multi-line and crossing handling or whether they were tested on real data.
- Radar micro-Doppler learned curve extraction was not specifically found; TFFR-Net is the closest.
- The SOAP journal venue (I believe PRD 2019) was not confirmed on the page read.

## 4. Which methods exploit harmonic structure, temporal continuity, or both?

### Takeaway
Most contour and line extractors use only temporal continuity (graph search, PHD filters, HMM/Viterbi, CNN/U-Net maps with local receptive fields). Harmonic structure is used explicitly mainly in passive-sonar harmonic-family association, machinery multi-order speed tracking, ENF harmonic combination, and Lampert's spectrogram track detector, where a harmonic prior doubled the detection rate. Methods that do both are classical (MOPA-style multi-harmonic plus Viterbi; sonar harmonic-family detection on tracked lines). I found no learned line tracker in these fields that models a fundamental and its harmonics as one object.

### Cited Findings
- Lampert's thesis: "Adding prior knowledge of the harmonic process reportedly increases the detection rate by a factor of two" — [Lampert 2010 thesis (BMVA)](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/theses/2010/2010-lampert.pdf)
- Passive sonar: "a key problem ... is to group the frequency tracks into their corresponding harmonic sets"; Terry & Mason proposed template matching for harmonic-set association on single-sensor data — [Terry & Mason, IOA proceedings](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/jl_terry_jc_mason_template_matching_and_data_fitting_in_passive_sonar_harmonic_detection.pdf)
- A patent describes deciding which lines are harmonics of a fundamental and then integrating the fundamental and harmonics to raise detection sensitivity. Another uses a harmonic-group detector with a "harmonic evidence factor" across sonobuoys — [US 5034931](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5034931); [US 9229087](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9229087)
- A joint MUSIC-based method estimates the number of harmonic sources, their orders and fundamentals for ship noise — [Sensors & Transducers P_SI_433](https://sensorsportal.com/HTML/ST_JOURNAL/PDF_Files/P_SI_433.pdf)
- Machinery MOPA combines per-harmonic speed PDFs (multi-order) and is often paired with Viterbi decoding of speed (temporal) — [PHM Society](https://www.papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phmap/article/view/3718); [ASME Turbo Expo](https://asme-turboexpo.secure-platform.com/a/solicitations/243/sessiongallery/19012/application/153005)
- ENF: combining several mains harmonics with robust weighting or clique selection (because some harmonics are corrupted) improves robustness — [arXiv 2011.03414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03414v1)
- Odontocete work: Li 2020 and Li 2023 include 5–50 kHz to cover "whistles and their harmonics" but describe no harmonic grouping. VocalMat treats a harmonic as a separate overlapping component — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894); [VocalMat](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59161)
- GW SOAP tracks a single narrowband track per sub-band; there is no harmonic model — [ar5iv 1903.12614](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.12614)

### Inferences
- For EOD, harmonics are the most informative extra evidence for a faint fundamental, and the main source of false lines (failure mode (c): harmonics of low-frequency fish). Lampert's two-fold gain and the sonar/ENF/machinery practice of integrating over harmonics support giving the network harmonic-aligned input. Examples: channels holding the spectrogram sampled at k·f for k = 1..K on the linear axis (an HCQT analogue), or harmonic-sum features. The network would then be trained to output salience only at fundamentals.
- Robust harmonic weighting matters (ENF clique selection, MOPA entropy weighting): some harmonics of a fish will be masked by other fish or by mains combs. The network or feature design should tolerate missing harmonics rather than require all of them.

### Gaps
- No learned multi-line detector outside music/speech with explicit fundamental-plus-harmonics output was found. If one exists, it was not surfaced by these searches.

## 5. Synthetic-data training: how realistic did it need to be, and how was the sim-to-real gap measured or closed?

### Takeaway
Bioacoustics measured the gap directly. Synthetic contours blended into *real* background noise got close to real-data training (F1 0.792 vs 0.884), and even shape priors from natural-image edges beat the classical baseline (0.722–0.751 vs 0.634). Realistic shapes helped more than realistic rendering, and GAN rendering added only about 0.5–1.7 F1 points. Pseudo-labels from classical trackers plus a recall-aware loss matched full supervision. Sonar line-detection nets are mostly validated only in simulation. GW CW (G2Net) closed the gap with real detector noise and line-like artefacts injected into training data.

### Cited Findings
- Li 2020: synthetic whistles from 6.25 % of annotated contours, blended into real whistle-absent noise, gave F1 0.770 (0.792 with recall-guided resampling) vs 0.884 for training on all real annotated patches and 0.634 for the classical baseline. Natural-image edges gave 0.751 (human edges) and 0.722 (Canny, zero annotation) — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Li 2020 synthesis is additive in the normalised log-magnitude domain (X' = X + α(Y∗G)), with intensity randomised over 24 dB and real backgrounds supplying the noise statistics — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Li 2023 (GAN): augmentation helped at every data size, but gains shrank as real data grew (+1.69 F1 at 2,500 real patches, +0.46 with all data). Removing parts of the GAN pipeline (e.g., the quality filter) lowered mean F1 by 1.44 — [Li et al. 2023 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02714)
- Li 2023 (JASA): pseudo-labels from classical extractors plus L_recall reached 86.31–87.20 F1 vs 87.47 for full supervision. Naive training on the same pseudo-labels gave 74.14 — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)
- DeepLofargram: synthetic random-walk sinusoids in white Gaussian noise, with curriculum pretraining at higher SNR because "training deep network with low SNR lofargrams is difficult to converge"; no real-data test — [arXiv 1912.00605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00605)
- U-Net++ for LOFARgrams trained from a physics forward model of ship noise — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1528111/full)
- G2Net: ML solutions trained on data simulated on the fly (PyFstat) and injected line-like features; one team trained separate networks for Gaussian and real noise. The U-Net degraded negligibly on real noise, while a DP track-finder's false-positive rate rose about 10× — [arXiv 2509.06445](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06445v2)
- Dreissigacker & Prix 2020 moved CW CNNs from Gaussian to two-detector, more realistic noise — [arXiv 2005.04140](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04140)
- TFFR-Net trains on synthetic chirps (0–25 dB, ≤ 6 components) and claims generalisation to real bat, power-system and seismic signals — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149622)

### Inferences
- The strongest lever was the realism of the background (real noise, real interferers), not of the rendered signal. wavetracker's benchmark already sums real recordings electrode by electrode, which is more physical than Li's log-domain blending. This supports making unlimited train mixtures, with real field noise including mains combs, the main training source.
- The residual gap of about 0.09 F1 (Li: 0.792 vs 0.884) came from missing real shape variety. For EOD, the analogue is real rises, chirps and drift statistics, which the source recordings supply.
- G2Net suggests deliberately adding hard negatives (line-like artefacts, combs, harmonics of low-frequency fish) to training. Real-noise robustness came from that, not from architecture.
- Pseudo-label training (Li 2023 JASA) offers a route to use unlabelled field recordings, by labelling them with the current wavetracker pipeline and training with a recall- or precision-aware loss. Their key warning is that naive training on tracker labels can do worse than the tracker itself.

### Gaps
- None of the bioacoustic papers measured performance vs SNR for synthetic- vs real-trained models, so whether synthetic training helps specifically at the noise floor is untested there.
- I found no domain-adaptation (adversarial or self-training) study for spectrogram line detectors in these fields.

## 6. Exact crossings and lines running in parallel closer than the resolution

### Takeaway
Crossings are usually resolved after detection by trajectory continuity: polynomial forward/backward fits at merged graph nodes in silbido, labelled particles in SMC-PHD, and transition priors in HMMs. The learned map only marks the energy. Recent TF work resolves crossings by adding a frequency-rate dimension (TFFR-Net), by chirp-rate differences, or by explicitly modelling the interference pattern between close modes. No field reported a solved method for two lines persistently closer than the frequency resolution. Multi-line HMMs produce "mixed tracks", SOAP keeps only one track per band, and close-mode methods adapt the window or exploit interference.

### Cited Findings
- Silbido and Deep Whistle: crossing whistles merge into one graph; junction nodes with several inputs and outputs are candidate crossings, and forward/backward polynomial fits assign branches — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894); [PubMed 21973376](https://www.pubmed.gov/21973376/); [Li et al. 2023 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02714)
- Li 2020's network "learns archetypical patterns (e.g., crossings, frequency modulated sweeps)" from patches; crossings are among the primitive shapes in its implicit dictionary — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- SMC-PHD keeps identities with labelled particles but fragments more (1.80 segments per whistle vs 1.13–1.18 for CNN + graph search) — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)
- TFFR-Net: in 2-D TF, 78.6 % of simulated multicomponent cases have ridge crossings; in TF plus frequency-rate it is 0.9 %. Instance discrimination happens in the lifted space — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149622)
- Sensors 2025: crossing components are separated by their differing linear chirp rates — [MDPI Sensors 25(7):2030](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/7/2030)
- Polisano et al. 2025: an identified interference component defines where to adapt the window length, for "better ridge detection when modes are close" — [arXiv 2503.14990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14990)
- Multi-line HMM: produces a "mixed" track that may need separating — [search summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/multiple-target-tracking-and-multiple-frequency-line-tracking-using-hidden-markov-models/812459752159182849-3812)
- SOAP returns one track per sub-band; ranked alternatives are lost — [ar5iv 1903.12614](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.12614)
- AMTC peels traces off one at a time (dominant trace, compensate, next) — [arXiv 2005.06686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06686)
- Bat source separation counts sources and assigns per-source masks where calls overlap in time and frequency — [BVS mdl-32237826](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-32237826)

### Inferences
- EOD crossings are usually shallow-angle drifts, so the chirp-rate cue that resolves crossing whistles and chirps (TFFR, Sensors 2025) will often be weak. The per-electrode amplitude pattern is the stronger cue for which fish continues where, which is why per-electrode inputs and identity cues belong in the detector or linker.
- Two fish within 1–3 Hz (below or near 2–5 bins at 0.61 Hz) beat with a period of about 0.3–1 s, i.e. 2–6 frames at 0.16 s. This leaves a temporal amplitude-modulation signature in the summed spectrogram and, because the fish differ in position, different beat phase and depth across electrodes. Polisano-style interference modelling suggests training the network to read this signature and output two salience peaks, which per-frame peak picking cannot do. Training targets would need sub-bin frequency labels (e.g., Gaussian-blurred at the true frequency), as in Li's blurred masks.
- A query/instance head (TFFR-Net, transformer whistle instance segmentation, bat mask separation) is the learned alternative to merging graphs at crossings. Over 30 fish and hours of data it would still feed a tracker or linker for long-range identity.

### Gaps
- No source quantified detection or tracking when two lines persist closer than one frequency bin; the sub-resolution case is untested in the literature found.
- How GM-PHD and SMC-PHD behave with persistent close-parallel targets (track coalescence) was not verified from the full texts.

## 7. What transfers to the EOD salience detector (synthesis across fields)

### Takeaway
The best-supported recipe from these fields: a no-pooling or high-resolution fully convolutional salience network trained on synthetic mixtures over real backgrounds, its thresholded peaks fed to the existing tracker, with hard negatives (lines, harmonics) in training and recall- or precision-aware losses for incomplete labels. Add to that what no single field did together: harmonic-aligned input, per-electrode spatial cues (an analogue of the GW per-detector line veto), and an explicit treatment of crossings and sub-resolution pairs.

### Cited Findings
- Learned salience plus an unchanged classical tracker gave +0.25 F1, mostly from precision (0.634 → 0.956), on hard multi-whistle data with crossings — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- CNN context suppresses false positives from transients that per-frame peak picking accepts; residual false positives are narrowband artefact lines and gap-bridged sequences — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26799/1/Conant_2022_JSA_Silbido_profundo_CC.pdf)
- Multi-detector line classification (signal / line / noise) with per-detector inputs made a fully automated CW search competitive — [arXiv 2007.08207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08207)
- A harmonic prior doubled the track detection rate — [Lampert 2010 thesis](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/theses/2010/2010-lampert.pdf)
- Recall-aware loss on tracker-generated pseudo-labels matched full supervision — [Li et al. 2023 JASA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889)
- Real-noise robustness came from injecting line-like artefacts into training — [arXiv 2509.06445](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06445v2)
- Fragmentation (segments per truth track), coverage and deviation are standard tonal-extraction metrics alongside P/R — [Li et al. 2023 JASA PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65889/noaa_65889_DS1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Concrete design implications, all my judgement:
  1. Keep the frequency resolution in the network (no frequency pooling, or a U-Net with skip connections), because the targets are bin-accurate and fish can be 2–5 bins apart.
  2. Give a wide temporal context (seconds) for continuity, plus harmonic-stacked channels for harmonic evidence.
  3. Include per-electrode spectrograms (or a pattern-invariant aggregation over electrodes) so that common-mode lines and harmonics, which share their fundamental's pattern, can be rejected.
  4. Train on unlimited real-source mixtures with field noise, plus explicit hard negatives.
  5. Use a loss that down-weights background where truth is known to be incomplete (L_recall-style), and masks positives where the truth fish is attenuated below the noise.
  6. Report P/R vs per-bin SNR, fragmentation and coverage.
  7. Consider a DP/Viterbi or multi-target back end on the salience map before the tracker, given G2Net's evidence that track statistics add low-SNR sensitivity.
- Chirps (< 1 frame) resemble the "short whistles < 150 ms" that whistle evaluations exclude. None of these fields offers a validated solution for sub-frame excursions; they will likely need to be bridged by the tracker rather than detected.

### Gaps
- No field combined learned salience with multi-sensor spatial identity cues for tonal tracking. That combination remains untested anywhere I found.
- Compute or throughput figures exist only for whistles (3–5.5× real time on one 1080Ti or 2080 Ti at 192 kHz). How they scale to 16-electrode, hours-long EOD recordings at 0.61 Hz resolution was not reported.
