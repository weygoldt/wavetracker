# Training regime for a dense EOD-frequency salience detector: semi-synthetic mixtures, incomplete/noisy labels, lab-to-field adaptation

Scope note: these notes cover the training regime only (data generation, targets, losses, label handling, self-training, domain shift, evaluation, data quantity). Facts carry a source link. Statements under "Inferences" are my own judgement for the wavetracker setup (linear 0.61 Hz bins, 0.16 s frames, up to 30+ fish, 2-16+ electrodes, RTX 4080 16 GB, 62 GB RAM). Several numbers come from search-engine summaries of papers rather than full-text reads; those are marked "(via search summary)".

## 1. Mixing domain: on-the-fly mixture augmentation, costs, storage

### Takeaway
Speech separation consistently gains about 1-2 dB from "dynamic mixing" (remixing new mixtures every epoch), and the gain is largest when the pool of clean sources is small, which is the wavetracker situation. For this project the exact and cheap analogue of time-domain dynamic mixing is summing per-source, per-electrode complex STFTs (cached once per source and warp factor) and only then taking power. Summing power spectrograms is wrong in exactly the regime that fails most often (two fish within a few Hz).

### Cited Findings
- SepFormer (Subakan et al., ICASSP 2021; arXiv 2010.13154): WSJ0-2mix test SI-SNRi 20.4 dB without dynamic mixing vs 22.3 dB with it. The paper's validation ablation lists 20.5 dB without DM, so the gain is about 1.9-2.0 dB depending on the table (via search summary) — [Subakan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.13154)
- Wavesplit (Zeghidour & Grangier, arXiv 2002.08933, 2020; journal version not verified here) describes dynamic mixing as online augmentation: it samples random windows of training recordings and sums them after random gains, creating new examples indefinitely. On WHAM! and WHAMR!, ΔSI-SDR rises from 15.4 to 16.0 dB and from 12.0 to 13.2 dB with DM (Table IV; via search summary) — [Zeghidour & Grangier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08933)
- Ravenscroft, Goetze & Hain (arXiv 2304.07142, 2023; peer-reviewed venue not verified here): "DM often results in a 1.0 to 1.5dB SISDR performance improvement dependant upon the model and dataset." Combining DM with a training-signal-length limit of 4.42 s (random crops) matched full-length SepFormer+DM on WHAMR (ΔSI-SDR 14.0 dB in both cases) and cut epoch time from 85 to 59 min, a 44% reduction. Random start-index sampling of crops alone already gives unique examples each epoch without simulating new mixtures — [Ravenscroft et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07142)
- An APSIPA 2022 empirical study of mixture-generation strategies reports that the benefit of dynamic mixing is largest for training sets with a limited number of clean sources (via search summary) — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/63be2deb90e50fcafd0a36cd/an-empirical-study-of-training-mixture-generation-strategies-on-speech-separation-dynamic)
- A 2023 study on data augmentation for speech separation found that combining non-source-preserving strategies (CutMix, Mixup and dynamic mixing) generalised best (via search summary) — [UniGe IRIS record](https://iris.unige.it/handle/11567/1141940)
- IIANet (audio-visual separation) reports that on-the-fly DM with random gains added only about 0.1 min per epoch (via search summary) — [IIANet, arXiv 2308.08143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08143)
- Deep salience (Bittner et al., ISMIR 2017) built training mixes linearly from multitrack stems, "with weights estimated from the original mixes using a least squares fit". Mixing happened in the audio (linear) domain before the HCQT, not on magnitude representations — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- Li et al. (IJCNN 2020) synthesised whistle training samples by blending contours into spectrogram patches of whistle-absent ocean recordings with a random intensity factor α (X' built from background X plus the scaled contour). This is spectrogram-domain injection, accepted there because whistles rarely share bins with each other — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)

### Inferences
- Physics of the choice (my derivation): the STFT is linear, so STFT(Σ_s x_s) = Σ_s STFT(x_s) exactly, given the same window, hop and alignment to the hop grid. Power is not linear: |X1+X2|² = |X1|² + |X2|² + 2Re(X1·X2*). If the 0.61 Hz resolution comes from an analysis window of ≈1/0.61 ≈ 1.6 s, two fish Δf apart produce a cross term that rotates at Δf from frame to frame. Two examples: Δf = 3 Hz gives a beat period of 0.33 s, about 2 hops; Δf < 0.61 Hz (same bin) gives a slow beat with power swinging between (a−b)² and (a+b)² over more than 10 frames. Power-domain summing replaces this with a constant a²+b², so the network would never see the interference patterns of real close fish, which are exactly the measured "within 3 Hz" failure mode. Power summing is acceptable only for lines that never share leakage bins.
- Exact operations in the complex-STFT domain: per-source gain (real scale), a constant per-source phase rotation (equivalent to x·cosφ − H{x}·sinφ, a valid real signal; a cheap way to randomise beat phase between sources), time offsets by integer numbers of hops, and adding field-noise STFTs computed with the same physical-unit parameters. Not exact: time-warping (frequency scale 0.4-1.6). It changes both the frequency and the time axes, so it must be applied in the time domain before the STFT. Practical recipe: precompute each train source's per-electrode complex STFT for a discrete set of K warp factors (for example 8-16 factors spanning 0.4-1.6), cache them, and draw mixtures on the fly by choosing sources, warp indices, gains, phases and hop offsets.
- Storage scaling (my arithmetic, not sourced): the cache cost is S_sources × K_warps × N_electrodes × T × F × bytes and does not depend on how many mixtures are drawn, unlike storing mixtures. Complex fp16 (two halves) costs 4 B per bin, the same as fp32 power, but per electrode and per source. For scale: if a summed fp32 power spectrogram is ~30 MB, one source-electrode-warp complex fp16 copy over the same T-F area is also ~30 MB. Sixteen electrodes × 8 warps then costs ~3.8 GB per source. A few dozen train sources therefore need a few hundred GB on disk (memory-mapped, chunked by time) unless the frequency axis is cropped to the band the input actually uses. Per the project rule, derive that band from the config and data (fundamental range × harmonics used), never hard-code it. Cropping to the band of fundamentals plus the first few harmonics, and chunking time into crops of a few tens of seconds, as the 4.42 s crops did in Ravenscroft et al., keeps per-batch I/O small. Mixing 30 sources × N_e electrodes of complex fp16 crops on the GPU is negligible next to the network's cost. This replaces 100 s and 6.5 GB of time-domain generation per mixture.
- Compromise if disk is short: keep complex STFTs only for the electrodes and band needed, and/or store the summed-over-electrodes power together with a reduced set of per-electrode complex channels. Alternatively, keep a fixed bank of time-domain-generated mixtures for dev/test (exact) and use complex-STFT dynamic mixing only for training.
- The STFT normalisation must be sample-rate independent (for example, divide by the window sum) so that 20 kHz lab sources and 48 kHz field noise add at consistent amplitudes when both use the same physical window length.

### Gaps
- No source was found that studies dynamic mixing in the complex-STFT domain specifically, or that compares power-domain against complex-domain mixing for multi-f0 targets. The beat-interference argument above is first-principles, not empirical.
- Exact Wavesplit/SepFormer DM implementation details (gain ranges, cost) come from search summaries, not full-text reads.

## 2. Targets and losses for sparse lines: blurring, BCE vs focal, imbalance, sub-bin offsets, peak picking, thresholds

### Takeaway
The established recipe is: Gaussian-blurred per-bin targets, sigmoid outputs trained with BCE (deep salience, CREPE), peak picking along frequency, and a minimum-salience threshold tuned on validation. For extreme imbalance, CenterNet's penalty-reduced focal loss normalised by the number of positives is the standard dense-keypoint alternative. Class-balanced weights need care: a later preprint found Basic Pitch's 0.05/0.95 weighting produced many false positives. Thresholds tuned on one dataset did not transfer to another (0.3 vs 0.05 optimum), which matters for lab-to-field.

### Cited Findings
- Deep salience (ISMIR 2017): ground-truth f0s are quantised to the nearest T-F bin, set to 1, and "Gaussian blurred in frequency such that the energy surrounding a ground truth frequency decays to zero within a quarter-tone". The stated reasons are to soften the penalty for near-correct predictions and to allow for annotations not being accurate to 20 cents. Loss: cross-entropy with continuous targets y, ŷ ∈ [0,1], Adam optimiser. Architecture: 5 fully convolutional layers, no pooling ("we do not want to be invariant to small shifts in time frequency"), and a final layer covering 14 semitones. HCQT input: 60 bins/octave (20 cents), 6 octaves from 32.7 Hz, harmonics {0.5,1,2,3,4,5}; the sub-harmonic helps disambiguate the fundamental from the first harmonic — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- Deep salience decoding: thresholding at 0.5 fails because neighbours of a peak also exceed 0.5 under blurred targets. They peak-pick along frequency and choose "the threshold that maximizes the multi-f0 accuracy on the validation set". The chosen threshold was 0.3, but on Bach10 the oracle optimum was 0.05, reaching 63% accuracy vs 68% (Duan) and 76% (Benetos). The remaining gap was recall, which they attributed to the training mixes (few simultaneous harmonically related pitches) — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- CREPE (Kim et al., ICASSP 2018): 360 output bins of 20 cents; target is Gaussian-blurred with a standard deviation of 25 cents; loss is binary cross-entropy summed over the 360 bins. The pitch estimate is a weighted average of bin pitches around the argmax (sub-bin decoding) — [Kim et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06182)
- CenterNet (Zhou, Wang & Krähenbühl, arXiv 1904.07850, 2019): Gaussian heatmap targets with element-wise max where Gaussians overlap; "penalty-reduced pixelwise logistic regression with focal loss". Positives are weighted by (1−Ŷ)^α·log Ŷ; negatives by (1−Y)^β·Ŷ^α·log(1−Ŷ), so negatives near a centre are down-weighted by (1−Y)^β. α=2, β=4, and the loss is normalised by the number of keypoints N. A local offset head, shared across classes, recovers discretisation error and is trained with L1 at centre points only; plain L1 beat Smooth L1 for size regression — [Zhou et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.07850)
- Basic Pitch (Bittner et al., ICASSP 2022; arXiv 2203.09893): BCE for each of three posteriorgram outputs. For the onset output, "heavy class imbalance ... drives models to output Yo = 0 everywhere", so they use class-balanced BCE with negative weight 0.05 and positive weight 0.95 ("set empirically"). The multipitch output has 3 bins per semitone; training uses 2 s crops at 22,050 Hz, batch 16, Adam lr 1e-3, and label-preserving augmentations (noise, EQ filters, reverb). The model has 16,782 parameters, uses 7 harmonics + 1 sub-harmonic in an HCQT approximation, and a limited frequency receptive field "removes the need for pitch shifting data augmentations" — [Bittner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- A later preprint (arXiv 2509.12712, 2025) reports that Basic Pitch's class-balanced weighting caused many false positives (large positive weights). Its authors switched to focal loss with γ=1 and positive weights 0.2 (notes) and 0.06 (onsets) (via search summary; preprint) — [arXiv 2509.12712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12712)
- Li et al. (IJCNN 2020) whistle confidence maps: binary 1-pixel contour targets in 50×50 patches (100 ms × 6.25 kHz), trained with a Charbonnier loss (smooth L2), and class balance by sampling equal numbers of positive and negative patches. The confidence map is passed to an existing graph-search contour extractor at threshold 0.5 — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)

### Inferences
- Blur width must be set in Hz, not cents: on a linear axis with 0.61 Hz bins, two fish 3 Hz apart are ~5 bins apart. A Gaussian σ of ≈1 bin (≈0.6 Hz) keeps such neighbours as separate peaks; the deep-salience quarter-tone support (≈±15 Hz at 500 Hz) would merge them. Use the element-wise max (CenterNet), not the sum, where Gaussians of nearby fish overlap, so a target never exceeds 1 and two peaks stay separable.
- Imbalance estimate (my arithmetic): with σ ≈ 1 bin, each fish contributes ~2-3 bins of meaningful target mass. Thirty fish across a few thousand bins (e.g. 2000 Hz / 0.61 Hz ≈ 3300 bins, if that is the configured band) gives ~2-3% positive mass per frame. That is moderate, not COCO-extreme, so plain BCE on blurred targets (deep salience/CREPE) is a reasonable baseline. CenterNet-style focal loss (α=2, β=4, normalised by N positives) is the natural upgrade aimed at faint-fish recall, because it down-weights the many easy negatives. Avoid large fixed positive weights (Basic Pitch 0.95 onset weighting), given the false-positive evidence in arXiv 2509.12712.
- Sub-bin regression: a CenterNet-style offset head (L1, applied only at truth bins), or CREPE-style weighted-average decoding, recovers frequency below 0.61 Hz. This matters for the tracker/linker and for fish within 1-3 Hz. Supervision should use the continuous truth EODf at the frame centre.
- Chirps (~100 ms, shorter than one frame) should not be targets. Fast rises (tens of Hz per second, several Hz per 0.16 s frame) smear energy within a long analysis window; the target should follow the truth frequency at the frame centre, and evaluation should be stratified by |df/dt|.
- Peak picking plus a threshold tuned on a dev set (deep salience) is the minimum. Because the optimum threshold moved from 0.3 to 0.05 across datasets in deep salience, calibrate the threshold separately for lab-mixture dev and field dev. Also report threshold-free precision-recall curves.
- Harmonic false lines: deep salience's sub-harmonic channel exists precisely to separate the fundamental from the first harmonic. Inputs that stack harmonically related bins (HCQT-like stacking on the linear axis by integer multiples, as in Basic Pitch's shifted-copy approximation) give the network the evidence it needs to reject harmonics of low fish as fundamentals.

### Gaps
- No reported head-to-head of BCE vs focal loss for multi-f0 salience on comparable data was found; the evidence is from object detection and note-onset transcription.
- No source quantifies the optimal blur σ in Hz for densely spaced lines.

## 3. Incomplete labels / missing annotations: PU learning, ignore masks, sparse-annotation detection, noise-robust losses

### Takeaway
Two distinct truth defects need different tools. (1) Real but unlabelled lines in sources are a systematic missing-positive problem, best handled by ignore masks: zero loss on detected residual lines, carried through each mixture's warp and shift. This mirrors deep salience removing unannotated stems from its training mixes, and the ignore-region/soft-sampling approach in sparse-annotation detection. (2) Labelled frames below the noise floor are a "false positive in the label" problem, best handled by SNR-gated ignore masks. PU learning and noise-robust losses are less suitable as primary tools here, because the defects are spatially localisable.

### Cited Findings
- Deep salience avoided the unlabelled-pitch problem at the data level: to have "a mix where all pitched content is annotated", they re-created partial mixes from only the annotated stems, monophonic stems (labelled by pYIN as proxy truth) and percussive stems, "with all 'unknown' pitch content removed" — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- Soft Sampling (Wu, Bodla, Singh, Najibi, Chellappa & Davis; arXiv 1806.06986, 2018/2019): with 30% of annotations dropped and labelled as background, Faster-RCNN lost only about 5% on PASCAL VOC. Soft sampling re-weights RoI gradients by overlap with positives and beat baselines by over 3% on OpenImagesV3, which has real missing annotations (via search summary; WACV venue not confirmed) — [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06986)
- Background Recalibration Loss (Zhang, Chen, Shen et al., ICASSP 2020; arXiv 2002.05274): a focal-loss-based one-stage-detector loss that recalibrates the background loss for anchors likely to be unlabelled objects. Reported results: +9.0% mAP@[.5:.95] over baseline on COCO with 50% of annotations erased, and +6.4/+4.8 mAP50/mAP75 with a scaling factor of 0.1 in ablation (via search summary) — [Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05274)
- Co-mining (Wang, Yang, Cao & Zhang, AAAI 2021): a Siamese network whose two branches (original and augmented image) predict pseudo-label sets for each other, to mine unlabelled instances. The authors argue that reweighting hard negatives or ignoring unlabelled regions only "alleviate" the problem. Reported gain: 1.4-2.1% with RetinaNet over baselines on sparsely annotated COCO — [Wang et al. 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16385)
- nnPU (Kiryo, Niu, du Plessis & Sugiyama, NeurIPS 2017): the unbiased PU risk estimator can go negative with flexible models and overfit badly. The non-negative estimator clips the negative-class part of the risk at zero and allows deep networks to be trained from limited positive data. It requires the class prior π — [Kiryo et al. 2017](https://papers.NeurIPS.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/7cce53cf90577442771720a370c3c723-Paper.pdf). Class-prior mismatch is a known problem — [Nakajima & Sugiyama, arXiv 2107.05045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05045v2)
- Generalized Cross Entropy (Zhang & Sabuncu, NeurIPS 2018): a loss family interpolating between MAE (noise-robust but slow and poor with DNNs on large datasets) and CE. It was evaluated on CIFAR-10/100 and Fashion-MNIST with synthetic label noise — [Zhang & Sabuncu 2018](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/hash/f2925f97bc13ad2852a7a551802feea0-Abstract.html)
- In sound event detection, DCASE 2024 Task 4 systems combined mean teacher with pseudo-labelling to handle partially missing labels across heterogeneous datasets — [Huang et al. DCASE 2024 report](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2024/technical_reports/DCASE2024_Huang_77_t4.pdf); [Chen et al. DCASE 2024 report](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2024/technical_reports/DCASE2024_Chen_15_t4.pdf)

### Inferences
- Truth defect 1 (residual real lines in sources, e.g. the 1066 Hz line 28 dB below the fish): the missing labels are systematic, not random. The same faint line appears, warped, in every mixture that uses that source, so the network would learn a consistent rule that "faint stable lines are not fish". That is worse than the random-dropout setting where detectors proved tolerant (Soft Sampling's ~5% loss at 30% dropped). The deep-salience solution (remove unknown content) is not available, because the line is inside the source recording. The closest equivalent is an ignore mask. Recipe: (a) on each source alone, find residual spectral lines not explained by the truth fish (two-fish sources are easy to analyse); (b) store them as per-source masks in source T-F coordinates, so they follow the same warp, shift and crop as the source; (c) set the loss weight to 0 within a few bins of them in every mixture. Critical exception: do not mask integer harmonics of labelled fish. Those are real lines the network must learn to reject (a measured false-line failure mode), so they must stay as negatives.
- Turning masked lines into positives (Co-mining-style pseudo-labels) is optional and riskier. Only lines that look like EODs (stable, with harmonics co-modulating) could be promoted; stray instrument lines should stay ignored. The ignore mask is the safe default; the reported pseudo-mining gains over ignore in detection are small (1.4-2.1%).
- BRL-style soft down-weighting of confident background predictions is a cheap complement for residual lines the line-finder misses: a mild version reduces the penalty when the network is confident of a line in an unlabelled region.
- Truth defect 2 (labelled frames below the noise floor): as positives they teach hallucination; flipped to negatives they teach suppression of real faint fish. Ignore both ways. Recipe: compute per-frame SNR (truth.npz has snr_db), set the loss weight to 0 for that fish's target where snr_db < a threshold, and optionally use a soft weight ramp over a few dB above it. Do not delete the frames from truth; keep them for evaluation, but score them in a separate "undetectable" stratum where hits are not penalised as FPs and misses are not counted against recall.
- Truth defect 3 (ridge-completed gaps, freqs_detected=False): these are plausible but lower-confidence positives. Options are a reduced loss weight (e.g. 0.5) or a wider target σ on those frames. The existing benchmark already scores detected and completed frames apart; keep that split in training diagnostics.
- PU learning fits the field archive (positives only from lab mixtures, field all unlabelled) poorly. The positive distribution differs from the field positives (covariate shift), and π varies by recording, so nnPU's prior assumption is shaky. GCE/SCE-style robust losses are designed for random label flips in classification and offer no way to localise the known defects. Neither is a first-line tool here.

### Gaps
- No paper was found on missing labels in dense multi-f0 / pitch salience specifically; all sparse-annotation evidence is from object detection (random annotation dropout) and SED.
- No quantitative comparison of "ignore mask" vs "treat as negative" for systematic (non-random) missing labels was found.

## 4. Self-training / teacher-student on unlabelled field data

### Takeaway
Teacher-student methods (Noisy Student, Mean Teacher, Unbiased Teacher) give large gains when labelled data are scarce. In the DCASE SED setting, however, adding unlabelled data does not always help, and naive pseudo-labelling is known to lock in errors (confirmation bias). For field EOD data, self-training is promising for closing the lab-to-field gap only if pseudo-labels are filtered by track-level consistency and harmonic checks, labelled mixtures stay in every batch, and success is judged on a small hand-curated field dev set. Otherwise it will reinforce harmonics and stray lines.

### Cited Findings
- Noisy Student (Xie et al., CVPR 2020; arXiv 1911.04252): an EfficientNet teacher pseudo-labels ~300M unlabelled images; an equal-or-larger student trains on labelled plus pseudo-labelled data with noise (dropout, stochastic depth, RandAugment) and is iterated as the new teacher. Results: 88.4% ImageNet top-1 (+2.0 over the prior SOTA, which used 3.5B weakly labelled images); ImageNet-A 61.0→83.7%; ImageNet-C mCE 45.7→28.3; ImageNet-P mFR 27.8→12.2 — [Xie et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04252)
- Arazo et al. (IJCNN 2020; arXiv 1908.02983): naive pseudo-labelling "overfits to incorrect pseudo-labels due to the so-called confirmation bias". Mixup mitigates it, but "mixup alone does not guarantee robustness" with few labels; adding a minimum number of labelled samples per mini-batch fixes this — [Arazo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983v5)
- Unbiased Teacher (Liu et al., ICLR 2021): class imbalance in detection biases pseudo-labels. A teacher (EMA of the student) generates pseudo-labels for differently augmented inputs, and focal loss counters the imbalance-induced bias. Reported: +6.8 mAP over the prior SOTA at 1% labelled COCO, and ~10 mAP over supervised at 0.5-2% labels (via search summary) — [Liu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09480v1)
- Couple Learning (Tao et al., ICASSP 2022; arXiv 2110.05809): mean teacher plus a pseudo-label generation model on DCASE 2020 Task 4, reaching ~44.25% event-F1 on the public eval set vs a 32.39% baseline. The mean-teacher consistency term offsets pseudo-label noise — [Tao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05809v2)
- DCASE 2022 Task 4 report (Li et al.): two-stage CRNN with mean teacher and teacher pseudo-labels. The best mean-teacher configuration used weak + synthetic + strong real data, and adding unlabelled data to the synthetic-plus-real mix performed worse than leaving it out — [Li et al. DCASE 2022](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2022/technical_reports/DCASE2022_Li_56_t4.pdf)
- DCASE 2023 Task 4 (arXiv 2306.06461): two-stage mean-teacher training; ensembling student and teacher predictions improved pseudo-label quality — [arXiv 2306.06461](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06461)
- Lauha et al. (Methods in Ecology and Evolution 2022, 13(12):2799-2810): fine-tuning a bird-sound CNN on a small amount of local data improved accuracy on test recordings from the same area but not from a different region (German Alps) (via search summary) — [Lauha et al. 2022](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/83704)

### Inferences
- The specific confirmation-bias risk is the field false-line classes: harmonics of low-EODf fish, mains-hum combs, and stray electronic lines. A teacher trained on lab mixtures may mark these as fish; pseudo-labelling then teaches the student to keep doing so. Countermeasures (my design; each item is a standard SSL ingredient adapted to this domain):
  1. Positive pseudo-labels only on confident, long tracks: run the teacher, then the existing tracker and linker, and keep only frames on tracks that persist for many seconds with high salience. This is a temporal-consistency filter that frame-level FixMatch thresholds lack.
  2. Harmonic veto: drop pseudo-positives at integer multiples of a stronger concurrent line that co-modulate with it (during a rise the k-th harmonic moves k times as far). Also drop lines at hum frequencies that do not drift.
  3. Negatives only where the teacher is confidently empty; ignore everything in between (an ignore band, as in Section 3).
  4. Arazo's rule: a fixed share of every batch is labelled lab mixtures.
  5. EMA teacher (Mean Teacher / Unbiased Teacher) with focal loss on the pseudo-labelled part, plus strong student-side noise (Noisy Student): gain and SNR jitter, added field noise, time and frequency masking, and electrode dropout.
- DCASE evidence that unlabelled data can hurt (Li et al. 2022), together with Lauha's finding that local adaptation does not transfer across regions, means self-training gains must be measured on held-out field sites/days that were not pseudo-labelled. A gain on the pseudo-labelled recordings themselves is not evidence.
- Ordering judgement: build the supervised detector with field-noise mixing first (Section 5) and measure the field gap on a small curated field set. Add self-training only if a gap remains; it is the most expensive and most failure-prone step.

### Gaps
- No published self-training study on electric-fish recordings or on dense multi-f0 salience with unlabelled field audio was found.
- No FixMatch-style dense-prediction study for spectrogram line detection was located; the dense SSL evidence is from object detection and SED.

## 5. Lab-to-field domain shift: noise libraries, SNR/gain, frequency scale, harmonic profiles, hum combs

### Takeaway
The best-documented sim-to-real levers for spectrogram detectors are: (1) mixing targets into many real background recordings at randomised, including very low, SNRs; (2) broad pitch/time resampling; (3) a large volume of synthetic data. Restricting SNR or event-rate variety reduces real-data F1. A measurable synthetic-to-real gap typically remains (DESED), and small amounts of target-domain data help mainly in-domain.

### Cited Findings
- DRASDIC (Hoffman et al., arXiv 2503.00296, revised Sept 2025; preprint) applies domain randomisation for few-shot bioacoustic SED. Recipe: target SNR from a two-component Gaussian mixture with means ~U(−12, 7) dB and SDs ~U(0, 5) dB, with a curriculum lowering the minimum SNR from 0 to −20 dB; two background tracks per scene drawn from several large unlabelled/background corpora (SilentCities, DeepShip, SanctSound and others); resampling rates {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1, 1, 1.5, 2}; time reversal p=0.2; recorded room impulse responses; randomised event rates. Scale: >8,800 h of strongly labelled synthetic audio, from ~5,540 h of backgrounds and 577 h of "pseudovox". Real test (FASD13) F1: 0.445 full. Reducing synthetic data to 220 h gave 0.354, and to 22 h gave 0.130; 22 h of non-synthetic data alone gave 0.135. Only-high-SNR events gave 0.402 and only-low-SNR 0.438; restricted event rates gave 0.381/0.370. Removing pitch/time shift gave 0.457 on test but 0.613 (vs 0.704) on validation. The design was chosen by validation-based model selection — [Hoffman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00296v2)
- Soltero, Siqueira & Gutschmidt (arXiv 2507.16235, July 2025; preprint): synthetic 10 s soundscapes of little-owl calls mixed into clean background segments plus 58 contaminant sounds (wind, rain, vehicles, non-target animals), with labels generated automatically. From 30 unique calls, 1,000 soundscapes were best (mean AUC 0.920 ± 0.014, F1@0.5 0.776 ± 0.034, on 74 labelled real segments); 8,000 dropped AUC to 0.887. The authors suggest overfitting to augmentation artefacts but did not test it. Training on −10 to 0 dB SNR produced the model that detected low-SNR calls. There was no comparison with training on real annotations — [Soltero et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16235v1)
- Li et al. (IJCNN 2020): whistle contours injected into real whistle-absent ocean backgrounds. Contour-extraction F1 on 911 test whistles: graph search 0.634; fully supervised on 148,224 real patches (WGT) 0.884; synthesis from 6.25% of annotations 0.770 (0.792 with recall-guided synthesis); from image-edge contours with no whistle labels 0.722-0.751. Synthesis from real whistle shapes beat synthesis from image edges — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- Silbido profundo (Conant et al., JASA 2022, 152:3800-3808): the packaged Li et al. network reached precision >96% and recall ~80% on a two-species benchmark subset, and agreed strongly with NOAA annotations on unseen Pacific towed-array data, where precision/recall could not be measured (via search summary) — [Conant et al. 2022](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/26799)
- DESED / DCASE Task 4 (Turpault, Serizel et al., 2019-2021): strongly labelled synthetic soundscapes (Scaper) plus weak/unlabelled real clips. The authors flag the synthetic-vs-recorded mismatch, find that defaults in soundscape generation are sub-optimal, and find that real soundscapes contain many non-target events. Adding non-target events to synthetic scenes was studied as a fix — [Turpault et al. 2020 (arXiv 2007.03931)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03931.pdf); [Turpault & Serizel, DCASE 2020](https://dcase.community/documents/workshop2020/proceedings/DCASE2020Workshop_Turpault_85.pdf). Optimal-transport feature alignment reduced the DESED synthetic-real mismatch, but a gap remained — [Olvera et al., DCASE 2021](https://dcase.community/documents/workshop2021/posters/DCASE2021Workshop_Olvera_72-poster.pdf)
- CREPE evaluated noise robustness by degrading test audio with the Audio Degradation Toolbox — [Kim et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06182)
- Basic Pitch trained with label-preserving noise, EQ and reverb augmentation, and avoided pitch-shift augmentation by limiting the frequency receptive field — [Bittner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)

### Inferences
- The closest analogue to DRASDIC's evidence for this project: real field-noise STFTs (from the unlabelled archive, segments with no or few fish, or with fish lines masked out as "ignore") added in the complex domain at randomised gain. Use a wide SNR distribution reaching well below 0 dB per fish (DRASDIC's curriculum down to −20 dB; Soltero's −10 to 0 dB for faint calls). Most misses are below 6 dB SNR, so SNR breadth is the single most relevant randomisation. Evidence that restricting SNR to high values hurts: 0.402 vs 0.445 in DRASDIC.
- Field noise is not fish-free: field "noise" segments can contain faint fish, which become unlabelled real lines (defect 1 again). Either pick segments verified as empty, or run the line-finder on the noise segment and ignore-mask its lines too.
- The frequency-scale augmentation (0.4-1.6) already resembles DRASDIC's resampling range (0.3-2). Note: with a limited frequency receptive field, Basic Pitch reported pitch-shift augmentation as unnecessary. The point of warping here is to diversify EODf spacing and density, not translation invariance.
- Harmonic-profile augmentation (randomly re-weighting harmonic amplitudes per source) is physically motivated: field fish differ in orientation and distance, so their harmonic ratios differ across electrodes. It is cheap in the complex domain only if harmonics can be isolated per source, for example via the source's own truth. No source tests it; it is unproven.
- Mains-hum combs (50/60 Hz and harmonics, with small frequency jitter and per-electrode amplitude) should be added as negatives so the network learns non-fish lines that do not drift. Hum frequency should be randomised, not hard-coded. No source tests this for fish; it is unproven.
- Volume matters (DRASDIC: 220 h → 22 h of synthetic data cut F1 from 0.354 to 0.130). Dynamic complex-STFT mixing gives effectively unlimited mixtures, but diversity is bounded by the number of distinct train sources and field-noise segments. Expanding the noise library is cheap; expanding labelled sources is not.
- Electrode count and geometry differ between lab and field. An architecture that pools per-electrode features over a variable electrode set, trained with random electrode dropout and subsetting, is part of the domain-shift answer; this is an inference only.

### Gaps
- No sim-to-real study for weakly electric fish recordings was found.
- No published evidence was found on randomised mains-hum or harmonic-profile augmentation for spectrogram detectors.
- DRASDIC and Soltero are 2025 preprints, not peer reviewed.

## 6. Evaluation protocol: frame-level multi-f0 metrics, identity metrics, clean dev/test

### Takeaway
mir_eval.multipitch provides per-frame precision, recall, accuracy and error decompositions, but its default tolerance is ±0.5 semitone. That equals ±14.6 Hz at 500 Hz and ±29 Hz at 1000 Hz, far too loose for fish a few Hz apart, so the tolerance must be set in Hz. HOTA complements IDF1 by balancing detection and association. Splits must be made at the recording/session level (as deep salience and CREPE did with artist-conditional splits), and any data-driven synthesis or hard-example mining must not see dev data.

### Cited Findings
- mir_eval.multipitch: "By default, a frequency is 'correct' if it is within 0.5 semitones of a reference frequency" (`window=0.5`), computed in log-2 semitone space with per-frame true-positive counts summed over time. Returned metrics: Precision = ΣTP/Σn_est, Recall = ΣTP/Σn_ref, Accuracy = ΣTP/Σ(n_est + n_ref − TP), plus substitution/miss/false-alarm/total errors and chroma variants — [mir_eval multipitch docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/multipitch.html)
- mir_eval's note transcription uses bipartite matching (vs MIREX's greedy matching), and scores can differ by ~1-2% between the two — [mir_eval transcription docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html)
- HOTA (Luiten et al., IJCV 2021; arXiv 2009.07736): a single tracking metric combining detection, association and localisation. It decomposes into sub-metrics for the basic error types and aligns better with human judgement. Earlier metrics "overemphasize the importance of either detection or association" — [Luiten et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07736)
- Deep salience used an artist-conditional randomised split (all tracks of an artist in one group), a test set restricted to MedleyDB (28 tracks), and threshold selection on validation only — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf). CREPE used artist-conditional folds for MDB-stem-synth "to avoid training and testing on the same" artists — [Kim et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06182)
- Li et al. (2020) used validation data (the 6.25% annotated subset) inside training, to drive recall-guided synthesis. They evaluated contour extraction at the single operating point used in DCLDE 2011, counting a match when the mean frequency deviation was < 350 Hz and the overlap was sufficient — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- DCASE 2023 systems report threshold-independent PSDS1/PSDS2 for SED — [arXiv 2306.06461](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06461)

### Inferences
- Tolerance conversion (my arithmetic): ±0.5 semitone = ±f·(2^(1/24)−1) ≈ ±0.0293·f, i.e. ±1.8 Hz at 60 Hz, ±14.6 Hz at 500 Hz, ±29 Hz at 1000 Hz, and ±59 Hz at 2000 Hz. With lines 1-5 Hz apart, the default would credit a detection to the wrong fish and inflate both precision and recall. Use a fixed Hz tolerance tied to the resolution (e.g. ≤1-2 bins, about 0.6-1.2 Hz) with one-to-one (Hungarian) matching per frame. Report sensitivity at two or three tolerances, as CREPE did with 10/25/50-cent thresholds.
- Report recall stratified by per-frame truth SNR (the snr_db field), by distance to the nearest other line (<3 Hz vs more), by |df/dt| (rises), and by detected vs ridge-completed truth. Report FPs stratified by type: harmonic of a truth fish, hum frequency, other. These strata map onto the measured failure modes and onto the loss masks in Section 3.
- Identity: run the unchanged tracker and linker on detections, then report IDF1 and HOTA (DetA/AssA). HOTA shows whether better frame recall translates into fewer fragmentations.
- Clean protocol when train mixtures share recording conditions with dev: (a) split sources by recording session/day/tank and individual fish, not by mixture. Deep salience's and CREPE's artist-conditional splits are the precedent; the existing dev/test/train source split should be checked for this. (b) Generate dev mixtures once, frozen, from dev sources only, with seeds and parameters recorded. (c) Restrict all data-driven choices to train sources: residual-line masks, SNR curricula, and hard-example mining like Li et al.'s recall-guided synthesis (which used validation data). (d) Tune thresholds and hyperparameters on dev; keep the already-scored test split untouched until a final report. (e) For field: hold out whole field sites/days as a field dev set, never pseudo-labelled and never noise-sampled. Noise segments for augmentation come only from other recordings.
- Because the dev and train sources share the lab, dev scores will overstate field performance (the DESED-type gap). A small, separately curated field evaluation set is the only real lab-to-field measure; mixtures with added field noise are an intermediate proxy, not a substitute.

### Gaps
- No standard metric was found that handles "ignore" regions (e.g. undetectable truth frames) in multi-f0 evaluation; mir_eval has no ignore mask, so this must be custom.
- The exact matching rule inside mir_eval.multipitch's per-frame counting (one-to-one or not) was not confirmed from the docs page.

## 7. Data quantity in comparable detectors

### Takeaway
Comparable dense pitch/contour detectors were trained on roughly 6-16 h of (often resynthesised or remixed) audio, or about 10^5 labelled spectrogram patches. Synthetic-scene SED detectors improved strongly with synthetic volume up to thousands of hours. Performance kept rising with data well past the point where human annotation stops being affordable, which supports unlimited mixture generation. The binding constraint for wavetracker is the number of distinct train sources, not the number of mixtures.

### Cited Findings
- Deep salience: 240 multitracks in total (108 MedleyDB + 132 pop); train/val/test 184/28/28 full tracks, "about 10 hours of training data and 2 hours of validation data". Truth was partly human f0 annotations and partly pYIN on monophonic stems — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- CREPE: trained and evaluated on resynthesised datasets with exact f0: RWC-synth (6.16 h) and MDB-stem-synth (230 tracks, 25 instruments, 15.56 h) — [Kim et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06182)
- Li et al. (2020) whistles: 148,224 training patches (74,112 positive + an equal number of negative), 50×50 bins each (100 ms × 6.25 kHz). Performance increased with training fraction (6.25-100%), with the largest increase up to ~25%; 600k Adam iterations, ~8 h on a GTX 1080 Ti — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)
- DRASDIC: >8,800 h synthetic; F1 0.445 (full) vs 0.354 (220 h) vs 0.130 (22 h) — [Hoffman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00296v2)
- Soltero et al.: 30 calls × 1,000 ten-second soundscapes (~2.8 h) was best; more (8,000) slightly hurt AUC — [Soltero et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16235v1)
- Basic Pitch trained on a union of datasets (Molina, GuitarSet, MAESTRO, Slakh, Phenicx, iKala, MedleyDB) — [Bittner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Dynamic mixing helps most when clean sources are few (APSIPA 2022, via search summary) — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/63be2deb90e50fcafd0a36cd/an-empirical-study-of-training-mixture-generation-strategies-on-speech-separation-dynamic)

### Inferences
- Scale comparison (my arithmetic): one 10-minute 30-fish mixture at 0.16 s frames holds ~3,750 frames × 30 ≈ 1.1×10^5 labelled fish-frames, the same order as Li et al.'s whole positive patch set. Label count is not the bottleneck. Diversity is: the number of distinct fish, sessions and electrode configurations in the train sources, and the number of distinct field-noise segments.
- On one RTX 4080, complex-STFT dynamic mixing plus a deep-salience-sized fully convolutional network can plausibly stream hundreds of synthetic hours per day. Training until dev saturation (not a fixed epoch count) is feasible; disk I/O for the per-source cache is the likely limiter (Section 1). This is an inference, not measured.
- The Soltero result (more synthetic data slightly hurt) and the DRASDIC result (more helped) are reconciled by diversity. Repeating 30 calls 8,000 times adds artefacts, not information, so returns diminish once source diversity is exhausted. A learning curve over the number of train sources (not mixtures) on dev would tell whether recording more single-pair sources is worth it.

### Gaps
- No data-quantity study for electric-fish detectors exists to my knowledge; none was found.
- Basic Pitch's total training hours were not extracted.

## 8. Overall judgement: which techniques matter most for this setup

### Takeaway
In order of expected impact (my judgement, based on the evidence above):
1. Correct truth handling via ignore masks: residual source lines (but not harmonics of labelled fish), sub-noise-floor frames, and down-weighted ridge-completed frames.
2. Complex-STFT dynamic mixing with wide SNR/gain randomisation and real field noise.
3. Hz-scale blurred targets with max-merging, plus a focal-style loss and sub-bin offsets.
4. A Hz-tolerance, SNR-stratified, source-split evaluation with field hold-outs.
5. Teacher-student self-training on the field archive, only after 1-4, gated by tracker-consistency and harmonic vetoes.

### Cited Findings
- Dynamic mixing gives a consistent +1-2 dB in separation, largest with few sources — [Ravenscroft et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07142); [Subakan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.13154); [AMiner APSIPA 2022 record](https://aminer.org/pub/63be2deb90e50fcafd0a36cd/an-empirical-study-of-training-mixture-generation-strategies-on-speech-separation-dynamic)
- The deep-salience precedent removed unlabelled pitched content from training mixes rather than leaving it as implicit negatives — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)
- SNR and volume randomisation measurably change real-data F1 in bioacoustic synthetic training — [Hoffman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00296v2)
- Confirmation bias, and cases where unlabelled data hurt, are documented — [Arazo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983v5); [Li et al. DCASE 2022](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2022/technical_reports/DCASE2022_Li_56_t4.pdf)
- Thresholds do not transfer across datasets (0.3 vs 0.05) — [Bittner et al. 2017](https://brianmcfee.net/papers/ismir2017_salience.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rationale for the ranking: the measured failure modes are faint-fish misses, close-fish confusion, false lines and fragmentation. Faint-fish misses are driven mostly by how faint fish are labelled (masks) and by the SNR distribution seen in training. Close-fish confusion needs complex-domain mixing (beats) and narrow Hz targets. False lines need harmonic-aware inputs and harmonics kept as negatives. Fragmentation is evaluated via HOTA/IDF1 after the unchanged tracker. Self-training addresses only the residual field gap and carries the largest risk.
- Cheapest high-value experiment: two otherwise identical training runs, one with ignore masks and one without, scored by SNR-stratified recall and false-line rate on dev mixtures. This directly tests whether truth defects 1-2 are the bottleneck.
- Second experiment: power-domain vs complex-domain mixing, scored on the <3 Hz-separation stratum.

### Gaps
- The ranking is a judgement; no source compares these interventions jointly on a comparable task.
